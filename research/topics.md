# Topics

The one list of topics a paper note can carry in its `topics:` field. Every topic has a `Topic - <name>.md` note in the notes folder, regenerated by `research/_tools/build_topics.py`. The names below are used exactly as written.

## How a paper gets its topics

- A paper belongs to **every** topic whose definition it satisfies. Two or three is normal. One is rare. Five or more means the definitions were read too loosely.
- The definition decides, not the folder the PDF sits in and not the paper's own vocabulary.
- A topic is **proposed** only when no existing topic fits the paper at all. The proposal names the topic, gives a one-sentence definition in the style below, and says why each nearby topic does not fit. The main session reads this file, and either adds the topic here or names the existing topic to use. Readers never add rows to this file.

## Judging and evaluation

| Topic | Definition |
|---|---|
| LLM and agent judges | Using an LLM or an agent to evaluate agents or model outputs. Judge designs and prompting, agentic judges that inspect trajectories or run code, reference-based and reference-free judging, methods that make a judge more accurate or less biased. The paper's subject is the judge or the act of judging. |
| judge and decision-model audits | Empirical studies of how judges or decision models behave: biases, adversarial cues, calibration measurement, probabilistic coherence, robustness, cost, failures inside agent loops, meta-evaluation benchmarks of judges. Measurement is the contribution. |
| trained judges and reward models | Fine-tuned judges, reward models, process reward models, critics, reasoning judges, small-encoder judges, and how they are trained. |
| agent evaluation benchmarks | Benchmarks and harnesses for agents: task suites, environments, trajectory and step-level scoring, verifier failures, deployment-aware evaluation. |
| user simulation | Simulated users for multi-turn agent evaluation: building and steering them, measuring their fidelity to real users, benchmarks for simulators. |
| alternative evaluation paradigms | Debate, arenas and pairwise tournaments, consistency checks, execution-based verification, cross-examination, judge-the-judge. Ways to evaluate that are not a single judge scoring an output. |
| self-evolving tests | Generated or adversarial test sets, self-improving judges, evaluator loops that refine their own tests from where they missed. |
| judges as reward signals | Using a judge, verifier, rubric or other automatic check as the reward that trains a policy (RLVR, RLAIF, judge-in-the-loop), and what that reward does to the policy: what it learns, reward hacking, spurious or noisy rewards, whether the reward is what drives the gain. The subject is the training signal and its effect, not the judge's own accuracy. |
| stopping rules | When to stop sampling, testing or evaluating: sequential tests (SPRT, group sequential), adaptive testing, Bayesian optimal stopping, sample-size reduction with valid error control. A confidence gate that routes cases to a bigger model is not a stopping rule unless framed as sequential testing. |

## Decision models

| Topic | Definition |
|---|---|
| decision models | Typed, System One, single-pass decision models (Jev and its kin: Laya, Kev, Clef, Decider, Bongard): architecture, theory, interface, properties, and their use inside agents or pipelines. Any paper whose subject is such a model. |
| open decision models | Building, training, distilling, adapting, reproducing or releasing an open-weight decision model, or a recipe for getting one from an existing LLM or encoder. The contribution includes a model or a recipe, not only measurements of a hosted one. |

## Mechanisms

Cross-cutting techniques and failure modes. A paper carries one of these when the mechanism is a main subject, not a passing mention.

| Topic | Definition |
|---|---|
| confidence-gated escalation | A cheap model or judge answers first and its low-confidence cases go to a stronger model, a judge or a human. Cascades, deferral, how the threshold is chosen, whether it transfers to new data, whether the stages make the same errors. |
| calibration | Making reported probabilities mean what they say: proper-scoring-rule training (Brier, log score), post-hoc recalibration (temperature, Platt, isotonic), probabilistic coherence, and when a fix transfers. |
| presentation sensitivity | Decisions that change with option order, option names or labels, the set of listed options, question form or request serialisation, though the content is logically the same; and methods that remove the sensitivity. |
| abstention and fallback | None-of-the-above, defer, other, ask a clarifying question, null gates: whether a model can decline to pick from the menu, and how reliably. |
| adaptive reasoning depth | Spending extra compute (looping, partial chain of thought, handing off to a slow reasoner) only on inputs where the fast decision is unsure, and how the switch is decided. |

## Game theory

The lab's game-theory areas, from `game theory/`. A paper carries one when it uses or studies that area's objects.

| Topic | Definition |
|---|---|
| contract theory | Hidden action and hidden type: principal-agent models, moral hazard, adverse selection, multi-agent contracting, relational and incomplete contracts, algorithmic contract design, principal-agent with a learning agent. |
| cooperative game theory | Coalitions and credit assignment: core, Shapley value, nucleolus, coalition formation, hedonic games, cost sharing. |
| information design | Controlling what agents know: Bayesian persuasion, information disclosure, Bayes correlated equilibrium, multi-receiver design, algorithmic persuasion. |
| inverse reinforcement learning | Recovering a reward function from observed behaviour or corrections: maximum-entropy IRL, cooperative IRL, assistance games. |
| mechanism design | Controlling what agents are paid: revelation principle, incentive compatibility, VCG, auctions, implementation theory, mechanism design without money, dynamic and robust mechanism design. |
| preference learning | Learning what a person wants from binary or pairwise feedback: Bradley-Terry models, RLHF-style reward models, active preference elicitation. |
| social choice | Aggregating outputs, votes, rankings and judge scores from several agents: Arrow, Gibbard-Satterthwaite, voting rules, judgment aggregation, fair division, computational social choice. |
| stackelberg games | Leader commits, follower best-responds: the orchestrator-worker relationship, Stackelberg equilibrium, security games, commitment computation. |
| team theory and distributed decision making | Agents that share an objective but not information: common-payoff games, Dec-POMDPs, information structures, consensus and coordination, decentralised control, credit assignment. |
