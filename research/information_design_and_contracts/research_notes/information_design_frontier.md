# The research frontier of information design / Bayesian persuasion, 2022 – October 2026

Scope note. Foundations (Kamenica–Gentzkow 2011, Bergemann–Morris 2016/2019, Dughmi–Xu 2016) are cited only as anchors. LLM-agent persuasion is covered by another researcher; it is pointed to once in Q2. "Published" means a journal or refereed-proceedings record was found; "preprint" means arXiv only. Dates are arXiv submission dates unless stated.

## Q1. Algorithmic Bayesian persuasion: what is tractable and what is hard?

### Takeaway
The single-receiver problem is an LP and the anchors (Dughmi–Xu 2016; Dughmi's 2017 survey) settled that hardness comes from many receivers, public signals and limited message spaces; the 2022–2026 work extends the hardness-and-approximation program to relaxed receiver rationality, multi-sender equilibria, auction-plus-disclosure, and a new "price of explainability" line (EC 2026) that prices the loss from restricting to simple, auditable schemes.

### Cited Findings
- Anchor: Dughmi and Xu, "Algorithmic Bayesian Persuasion" (STOC 2016; arXiv 1503.05988), first computational treatment of the single-receiver problem — [arXiv 1503.05988](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.05988)
- Anchor survey: Dughmi, "Algorithmic Information Structure Design: A Survey," ACM SIGecom Exchanges 15(2), January 2017, pp. 2–24; organised as single-agent vs multi-agent, and multi-agent as public-channel vs private-channel revelation — [SIGecom Exchanges PDF](https://sigecom.hosting.acm.org/exchanges/volume_15/2/DUGHMI.pdf); [issue TOC](https://sigecom.org/exchanges/volume_15/2/)
- Anchor: Dughmi and Xu, "Algorithmic Persuasion with No Externalities" (EC 2017, pp. 351–368; arXiv 1609.06825): many receivers, binary actions, no externalities; public and private signalling "differ sharply in both efficacy and computational complexity" — [arXiv 1609.06825](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.06825)
- Published: Castiglioni, Celli and Gatti, "Public Bayesian Persuasion: Being Almost Optimal and Almost Persuasive," Algorithmica (2023), DOI 10.1007/s00453-023-01123-1 (arXiv 2002.05156). Computing a sender-optimal public persuasive scheme "is not approximable even in simple settings," so positive results are bi-criteria (approximately optimal and approximately persuasive); the paper "fully characterize[s] the computational complexity of computing a bi-criteria approximation of an optimal public signaling scheme" with arbitrary states, actions and utilities — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00453-023-01123-1); [arXiv 2002.05156](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2002.05156)
- Published: Babichenko, Talgam-Cohen, Xu and Zabarnyi, "Multi-channel Bayesian Persuasion" (ITCS 2022): each receiver observes a subset of the sender's channels, interpolating between public and private persuasion; for utility classes where both public and private are tractable, general multi-channel persuasion is hard, which the authors call evidence of "both the conceptual and the computational hardness" of the model — [LIPIcs ITCS 2022](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITCS.2022.11)
- Private persuasion with binary actions and no externalities is the tractable island: Arieli–Babichenko (2019) characterise optimal private schemes for supermodular, anonymous submodular and supermajority sender utilities; Babichenko–Barman give a tight (1−1/e)-approximation for monotone submodular sender utility and an efficient exact algorithm for anonymous utilities (as summarised in the Algorithmica 2023 paper) — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00453-023-01123-1)
- Limited communication (succinct schemes): Gradwohl, Hahn, Hoefer and Smorodinsky, "Algorithms for Persuasion with Limited Communication," SODA 2021 and Mathematics of Operations Research (2022; arXiv 2007.12489): with a constrained message space, approximating optimal sender utility within any constant factor is NP-hard; tractable under symmetry (exact polynomial-time characterisation) or independence (constant-factor approximation) of action utilities — [INFORMS MOR](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/moor.2021.1218); [arXiv 2007.12489](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.12489)
- Anchor for the succinctness thread: Dughmi, Kempe and Qiang, "Persuasion with Limited Communication" (arXiv 1606.07843): in bilateral trade about log(n)·log(1/ε) signals give a 1−ε approximation of optimal welfare (tight), but "the surprising logarithmic bound on the number of signals does not carry over" to revenue, and the general communication-constrained problem is hard to approximate — [arXiv 1606.07843](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.07843)
- Preprint: Yang and Zhang, "Computational Aspects of Bayesian Persuasion under Approximate Best Response" (arXiv 2402.07426; v1 12 Feb 2024, v2 13 Feb 2024). Receiver may take any action not too far from optimal; the revelation principle fails; polynomial-time exact algorithms when either the state space or the action space is small; a quasi-polynomial-time approximation scheme (QPTAS) in general; no polynomial-time exact algorithm unless P = NP — [arXiv 2402.07426](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07426)
- Published: Hossain, Wang, Lin, Chen, Parkes and Xu, "Multi-Sender Persuasion: A Computational Perspective," ICML 2024, PMLR 235:18944–18971 (arXiv 2402.04971). Solution concept is Nash equilibrium over senders' signalling policies; computing an equilibrium is PPAD-hard in general and even one sender's best response is NP-hard; the paper therefore seeks local Nash equilibria using a differentiable neural network to approximate the game's discontinuous utilities plus the extra-gradient method, reporting local equilibria that beat full-revelation equilibria — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/hossain24c.html); [arXiv 2402.04971](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04971); [ICML page](https://icml.cc/virtual/2024/poster/34852)
- Preprint: Cai, Li and Wu, "Algorithmic Information Disclosure in Optimal Auctions" (arXiv 2403.08145; 13 Mar 2024, single version). Seller jointly chooses buyers' signal structures and the allocation/payment rule; with signal design the problem "becomes NP-hard" (contrast Myerson 1981 with exogenous signals); a PTAS computes the optimal joint design within (1−ε) in expected revenue; partial disclosure yields revenue at least 1−1/e of optimal welfare for all valuation distributions — [arXiv 2403.08145](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08145)
- Preprint with EC 2026 abstract: Chen, Lin, Tang and Tucker-Foltz, "Explainable Information Design" (arXiv 2508.14196; v1 19 Aug 2025, v4 20 Jun 2026); a one-page abstract appears at EC 2026 as "The Price and Complexity of Explainable Information Design." Restricting to explainable (interval / convex-partitional / axis-aligned) schemes and measuring the "price of explainability" (PoE): worst-case PoE exactly 1/2 for interval partitions in one dimension (2/3 under a uniform prior), 1/(m+1) for convex-partitional policies in m dimensions, exponential-in-m for axis-aligned rectangles; computing the optimal explainable policy is NP-hard, but a 1/2-approximation is polynomial-time for piecewise Lipschitz utilities — [arXiv 2508.14196](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.14196)
- Preprint (2026): "No Extra Signals Needed: The Uniform Price of Explainable Information Design" (arXiv 2607.20364), a follow-up in the explainability thread; only the title was retrieved — [arXiv 2607.20364](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.20364)
- Preprint: Miyashita, "On the Sparsity of Optimal Information Structures" (arXiv 2608.00729; v1 1 Aug 2026, v2 8 Aug 2026; econ.TH). Treats information design as an LP over the Bayes-correlated-equilibrium polytope; extreme-point optima are sparse (many zero entries in the joint action-state distribution), bounded by the number of binding obedience constraints without assumptions on priors or payoffs; recommendations are often deterministic given the state or correlated so that agents infer others' recommendations; illustrated by an adoption game with network effects where the optimum recommends full adoption deterministically in high states and randomises over nested action profiles in low states — [arXiv 2608.00729](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00729)
- Preprint: Gan, Ghosh and Teh, "Persuading a Credible Agent" (arXiv 2410.23989; 31 Oct 2024). An agent who claims a type acts optimally for that claimed type; "elicit-then-persuade" incentive-compatible mechanisms can be suboptimal; optimal mechanisms need pre-signalling and non-binding elicitation (multi-stage), may require an unbounded number of rounds of exchange, and are computable in polynomial time — [arXiv 2410.23989](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.23989)
- Preprint: Harris, Immorlica, Lucier and Slivkins, "Algorithmic Persuasion Through Simulation" (arXiv 2311.18138; v1 29 Nov 2023, v5 12 Feb 2025). Sender knows a real-valued state but little about the receiver, and can query an oracle that simulates the receiver a fixed number of times before committing; characterises the optimal messaging policy for any distribution over receiver types and gives a polynomial-time querying algorithm; extensions to approximate oracles and costly queries — [arXiv 2311.18138](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.18138)
- Multi-player games (anchor 2021, arXiv 2109.12445): optimal private signalling in multi-player games contains optimal correlated equilibrium, which is NP-hard in succinct games such as congestion and scheduling games; private schemes depend exponentially on the number of agents — [arXiv 2109.12445](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.12445)
- Preprint: "LQG Information Design" (arXiv 2312.09479; Dec 2023) works the Bergemann–Morris second step (choosing the best BCE) for quadratic payoffs with Gaussian states — [arXiv 2312.09479](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.09479)

### Inferences
- The tractability map in 2026: single receiver = LP; private persuasion with binary actions and no externalities = tractable or constant-factor approximable by utility class; public persuasion = inapproximable, bi-criteria only; multi-channel = hard; limited messages = NP-hard to any constant unless symmetric/independent; approximate-best-response receivers = QPTAS, exact NP-hard; multi-sender equilibria = PPAD-hard; auction + disclosure = NP-hard but PTAS; explainable schemes = NP-hard exact, 1/2-approximation.
- "Succinct" schemes are studied under two names: limited communication (number of signals, 2016–2022) and explainability (simple partition structure, 2025–2026). Miyashita's sparsity result is the economics-side counterpart: optima are sparse because they sit at vertices of the BCE polytope.
- The CS side has shifted from "optimal scheme for a known receiver" to "optimal scheme under a relaxed or uncertain receiver model" (approximate best response, credible agents, simulated receivers), which is where it meets the robust-design literature of Q3.

### Gaps
- Hoefer, Manurangsi and Psomas, "Algorithmic Persuasion with Evidence," was reported in a reference list as ACM TEAC 2024, but no primary page was retrieved; venue and year unverified.
- No single post-2017 survey of algorithmic persuasion was found; the EC 2023 tutorial (Q6) is the closest substitute.
- The contents of arXiv 2607.20364 ("No Extra Signals Needed") were not retrieved.

## Q2. Online and learning-based persuasion; receivers who learn; persuasion without a common prior

### Takeaway
Between 2023 and 2026 the Castiglioni–Marchesi–Gatti group (Politecnico di Milano) closed the single-receiver online rate at Θ(√T) with efficient algorithms, then removed every knowledge assumption ("Without a Clue," NeurIPS 2024) at the price of an unavoidable exponential dependence on the numbers of states and actions; Li and Lin showed that when only the prior is unknown the rate collapses to Θ(log T) (Θ(log log T) for binary actions); and Lin and Chen flipped the roles, showing that a receiver running no-swap-regret learning removes the sender's commitment advantage while mean-based learners can be exploited.

### Cited Findings
- Anchor: Zu, Iyer and Xu, "Learning to Persuade on the Fly: Robustness Against Ignorance" (EC 2021; arXiv 2102.10156): unknown prior, O(√T log T) regret with a matching Ω(√T) lower bound up to logs — [arXiv 2102.10156](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.10156v1)
- Published: Bernasconi, Castiglioni, Celli, Marchesi, Trovò and Gatti, "Optimal Rates and Efficient Algorithms for Online Bayesian Persuasion," ICML 2023, PMLR 202:2164–2183. Tight Õ(T^{1/2}) regret for a single receiver under bandit/partial feedback, improving the previous Õ(T^{4/5}); first no-regret guarantees for the multi-receiver setting; no-regret algorithms with polynomial per-iteration running time that rely on type reporting — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/bernasconi23a.html); [ICML poster](https://icml.cc/virtual/2023/poster/24701)
- Published: Bacchiocchi, Bollini, Castiglioni, Marchesi and Gatti, "Online Bayesian Persuasion Without a Clue," NeurIPS 2024 (arXiv 2411.06141). Sender knows neither the prior nor the receiver's utilities. Regret upper bound Õ(C(d+n,d)·n^{3/2}·d^3·√(BT)) where d = states, n = actions, B = bit complexity; lower bounds: some instance with n = d+2 forces 2^{Ω(d)} regret (the binomial blow-up is unavoidable) and Ω(√T) for any algorithm; PAC sample complexity Õ((n^3/γ^2)·log^2(1/η)·(d^8 B + d·C(d+n,d))) with lower bounds 2^{Ω(n)}, 2^{Ω(d)} even with known prior, and Ω((1/γ^2) log(1/η)); the method searches a non-standard representation of signalling schemes to learn the receiver's best-response regions — [NeurIPS proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/8c1075ffea0abbf0bb94c9de43638e58-Abstract.html); [arXiv 2411.06141](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.06141)
- Published (extended abstract) + preprint: Li and Lin, "Information Design with Unknown Prior" (arXiv 2410.05533; v1 7 Oct 2024, v4 30 Sep 2025; preliminary version as extended abstract at ITCS 2025). Designer infers receivers' prior from actions in repeated interaction; regret relative to the known-prior optimum is tight at Θ(log T) in general and Θ(log log T) for binary actions — [arXiv 2410.05533](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05533)
- Published: Lin and Chen, "Generalized Principal-Agent Problem with a Learning Agent" (arXiv 2402.09721; v1 15 Feb 2024, v7 21 Oct 2025); short version ICLR 2025 spotlight, full version accepted at Quantitative Economics. Principal without commitment, agent learns; reduction to a one-shot problem with an approximately best-responding agent. If the agent is no-regret with regret Reg(T), the principal guarantees U* − O(√(Reg(T)/T)); if the agent is no-swap-regret, the principal gets at most U* + O(SReg(T)/T), so such learners remove the principal's advantage; mean-based (no-regret but not no-swap-regret) learners can be exploited well above U*. Results cover Bayesian persuasion explicitly — [arXiv 2402.09721](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09721)
- Published: Bernasconi, Castiglioni, Marchesi, Gatti and Trovò, "Sequential Information Design: Learning to Persuade in the Dark" (NeurIPS 2022) and journal version "Online learning in sequential Bayesian persuasion: Handling unknown priors" (2025; Politecnico di Milano record 11311/1285730). Receiver faces a sequential decision problem; with bandit feedback and a parameter α ∈ [1/2, 1], sender regret is T^α and receiver regret T^{max{α, 1−α/2}}; the trade-off is shown tight only for α ∈ [1/2, 2/3], "leaving as an open problem matching the lower bound for the other values of the parameter α"; full feedback gives Õ(√T) for both — [NeurIPS 2022 PDF](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/6604fbf7548524576c9ee2e30b0d5122-Paper-Conference.pdf); [Polimi record](https://re.public.polimi.it/handle/11311/1285730); open-problem wording quoted in [arXiv 2402.03077v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.03077v2)
- Preprint: Vurankaya and Topcu, "Learning to Persuade Privately Informed Receivers" (arXiv 2607.28342; 30 Jul 2026). Binary-action receiver also consults a fixed external signalling scheme the sender cannot see; sender observes only actions; regret Õ(T^{3/4}) against the optimum of a sender who knows the private scheme, polynomial in state-space size and the receiver's signal alphabet, via a reduction of learning the exponentially large belief-space partition to one-dimensional change-point detection — [arXiv 2607.28342](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28342)
- Preprint: arXiv 2403.08741 (2024) studies costly persuasion with continuous states and actions and reports O(log T) regret for an objective that penalises more informative policies (authors not retrieved) — [arXiv 2403.08741](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.08741v1)
- Published: Parakhonyak and Sobolev, "Persuasion without Priors," Economic Theory (accepted 2025). Sender has no prior and minimises worst-case regret relative to the Bayesian benchmark; in the binary-state, binary-action case the optimal mechanism has a continuum of messages and is a randomisation over two-message mechanisms — [Oxford ORA](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:467725e2-d1f0-460e-8d84-a7a9a7558119/files/szp38wd802)
- LLM pointer (covered by another researcher): "Verbalized Bayesian Persuasion" (arXiv 2502.01587, Feb 2025) and the simulation-oracle motivation of Harris et al. (arXiv 2311.18138) mark where persuasion meets LLM agents — [arXiv 2502.01587](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01587)

### Inferences
- The regret landscape by what is unknown: unknown prior only → Θ(log T) (Li–Lin); unknown receiver utilities, finite states → Θ(√T) in T but exponential in d and n (Bacchiocchi et al.); receiver with an unobserved private information source → Õ(T^{3/4}) (Vurankaya–Topcu, no lower bound reported).
- The "learning receiver" strand is the field's answer to "persuasion without a common prior / without commitment": commitment is replaced by the agent's learning dynamics, and the sender's achievable payoff depends on which no-regret class the agent runs.
- Sample-complexity (PAC) learning of signalling schemes is now an object in its own right (Bacchiocchi et al. 2024), distinct from regret.

### Gaps
- The conclusions of arXiv 2411.06141 and 2402.03077 were not reachable in the fetched HTML, so the authors' own stated future directions could not be quoted.
- Feng, Tang and Xu, "No-regret Bayesian recommendation" (EC 2025) was mentioned in a reference list with "arXiv 2412.14400," but that identifier resolves to Kolotilin, Li and Zapechelnyuk, "Monotone Persuasion" (18 Dec 2024); the correct identifier for the Feng–Tang–Xu paper was not found.
- Castiglioni, Celli, Marchesi and Gatti, "Online Bayesian Persuasion" (NeurIPS 2020) and "Multi-receiver Online Bayesian Persuasion" (ICML 2021) are the pre-2022 anchors but were not fetched directly.

## Q3. Robust information design and non-Bayesian receivers

### Takeaway
The economics side has settled three canonical robust models — Dworczak–Pavan's lexicographic worst-case-then-Bayesian sender (Econometrica 2022), Kosterina's sender who does not know the receiver's prior (Theoretical Economics 2022), and Hu–Weng's privately informed receiver — and 2026 preprints now ask meta-questions: when does robustness hold generically (Gradwohl–Hu–Smorodinsky), and what are axiomatic foundations for worst-case leakage (Kitano–Yanagita). The CS side treats bounded rationality computationally: quantal response (SODA 2024), approximate best response (QPTAS, 2024) and non-Bayesian updating (JPE 2022).

### Cited Findings
- Published: Dworczak and Pavan, "Preparing for the Worst but Hoping for the Best: Robust (Bayesian) Persuasion," Econometrica; posted to the journal's forthcoming page 3 May 2022 (CEPR DP15017 earlier). The sender first identifies the information policies that do best when Nature supplies additional information and coordinates receivers against her, then among those worst-case-optimal policies picks the one best under her own prior; the paper argues robustness calls for more disclosure than standard persuasion — [Econometrica forthcoming notice](https://www.econometricsociety.org/publications/news/New-Papers-Posted-to-Econometricas-Forthcoming-Page-2022-05-03.html); [CEPR DP15017](https://repec.cepr.org/repec/cpr/ceprdp/DP15017.pdf); [Northwestern PDF](https://faculty.wcas.northwestern.edu/apa522/Robust%20Persuasion.pdf)
- Published: Kosterina, "Persuasion with Unknown Beliefs," Theoretical Economics 17 (July 2022), 1075–1107. Sender does not know the receiver's prior and evaluates each information structure under the worst prior; an optimal signal has two realisations and is a hyperbola; ignorance leads the sender to recommend the high action in more states but with lower probability; greater ignorance can hurt both parties — [Theoretical Economics](https://econtheory.org/ojs/index.php/te/article/viewArticle/20221075); [econtheory.org](https://econtheory.org/?f=4742)
- Hu and Weng (2021, reported as JET): receiver may have exogenous private information unknown to the sender; sender maximises worst-case payoff over the receiver's possible private-belief distributions and then selects the best mechanism under its own conjecture (secondary summary in Iyer–Xu–Zu 2023, which groups Kosterina and Hu–Weng as "static persuasion models with robustness to exogenous receiver beliefs") — [arXiv 2307.03181](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.03181)
- Published: Feng, Ho and Tang, "Rationality-Robust Information Design: Bayesian Persuasion under Quantal Response," SODA 2024 (arXiv 2207.08253). Asks when the fully-rational-optimal scheme stays (near-)optimal for a quantal-response receiver, and designs schemes robust to uncertainty about the rationality level because estimating it "may be costly for the designer" — [arXiv 2207.08253](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.08253v1)
- Published: de Clippel and Zhang, "Non-Bayesian Persuasion," Journal of Political Economy (2022): concavification extends to a large class of belief-updating rules covering many non-Bayesian models — [IDEAS/JPE](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ucp/jpolec/doi10.1086-720464.html)
- Anchor: Tang and Ho, "On the Bayesian Rational Assumption in Information Design," HCOMP 2021: replaces full rationality with a discrete-choice model plus probability weighting, with MTurk experiments showing better fit and more effective policies — [AAAI HCOMP](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/HCOMP/article/view/18945)
- Preprint: Yang and Zhang (arXiv 2402.07426, Feb 2024) treat approximate best response as a robustness model; QPTAS; NP-hard exact (see Q1) — [arXiv 2402.07426](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07426)
- Preprint: Gradwohl, Hu and Smorodinsky, "Robustness of Persuasion to Receiver Preferences" (arXiv 2605.28265; v1 27 May 2026, v2 8 Sep 2026; econ.TH). Knightian uncertainty about receiver preferences; "continuity" (only the modeller is uncertain) and "robustness" (the sender is uncertain, modelled as worst-case or minimax-regret) are shown equivalent to each other and to robustness to tie-breaking; "robustness holds generically" — [arXiv 2605.28265](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28265)
- Preprint: Kitano and Yanagita, "An Axiomatic Model of Robust Bayesian Persuasion" (arXiv 2608.14017; 14 Aug 2026; econ.TH): sender expects the receiver may end up with more informative structures than intended and evaluates each structure under worst-case information leakage — [arXiv 2608.14017](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14017)
- Preprint: Liu, "Bayesian Risk Preference Persuasion" (arXiv 2607.13810; Jul 2026; math.OC): receiver's risk preferences shift after seeing information; optimal design characterised via the distribution of posteriors; applied to reinsurance — [arXiv 2607.13810](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13810)
- Preprint: Mensch, "Revealed Bayesian Persuasion" (arXiv 2504.01829; revised May 2026): necessary and sufficient conditions for choice data to be consistent with being persuaded, as a testing tool for empirical work — [arXiv 2504.01829](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.01829)
- Preprint: Harris, Immorlica, Lucier and Slivkins (arXiv 2311.18138): robustness to unknown receiver type via simulation queries (see Q1) — [arXiv 2311.18138](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.18138)
- Published: Parakhonyak and Sobolev, "Persuasion without Priors," Economic Theory (2025 acceptance), minimax-regret sender (see Q2) — [Oxford ORA](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:467725e2-d1f0-460e-8d84-a7a9a7558119/files/szp38wd802)

### Inferences
- Three robustness objects are in play and often conflated: uncertainty about the receiver's prior (Kosterina; Li–Lin learns it), uncertainty about the receiver's extra information (Dworczak–Pavan, Hu–Weng, Kitano–Yanagita; Vurankaya–Topcu learns it), and uncertainty about the receiver's preferences or rationality (Gradwohl–Hu–Smorodinsky, quantal response, approximate best response, Harris et al.).
- Minimax regret (Parakhonyak–Sobolev) and worst-case utility (Dworczak–Pavan, Kosterina) give qualitatively different optimal schemes (continuum of messages vs two realisations), so the choice of robustness criterion is itself a live modelling question.
- Robust persuasion and online persuasion are converging: the online papers of Q2 are robust-persuasion problems with the worst case replaced by learning over rounds.

### Gaps
- Hu and Weng's exact title, venue and year were not confirmed from a primary source (only a secondary description).
- Dworczak–Pavan's Econometrica volume/issue was not confirmed from a primary page (only the forthcoming notice).
- No 2024–2026 paper on persuading receivers with other specific behavioural models (level-k, cursed, etc.) was surfaced; coverage of that corner is thin here.

## Q4. Dynamic and sequential persuasion; Markov persuasion processes; persuasion in RL settings

### Takeaway
Markov persuasion processes (Wu et al., EC 2022) made dynamic persuasion an RL problem; the 2023–2025 follow-ups removed the assumption that the sender knows receivers' rewards (Bacchiocchi et al., NeurIPS 2025, with a tight regret versus persuasiveness-violation trade-off Õ(T^α) vs Õ(T^{1−α/2})), made receivers' beliefs depend on observed history (Iyer–Xu–Zu 2023, hard for general history lags), and studied the receiver's own sequential learning (Bernasconi et al. 2022/2025).

### Cited Findings
- Published: Wu, Zhang, Feng, Wang, Yang, Jordan and Xu, "Sequential Information Design: Markov Persuasion Process and Its Efficient Reinforcement Learning," EC 2022 (arXiv Feb 2022). Introduces MPPs: a sender with informational advantage steers a stream of myopic receivers in a finite-horizon Markov environment; with the model known, the optimal signalling policy follows a modified Bellman equation that enforces persuasiveness; in the online RL setting (utilities, priors, transitions unknown) the OP4 algorithm (Optimism–Pessimism Principle for Persuasion Process) achieves √T regret — [NSF PAR PDF](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10347107); [Google Research](https://research.google/pubs/sequential-information-design-markov-persuasion-process-and-its-efficient-reinforcement-learning/)
- Wu et al. assume the sender has perfect knowledge of receivers' rewards and deterministic rewards, and their regret bound is Õ(√T/D) where D can be exponentially small on some instances (as stated by Bacchiocchi et al. 2024) — [arXiv 2402.03077v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.03077v2)
- Gan et al., "Bayesian Persuasion in Sequential Decision-Making," AAAI 2022, and Bernasconi et al. (2023a) assume the sender knows transitions, priors and rewards and so study computing optimal policies rather than learning (as characterised by Bacchiocchi et al.) — [arXiv 2402.03077v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.03077v2)
- Published: Bacchiocchi, Stradi, Castiglioni, Marchesi and Gatti, "Markov Persuasion Processes: Learning to Persuade from Scratch," NeurIPS 2025 (arXiv 2402.03077; v1 5 Feb 2024, v2 6 Mar 2024). Sender knows nothing about the environment; full feedback: regret and persuasiveness violation both Õ(L^2|X|√(T|A||Ω|ln(1/δ))); partial feedback: regret Õ(T^α) and violation Õ(T^{1−α/2}) for α ∈ [1/2, 1]; lower bound: no algorithm achieves both R_T = o(T^α) and V_T = o(T^{1−α/2}), so the trade-off is tight — [NeurIPS 2025](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/65798a76cc176c29b6bfefe84b0a03ff-Abstract-Conference.html); [arXiv 2402.03077](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03077)
- Workshop: Bacchiocchi et al., "Markov Persuasion Processes: How to Persuade Multiple Agents from Scratch," ICML 2024 workshop — [mlanthology](https://mlanthology.org/icmlw/2024/bacchiocchi2024icmlw-markov)
- Preprint: Iyer, Xu and Zu, "Markov Persuasion Processes with Endogenous Agent Beliefs" (arXiv 2307.03181; v1 6 Jul 2023, v2 13 Jul 2023). Long-lived sender, Markov state, short-lived receivers whose beliefs depend on realised history; main model lets each receiver see history with an ℓ-period lag; sender payoff is ordered by informativeness of receivers' information (no-history is least informative); efficient algorithms for the full-history and no-history benchmarks; general lag ℓ is hard, "even identifying the right dependence on history"; a simple history-independent mechanism is approximately optimal when ℓ is large, via a robustness argument — [arXiv 2307.03181](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03181)
- Published: Bernasconi et al., NeurIPS 2022 / 2025 journal version: receiver with a sequential decision problem, sender–receiver regret trade-off (see Q2) — [NeurIPS 2022 PDF](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/6604fbf7548524576c9ee2e30b0d5122-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- Anchor: "On the Equilibrium Elicitation of Markov Games Through Information Design" (arXiv 2102.07152, 2021), information design in Markov games — [arXiv 2102.07152](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.07152)
- Published: Lin and Chen (ICLR 2025 / Quantitative Economics): repeated persuasion without commitment against a learning agent (see Q2) — [arXiv 2402.09721](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09721)

### Inferences
- The MPP line has a clear ladder: known model (Wu et al. planning; Gan et al.) → unknown transitions but known rewards (Wu et al. OP4) → nothing known (Bacchiocchi et al.) → history-dependent beliefs (Iyer–Xu–Zu). The next rung, long-lived or non-myopic receivers, is not addressed by any paper found.
- "Persuasiveness violation" as a second regret-like quantity, traded off against sender regret, is the distinctive object of the dynamic-learning literature (Bernasconi et al. 2022; Bacchiocchi et al. 2024/2025).

### Gaps
- Gan et al. (AAAI 2022) co-authors were not retrieved from a primary page.
- No paper with non-myopic (forward-looking) receivers inside an MPP was surfaced; whether that is open or simply missed by these searches is not established.
- Stated future directions in the NeurIPS 2025 MPP paper were not reachable.

## Q5. Multiple senders, intermediaries, networks, and information design combined with mechanism design

### Takeaway
Multi-sender persuasion was shown PPAD-hard (ICML 2024) and is now being attacked by receiver-side commitment (2026); the intermediary thread established that one mediator never helps the sender while two can (Arieli–Babichenko–Sandomirskiy 2022) and continues in Kosenko's 2025 job-market paper; the information-design-plus-mechanism-design strand produced Candogan–Strack (TE 2023: laminar partitional mechanisms, screening gains up to the number of types), Bergemann–Heumann–Morris "Screening with Persuasion" (JPE 2026) and the algorithmic Cai–Li–Wu auction result (NP-hard, PTAS, 1−1/e).

### Cited Findings
- Published: Hossain et al., "Multi-Sender Persuasion: A Computational Perspective," ICML 2024: PPAD-hard equilibria, NP-hard best response, neural local-equilibrium search (see Q1) — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/hossain24c.html)
- Preprint: "Multi-Sender Bayesian Persuasion with Imperfect Information" (arXiv 2607.08675; 9 Jul 2026): one receiver, several strategic senders; letting the receiver commit to action policies yields a fully informative equilibrium for any prior over the joint state space — [arXiv 2607.08675](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08675v1)
- Anchor: Li and Norman, "Sequential Persuasion," Theoretical Economics (2021): a first-moving sender cannot reduce informativeness in equilibrium; every sender except the first can without loss move only once — [econtheory.org](https://econtheory.org/?f=3474)
- Preprint: Arieli, Babichenko and Sandomirskiy, "Bayesian Persuasion with Mediators" (arXiv 2203.04285, 2022): sender reaches the receiver through a chain of mediators; the sender's optimal value is given for any number of mediators; one mediator reduces to constrained concavification and never helps the sender; a second mediator can raise the sender's value but optimal persuasion may then need more signals — [arXiv 2203.04285](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.04285)
- Working paper: Kosenko, "Mediated Persuasion" (JMP, October 2025): a mediator garbles the sender's signals before the receiver acts; conditions under which the receiver benefits; an algorithm for computing feasible posteriors — [Columbia PDF](https://www.columbia.edu/~ak2912/Andrew%20Kosenko%20JMP%20Mediated%20Persuasion.pdf)
- Published: Candogan and Strack, "Optimal Disclosure of Information to Privately Informed Agents," Theoretical Economics 18(3), 2023, 1225–1269 (arXiv 2101.10431, Jan 2021). Multiple privately informed agents in a game; an optimal mechanism always exists that is "laminar partitional" (for each reported type profile the state space is split into pieces with one recommendation each, convex hulls nested or disjoint) with bounded complexity; the ratio of the optimal payoff to the best payoff without screening can equal the number of types — [Theoretical Economics](https://www.econometricsociety.org/publications/theoretical-economics/2023/07/20/Optimal-disclosure-of-information-to-privately-informed-agents); [econtheory.org](https://econtheory.org/?f=5173)
- Published: Bergemann, Heumann and Morris, "Screening with Persuasion," Journal of Political Economy 134(2), 2026, 570–625 (arXiv 2212.03360, Dec 2022; CEPR DP17898) — [IDEAS arXiv record](https://ideas.repec.org/p/arx/papers/2212.03360.html); [CEPR](https://ideas.repec.org/p/cpr/ceprdp/17898.html)
- Preprint: Doval and Smolin, "Calibrated Mechanism Design" (arXiv 2025), listed as citing Bergemann–Heumann–Morris — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/p/arx/papers/2212.03360.html)
- Preprint: Cai, Li and Wu (arXiv 2403.08145, Mar 2024): joint signal and auction design NP-hard, PTAS, partial disclosure gives at least 1−1/e of optimal welfare (see Q1) — [arXiv 2403.08145](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08145)
- Preprint: "Optimal Fixed-Price Mechanism with Signaling" (arXiv 2411.10791; Nov 2024): seller is the informed party; places itself in "the combination of mechanism design with information design" — [arXiv 2411.10791](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.10791)
- Published: "Screening for Experiments," Games and Economic Behavior 142 (2023), 73–100 (authors not retrieved) — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/gamebe/v142y2023icp73-100.html)
- Preprint: "Exploration and Persuasion" (arXiv 2410.17086, Oct 2024), bandit exploration meets persuasion; only the listing was retrieved — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/p/arx/papers/2410.17086.html)
- Preprint: Li and Schipper, "Raising Bidders' Awareness in Second-Price Auctions" (arXiv 2412.12676, Dec 2024), information revelation in auctions — [arXiv 2412.12676](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12676)
- Preprint: Kolotilin, Li and Zapechelnyuk, "Monotone Persuasion" (arXiv 2412.14400; v1 18 Dec 2024, v2 20 Dec 2024; econ.TH): linear case; with an s-shaped objective and discrete state, optimal monotone signals are upper censorship while unrestricted optima may randomise; with an m-shaped objective and continuous state, optimal monotone signals are interval disclosures while unrestricted optima may need nonmonotone pooling; application to media censorship — [arXiv 2412.14400](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14400)
- Many-player / networks: Miyashita (arXiv 2608.00729, Aug 2026) uses an adoption game with network effects as the leading example of sparse optimal structures — [arXiv 2608.00729](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00729); "LQG Information Design" (arXiv 2312.09479) for quadratic-Gaussian games — [arXiv 2312.09479](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.09479); anchor arXiv 2109.12445 on singleton congestion games — [arXiv 2109.12445](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.12445)
- Anchor framework: Bergemann and Morris, "Information Design: A Unified Perspective," JEL 57(1), 2019, 44–95: implementable outcomes equal Bayes correlated equilibria — [AEA](https://www.aeaweb.org/doi/10.1257/jel.20181489)

### Inferences
- Multi-sender persuasion is the one subproblem where the economics equilibrium analysis (Li–Norman; competition models) and the CS hardness analysis (PPAD) point the same way: general equilibria are hard, so research moves to structural restrictions (sequential moves, receiver commitment) or heuristic local search.
- "Mechanism design with information design" has split into a structural strand (laminar partitions, monotone/censorship schemes) and an algorithmic strand (PTAS for auctions with disclosure); both share the finding that partial disclosure extracts information rent.
- Network-specific persuasion (public signals on graphs, k-cores) appears quiet in 2024–2026 relative to the surge in learning-based work; the surfaced many-player papers are LQG and sparsity results rather than network-structure results.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 paper specifically on persuasion over social networks was surfaced (searches returned the 2019 Bergemann–Morris survey and a 2021 LQG preprint); this may reflect search coverage rather than absence.
- Authors of "Screening for Experiments" (GEB 2023) and "Exploration and Persuasion" (arXiv 2410.17086) were not retrieved.
- Content of Bergemann–Heumann–Morris "Screening with Persuasion" beyond bibliographic data was not retrieved.

## Q7. Open problems that recent papers explicitly state

### Takeaway
Explicitly named open problems cluster around tightness gaps in learning rates, hardness frontiers without matching algorithms, and structural questions about history, explainability and multi-sender equilibria; the papers' own conclusion sections were largely unreachable, so this list is built from abstracts and introductions.

### Cited Findings
- Sequential persuasion with unknown priors: the sender/receiver regret trade-off T^α vs T^{max{α,1−α/2}} is tight only for α ∈ [1/2, 2/3], "leaving as an open problem matching the lower bound for the other values of the parameter α" (Bernasconi et al. 2022, as quoted by Bacchiocchi et al.) — [arXiv 2402.03077v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.03077v2)
- Without-a-clue online persuasion: regret and sample complexity are exponential in states d and actions n and this is proved unavoidable; the remaining question is which structured instance classes escape the C(d+n, d) blow-up (Appendix H notes a known-prior improvement by a factor 1/γ that is tight) — [arXiv 2411.06141](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.06141)
- Privately informed receivers online: Õ(T^{3/4}) regret with no matching lower bound reported, leaving the optimal rate open — [arXiv 2607.28342](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28342)
- Approximate-best-response persuasion: QPTAS vs NP-hardness of exact leaves the existence of a PTAS open; the authors suggest their ideas "may apply to other principal-agent problems where robustness matters" — [arXiv 2402.07426](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07426)
- Explainable design: exact optimal explainable policy is NP-hard while a 1/2-approximation is polynomial; the gap between 1/2 and the best achievable ratio, and the exponential-in-dimension PoE for rectangles, are the stated frontiers — [arXiv 2508.14196](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.14196)
- Multi-sender persuasion: equilibria are PPAD-hard; the authors retreat to local Nash equilibria found by neural extra-gradient methods, leaving tractable subclasses and global-equilibrium guarantees open — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/hossain24c.html)
- MPPs with history-dependent beliefs: optimal mechanisms for a general lag ℓ are hard, "even identifying the right dependence on history," with only large-ℓ approximations — [arXiv 2307.03181](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03181)
- Credible agents: optimality may require an unbounded number of rounds of information exchange, which raises whether bounded-round mechanisms approximate the optimum — [arXiv 2410.23989](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.23989)
- Learning agents: the sender's achievable utility depends on the agent's learning class (no-regret vs no-swap-regret vs mean-based); which dynamics real agents follow determines whether a principal can exploit them — [arXiv 2402.09721](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09721)
- Robustness: Gradwohl–Hu–Smorodinsky show robustness to receiver preferences holds generically, which leaves characterising the non-generic failure set and extending to multi-receiver settings as the natural next questions — [arXiv 2605.28265](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28265)
- Mediators: with two or more mediators optimal persuasion "may need more signals," and the general chain is solved only for the sender's value; mediator-side welfare and computation are taken up in Kosenko (2025) — [arXiv 2203.04285](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.04285); [Kosenko JMP](https://www.columbia.edu/~ak2912/Andrew%20Kosenko%20JMP%20Mediated%20Persuasion.pdf)
- Succinctness: logarithmic signal counts hold for welfare in bilateral trade but "do not carry over" to revenue, and the general communication-constrained problem is inapproximable (Dughmi–Kempe–Qiang); whether natural subclasses beyond symmetric/independent utilities admit succinct near-optimal schemes remains open (Gradwohl et al.) — [arXiv 1606.07843](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.07843); [INFORMS MOR](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/moor.2021.1218)

### Inferences
- Two cross-cutting themes recur: (i) rates and complexities are tight in T but exponential in instance size, so the live question is which structural restrictions give polynomial dependence; (ii) every relaxation of the receiver model (approximate, learning, credible, privately informed, non-Bayesian) breaks the revelation principle or commitment, and each paper rebuilds the solution concept from scratch, so a unifying treatment is missing.
- The sender-side problem that unites economics and CS is characterising when simple (two-signal, interval, monotone, explainable, history-independent) schemes are near-optimal; Kosterina, Kolotilin–Li–Zapechelnyuk, Chen–Lin–Tang–Tucker-Foltz, Iyer–Xu–Zu and Miyashita all touch it from different sides.

### Gaps
- The conclusion/future-work sections of arXiv 2411.06141 and 2402.03077 were not present in the fetched HTML ranges, so their authors' own lists of open problems could not be quoted.
- The Kamenica–Kim–Zapechelnyuk "open issues" list was paywalled.
- No paper was found that explicitly lists open problems for information design in networks or for non-myopic receivers in MPPs.
