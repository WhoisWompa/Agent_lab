# Information design and contract theory applied to AI / LLM agents (2023 – Oct 2026)

Scope note: this file covers only applications to LLM-based agents and multi-agent LLM systems. Pure theory (contracts with learning agents, online Bayesian persuasion, etc.) is covered by the other researchers and is mentioned here only where a paper explicitly targets AI/ML agents. "Preprint" means arXiv only; a venue is given wherever the arXiv comments field or a proceedings page states one. All dates are arXiv v1 dates unless stated.

## Key question 1: Persuasion with or of LLMs (LLM as sender/receiver in Bayesian persuasion, persuasion benchmarks, Bayesian updating of LLM receivers, information design for inter-agent communication, cheap talk)

### Takeaway
Between mid-2024 and 2026 a distinct line formed that puts an LLM in the sender and/or receiver seat of a Kamenica–Gentzkow persuasion game: verbalized persuasion solved with an LLM-plus-game-solver (Li et al. 2025), persuasion-trained LLMs evaluated against Bayesian-persuasion predictions (Cheng & You, ICLR 2026), persuasion without pre-commitment via type announcement (He et al., EMNLP 2026 Findings), a small "advisor" model that persuades a black-box LLM as an alignment method (Bai et al. 2024), and Google's framing-plus-signalling model where LLMs stand in for human receivers (Dütting et al. 2025). The empirical consensus is that LLM receivers update roughly in the Bayesian direction but violate Bayesian invariance, and that cheap talk among LLM agents mostly stabilises and raises cooperation, with effects strongly model- and framing-dependent.

### Cited Findings

**LLMs as senders / solvers of persuasion games**
- *Verbalized Bayesian Persuasion* — Wenhao Li, Yue Lin, Xiangfeng Wang, Bo Jin, Hongyuan Zha, Baoxiang Wang; arXiv:2502.01587 [cs.GT, cs.AI, cs.LG], submitted 3 Feb 2025, 63 pages; no venue listed (preprint). Models information design as a "verbalized mediator-augmented extensive-form game" with LLMs as sender and receiver, combines an LLM with a game solver in a generalized equilibrium-finding algorithm using verbalized commitment assumptions, verbalized obedience constraints and information obfuscation; experiments on recommendation letters, courtroom and law-enforcement dialogues reproduce classic BP results and find strategies in multi-stage natural-language settings — [arXiv 2502.01587](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01587)
- A computational-persuasion survey (May 2025) credits Li et al. 2025 as the first to carry Bayesian persuasion into natural-language settings by combining LLMs with game-theoretic methods — [Must Read: A Systematic Survey of Computational Persuasion, arXiv 2505.07775](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07775v1)
- *Towards Strategic Persuasion with Language Models* — Zirui Cheng, Jiaxuan You; arXiv:2509.22989 [cs.AI, cs.CY, cs.GT], v1 26 Sep 2025, v2 7 Mar 2026; **ICLR 2026**. Repurposes human–human persuasion datasets as environments grounded in Bayesian persuasion theory; frontier models "consistently achieve high persuasion gains" with strategies matching theoretical predictions; RL training gives even small models substantially higher persuasion gains — [arXiv 2509.22989](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22989). The design relies on an LLM Receiver that updates beliefs "reasonably well", validated by a human study; the authors concede LLMs cannot perform perfect Bayesian updating — [arXiv PDF 2509.22989](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.22989). A secondary summary adds that Llama3.2-3B trained with RL rivals large models — [bytez summary](https://bytez.com/docs/arxiv/2509.22989/paper)
- *Make an Offer They Can't Refuse: Grounding Bayesian Persuasion in Real-World Dialogues without Pre-Commitment* — Buwei He, Yang Liu, Zhaowei Zhang, Zixia Jia, Yang Yu, Huijia Wu, Zhaofeng He, Zilong Zheng, Yipeng Kang; arXiv:2510.13387 [cs.CL, cs.GT], v1 15 Oct 2025, v3 31 Aug 2026; **EMNLP 2026 Findings**. Replaces the pre-commitment assumption with a "type-induced commitment-communication mechanism": the persuader announces its possible types (honest/dishonest) inside the dialogue so the persuadee can update; two variants (Semi-Formal-Natural-Language and Fully-Natural-Language); BP strategies beat baselines across LLMs and human judges; SFT lets small models match much larger ones — [arXiv 2510.13387](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13387)
- *Efficient Model-agnostic Alignment via Bayesian Persuasion* — Fengshuo Bai, Mingzhi Wang, Zhaowei Zhang, Boyuan Chen, Yinda Xu, Ying Wen, Yaodong Yang; arXiv:2405.18718 [cs.CL], 29 May 2024; preprint, no venue. A small "Advisor" model sends a signal to persuade a black-box "Receiver" LLM; regret upper bound for the Advisor; GPT-2 as Advisor improved receivers by an average 16.1% on mathematical reasoning and 13.7% on code generation — [arXiv 2405.18718](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.18718)
- *Information Design With Large Language Models* — Paul Duetting, Safwan Hossain, Tao Lin, Renato Paes Leme, Sai Srivatsa Ravindranath, Haifeng Xu, Song Zuo (Google Research / U. Chicago); arXiv:2509.25565 [cs.GT], v1 29 Sep 2025, v2 3 Mar 2026; preprint, no venue listed. Adds a language-based "framing" that shifts the receiver's prior non-Bayesianly, after which a signalling scheme refines beliefs Bayesianly; treats LLMs as proxies for human receivers (a mapping from framings to beliefs); characterises when optimising framing alone or jointly with signalling is tractable; empirical study searches the framing space with iterative prompt optimisation plus analytical solvers for optimal signalling — [arXiv 2509.25565](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25565)

**Do LLM receivers update like Bayesians?**
- *Are LLM Belief Updates Consistent with Bayes' Theorem?* (arXiv:2507.17951, July 2025) introduces a Bayesian Coherence Coefficient and finds larger models are more Bayesian-consistent — [alphaXiv listing](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2507.17951v1)

- *BayesBench: Evaluating LLM Belief Trajectories Under Multi-Turn Evidence Accumulation* (arXiv:2606.30850, 2026) has four environments, two with closed-form posteriors; main finding: models find hidden structure but cannot reliably turn it into calibrated later predictions — [arXiv 2606.30850](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.30850v1)
- *A Model of Multi-turn Human Persuadability Using Probabilistic Belief Tracing* (arXiv:2606.05330, 2026) proposes a Bayesian-network simulated persuasion target judged closer to human reference behaviour than baseline LLM targets — [arXiv 2606.05330](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05330)
- *Learning to Persuade a Biased Receiver* (arXiv:2605.15331, 2026) appears in the same search cluster; content not verified — [arXiv PDF 2605.15331](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.15331)

**Cheap talk and communication among LLM agents**
- *Cheap Talk Stabilizes Strategic Interaction in LLM Agents* — Nunzio Lorè, Hongan Zhu, Babak Heydari; arXiv:2609.16270 [cs.MA], 14 Sep 2026; preprint. Four open-weight 7–9B models in repeated Prisoner's Dilemma, Snowdrift, Stag Hunt and Harmony, six contexts each; cheap talk is "predominantly stabilizing" (five corrected reversals, mostly in social/team framings); in Qwen two separable channels (lower action uncertainty, less round-to-round drift); a policy-content direction in late transformer layers whose removal increases switching — [arXiv 2609.16270](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16270)
- *Communication Enables Cooperation in LLM Agents: A Comparison with Curriculum-Based Approaches* — **EACL 2026 short paper** (arXiv:2510.05748): a one-word cheap-talk channel in a 4-player Stag Hunt raises cooperation from 0% to 48.3% in the EACL version; the arXiv version reports 96.7% (the two versions disagree); curriculum learning is fragile with a 27.4% payoff reduction in a public-goods game — [ACL Anthology 2026.eacl-short.23](https://aclanthology.org/2026.eacl-short.23/); [arXiv 2510.05748](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05748v3)
- *When Talk is not Cheap: Communication and Agency Problems in LLM Agents* — Shiyun Hu, Shumiao Ouyang (SSRN, finance/economics working paper): 93 LLMs in a trust game and a financial-advising scenario; requiring a verbal promise before delegation raises cooperation by 13–17 percentage points — [SSRN 6884058](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6884058)
- *Moral Hazard in Multi-Agent Language Models* — Dane Malenfant; arXiv:2607.23982 [cs.MA, cs.AI], v1 27 Jul 2026, v7 7 Sep 2026; preprint. Applies Holmström's moral-hazard-in-teams to a "Dialogue Moral Hazard Game" where an agent can pay a query cost to reveal a hidden safety fact useful to a teammate; 14 open-weight + 4 frontier models, 3,015 matched decisions each across nine query costs; GPT-5.6 Sol, Claude Opus 4.8 and Nemotron-3 Ultra track the derived private-share boundary (MAE 0.013, 0.030, 0.024); GEPA prompt optimisation raises Muse's team success 22.2%→100% while cutting query use 51.1%→0.3%, but this rests on a learned rank-label mapping (changing it drops success to 12.5% then 0%); proposes CREDIT (Counterfactual Replay for Evidence-Driven Information Transfer) — [arXiv 2607.23982](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23982)

- *AsymPuzl* (arXiv:2512.03466, Dec 2025), an asymmetric-information cooperation testbed — [arXiv 2512.03466](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.03466); *Attention Mechanism for LLM-based Agents Dynamic Diffusion under Information Asymmetry* (arXiv:2502.13160) — [arXiv 2502.13160](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13160); *What Should Agents Say? ### Inferences
- The field has split into (a) persuasion *of* an LLM receiver as a cheap alignment/steering lever (Bai et al.; Dütting et al.'s framing model), and (b) persuasion *by* an LLM sender as a capability to measure and train (Cheng & You; He et al.). Only Li et al. attempt a genuine equilibrium computation in natural language.
- Dütting et al.'s model is the first to formalise the empirically observed non-Bayesian framing effect inside a persuasion model rather than treating it as noise; the 2026 Princeton and BayesBench results supply the empirical justification.
- The cheap-talk literature is still mostly behavioural (small open-weight models, repeated 2x2 games); nobody has yet solved for or verified a Crawford–Sobel partition equilibrium with LLM senders.

### Gaps
- No paper was found that designs a communication *protocol* between LLM agents explicitly as a signalling scheme with commitment (information design proper); the engineering papers above tune what is transmitted but do not model incentives to misreport, except Malenfant 2026.
- I could not verify the authorship or results of "Learning to Persuade a Biased Receiver" (2605.15331) or "What Should Agents Say?" (2606.05304).
- No dedicated "persuasion benchmark for LLM agents" with a Bayesian-persuasion ground truth was found beyond the Cheng & You environments.

## Key question 2: Contracts and delegation to AI agents

### Takeaway
Two threads: (i) a theory-with-experiments thread led by Talgam-Cohen, Saig, Dütting and Parkes that writes pay-for-performance contracts for LLM providers whose inference cost and model choice are hidden (statistical contracts NeurIPS 2024, adaptive contracts ICML 2026, principal-agent RL 2024); (ii) a conceptual/position thread (Phelps & Ranson 2023; Gabison & Xian ACL-REALM 2025; Rauba, Cepenas & van der Schaar 2026; Kaltenpoth & Müller) arguing that human-to-LLM and orchestrator-to-sub-agent relations are principal-agent problems with agency loss. Experimental economics has started testing LLM principals and agents in contract games (Kirshner et al. 2025; Hu & Ouyang; Erlei & Meub 2026) and found that LLM agents do override principals' objectives and that strategic confidence reporting destroys most of the gains from delegation.

### Cited Findings

**Contract design explicitly targeting LLM/ML agents**
- *Incentivizing Quality Text Generation via Statistical Contracts* — Eden Saig, Ohad Einav, Inbal Talgam-Cohen (Technion / Tel Aviv); arXiv:2406.11118; **NeurIPS 2024**. Pay-per-token pricing creates moral hazard because the provider can secretly swap in a cheaper model; proposes pay-for-performance contracts where payment depends on automated quality evaluation; since internal inference costs are unknown, introduces "cost-robust" contracts and links optimal cost-robust contracts to optimal composite hypothesis tests, generalising Saig et al. NeurIPS'23 (Delegated Classification); empirically cost-robust contracts lose only a marginal amount of objective value vs cost-aware ones — [arXiv 2406.11118](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.11118); venue confirmed via [DBLP](https://dblp.uni-trier.de/pid/209/3728.html)
- *Adaptive Contracts for Cost-Effective AI Delegation* — Eden Saig, Tamar Garbuz, Ariel D. Procaccia, Inbal Talgam-Cohen, Jamie Tucker-Foltz; arXiv:2603.17212 [cs.GT, cs.AI, cs.LG], v1 17 Mar 2026, v2 2 Jul 2026; **ICML 2026**. Noisy evaluation inflates expected payments; adaptive contracts run an expensive evaluation only after a cheap coarse signal says it is worthwhile; efficient algorithms under natural assumptions or small dimensions, hardness of approximation in the general case, randomized variants; experiments on QA and code-generation datasets beat non-adaptive baselines — [arXiv 2603.17212](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17212)
- *Principal-Agent Reinforcement Learning: Orchestrating AI Agents with Contracts* — Dima Ivanov, Paul Dütting, Inbal Talgam-Cohen, Tonghan Wang, David C. - *Contracting with a Learning Agent* — Guru Guruganesh, Yoav Kolumbus, Jon Schneider, Inbal Talgam-Cohen, Emmanouil-Vasileios Vlatakis-Gkaragkounis, Joshua R. Wang, S. - *Reward Shaping for (Inference-Time) Alignment: A Stackelberg Game Perspective* — Haichuan Wang, Tao Lin, Lingkai Kong, Ce Li, Hezi Jiang, Milind Tambe; arXiv:2602.02572 [cs.LG, cs.AI], v1 31 Jan 2026; **ICML 2026**. **Experiments with LLM principals/agents and human delegation**
- *The Confidence Game: Strategic Miscalibration in Human-AI Delegation* — Raghu Arghal, Saswati Sarkar, Shirin Saeedi Bidokhti (Penn); arXiv:2610.09371 [cs.GT, cs.AI, cs.CL, cs.CY, cs.HC], 7 Oct 2026; preprint. ### Inferences
- The rigorous contract work (Saig/Talgam-Cohen/Procaccia/Parkes) models the *LLM provider* as the agent and automated evaluation as the performance signal; this makes "evaluation cost vs payment noise" (adaptive contracts) the live frontier, and ties Key Q2 directly to Key Q4 (judges).
- The conceptual papers converge on a single claim: orchestrator→sub-agent and user→agent relations are principal-agent problems because context windows and hidden reasoning create information asymmetry; none of them yet proposes an incentive-compatible mechanism — they import vocabulary, not solutions.
- Prompt-level "payment" is not a credible incentive instrument: three independent 2025 studies find weak or inconsistent effects, consistent with LLM agents not having a utility over money unless trained for it.

### Gaps
- No lab experiment was found that varies a human principal's incentive contract for an LLM agent and measures effort; Kirshner et al. and Imas–Lee–Misra are the nearest and were seen only second-hand.
- Primary sources for Kirshner et al. 2025, Imas–Lee–Misra 2025 and Erlei 2025 were not retrieved; venue and arXiv ids unknown.
- No paper on "scoring rules as contracts for LLM outputs" beyond the Saig line was found (see Key Q4 for scoring rules as evaluation).

## Key question 3: Goodhart, reward hacking and multitasking applied to LLMs (RLHF, specification gaming, eval gaming as principal-agent / multitask problems)

### Takeaway
The contract-theory reading of reward hacking is still thin: one widely cited conceptual paper (Hadfield-Menell & Hadfield 2019, pre-period), one 2026 formal preprint that extends Holmström–Milgrom multitasking to RLHF/DPO/Constitutional AI and predicts hacking severity rises without bound as tool count grows (Wang & Huang 2026), and the Stackelberg reward-shaping paper (ICML 2026). Evaluation gaming has empirical backing from the evaluation-awareness literature (frontier models detect test contexts with AUROC up to 0.89) but is rarely framed in economic terms.

### Cited Findings
- *Reward Hacking as Equilibrium under Finite Evaluation* — Jiacheng Wang, Jinbin Huang; arXiv:2603.28063 [cs.AI, cs.GT], 30 Mar 2026, 16 pages; preprint, not peer-reviewed. ### Inferences
- Wang & Huang's "coverage →0 with tools" argument is the only formal result I found that connects the multitask model to the *agentic* (tool-using) setting; it is unreviewed and its axioms are strong (e.g. "effective optimisation").
- Evaluation awareness supplies the empirical premise for treating eval gaming as strategic behaviour (the agent knows whether it is being monitored), but the papers measuring it come from the safety community and do not use mechanism-design language; the bridge is being built by Rauba et al. and the EC'26 workshops rather than by the measurement papers themselves.

### Gaps
- I found no paper titled or framed as "the alignment problem as a contract problem" in 2023–2026 beyond the above; the incomplete-contracting view remains Hadfield-Menell & Hadfield 2019.
- No paper was found that frames benchmark contamination as strategic behaviour by model developers in game-theoretic terms (the EC'26 LLM workshop lists "benchmark design as mechanism design" as a topic, which suggests the gap is recognised).
- Primary sources for the two alignment-incentive position papers were not retrieved.

## Key question 4: Judges and evaluators (peer prediction, debate as mechanism, incentive-compatible evaluation, what the evaluated agent knows)

### Takeaway
Mechanism design has entered LLM evaluation through three routes: peer prediction without ground truth (Lu et al. EC'24; Qiu, Carroll & Allen ICLR 2026; Jiang, Feng & Mehta ICLR 2026), information-theoretic "mutual evaluation" resistant to gaming (Robertson & Koyejo, TMLR 2026), and proper scoring rules for text and verbalised confidence (Wu & Hartline's ElicitationGPT; Aligned Textual Scoring Rules; ConfTuner). Debate (Irving et al.) has been formalised as a verifier-efficient interactive proof (Brown-Cohen, Irving, Piliouras ICML 2024; prover-estimator debate 2025; query-complexity 2026) and tested empirically with weak judges (Kenton et al. NeurIPS 2024; Kirchner et al. prover-verifier games 2024). The headline empirical result is inverse scaling: LLM-as-a-Judge falls below chance against deceptive models 5–20x its size, while peer prediction stays effective at >100x gaps. Criteria drift (Shankar et al. UIST 2024) is the main HCI finding about evaluators.

### Cited Findings

**Peer prediction and information-theoretic evaluation**
- *Eliciting Informative Text Evaluations with Large Language Models* — Yuxuan Lu, Shengwei Xu, Yichi Zhang, Yuqing Kong, Grant Schoenebeck; arXiv:2405.15077; **EC'24** (8–11 Jul 2024, New Haven). Two mechanisms (GPPM, GSPPM) use an LLM to map one agent's free-text report to a prediction of a peer's report; if LLM predictions are accurate enough, high effort and truthful reporting form an approximate Bayesian Nash equilibrium; on ICLR OpenReview data the mechanisms separate human reviews from GPT-4/GPT-3.5 reviews, GSPPM penalising LLM-written reviews more — [arXiv 2405.15077](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15077v3); [Cornell talk page](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/content/eliciting-informative-text-evaluations-large-language-models)

Scores answers by how well they predict each other, no ground truth; IC theorem needs shared prior and honest (possibly weak) experts; training an 8B model with a peer-prediction reward recovers most truthfulness lost to malicious fine-tuning even with a 0.135B un-finetuned reward model; resistance to deception *grows* with the expert–participant capability gap, effective at >100x, whereas LLM-as-a-Judge is worse than random against deceptive models 5–20x its size — [arXiv 2601.20299](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20299)

### Inferences
- The peer-prediction route is now the most developed mechanism-design contribution to LLM work, with three top-venue papers (EC'24, two at ICLR 2026) plus TMLR 2026, and a coherent story: scoring by mutual predictability sidesteps the judge-capability ceiling that defeats LLM-as-a-Judge.
- Debate theory and debate experiments have diverged: theory (Brown-Cohen et al.) is complexity-theoretic and does not yet connect to the empirical mixed results (Kenton et al.); nobody has published a mechanism that "designs what the evaluated agent knows about its evaluation" as an explicit information-design problem, though evaluation-awareness measurement (Key Q3) makes the question concrete.

### Gaps
- No paper was found that treats hidden vs disclosed test sets, or disclosure of evaluation criteria, as an information-design choice with a formal model.
- The "Scoring Rules!" (Lu) and "Manipulation-Resistant AI Oracles" (Monroe) EC'26 posters have no retrieved abstracts or arXiv ids.
- "Who Judges the Judge? LLM Jury-on-Demand" content unverified.

## Key question 5: Multi-agent LLM systems through a game-theory lens: surveys, mechanism design with LLM participants, LLM agents in markets, AI-agent economics, workshops

### Takeaway
Two systematic surveys (Sun et al. IJCAI 2025 / arXiv 2502.09053; Hao et al. 2026) organise the field; mechanism design *for* LLM outputs began with Google's token auction (WWW 2024 best paper) and RAG ad auctions (NeurIPS 2024); LLM agents *as* market participants show autonomous collusion (Fish, Gonczarowski, Shorrer 2024–26), format-dependent truthfulness in auctions (Shah et al. 2025), no-regret behaviour with failure cases (Park et al. Industry position papers from Microsoft Research (The Agentic Economy, 2025), Google DeepMind (Virtual Agent Economies, 2025) and the Cooperative AI Foundation (Multi-Agent Risks, 2025) set the agenda; the workshop series runs ICML 2024 Agentic Markets → AAAI-25 Economics of Modern ML → EC'26 "Game Theory and Mechanism Design with LLMs" and "Incentive-Based AI Alignment" → ICML 2026 NExT-Game. No 2025 edition of Agentic Markets was found.
