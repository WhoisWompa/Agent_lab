# Contract Theory Frontier, 2022 - October 2026 (with emphasis on algorithmic contract design)

Scope note: this file covers the research frontier only. Foundations (Holmström, Grossman-Hart, Carroll 2015) and LLM-agent applications are covered by other researchers; both appear here only as anchors or one-line pointers. Dates are given as arXiv submission dates where found; "published" means a conference or journal version was located. Where a venue is reported second-hand (from a later paper's bibliography rather than the venue itself) this is flagged.

## 1. Algorithmic contract design: simple vs optimal, complexity, combinatorial and multi-agent contracts

### Takeaway
The line started by Dütting, Roughgarden and Talgam-Cohen (EC 2019) has matured into a mapped tractability frontier: for a single agent choosing sets of actions, gross-substitutes-type reward functions are polynomial while submodular is NP-hard; for teams with binary actions, submodular and XOS rewards admit constant-factor approximations and those constants are now known to be tight; for teams where each agent picks sets of actions, submodular rewards have a constant-factor approximation and XOS is the named open frontier. The 2026 preprints push into online arrival of agents, fairness constraints (equal pay, anonymity, non-discrimination, envy-freeness), and a geometric "demand types" theory that conjectures the largest tractable class.

### Cited Findings
- The field's reference text is Dütting, Feldman, Talgam-Cohen, "Algorithmic Contract Theory: A Survey", Foundations and Trends in Theoretical Computer Science vol. 16 no. 3-4, pp. 211-412, publication date 31 Dec 2024; arXiv 2412.16384 v1 submitted 20 Dec 2024. It states that it "evolved from a tutorial at ... EC 2019, and a tutorial at ... STOC 2022". — [nowpublishers TCS-113](https://nowpublishers.com/article/Details/TCS-113); [arXiv 2412.16384](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16384)
- The survey's chapters are: 2 basic model; 3 optimal contracts (LP approach, implementable actions, binary action/outcome cases, shortcomings); 4 linear contracts (geometry, worst-case approximation, robust max-min optimality); 5 computational complexity (combinatorial actions, multi-agent, combinatorially-many outcomes, multiple principals); 6 private types; 7 data-driven contracts; 8 incentive-aware ML; 9 incomplete, vague and ambiguous contracts; 10 contracts for social good; 11 "beyond contracts" (delegation, scoring rules); 12 discussion. — [arXiv HTML 2412.16384v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.16384v1) (text extracted from the PDF)
- Survey, Section 3.1: whether computing an optimal (single-agent, explicit-matrix) contract admits a strongly polynomial algorithm, possibly under regularity assumptions, is open. — [arXiv HTML 2412.16384v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.16384v1)
- Survey, Section 4.3, Table 1 caption: "The worst-case approximation ratio for m=2 outcomes is still (partially) open"; the known lower bound is 2 when both outcomes have positive reward. The gap between the best monotone contract and the optimal contract is at least n-1 (DRT19 construction), and there are instances where the revenue-welfare gap is of order n. — [arXiv HTML 2412.16384v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.16384v1)
- "Combinatorial Contracts" (Dütting, Ezra, Feldman, Kesselheim), FOCS 2021, arXiv 2109.14260: a single agent chooses any subset of actions; success probability is a set function. If it is gross substitutes, the optimal contract is found with polynomially many value queries; the submodular case is NP-hard. — [arXiv 2109.14260](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.14260); [Google Research page](https://research.google/pubs/combinatorial-contracts/)
- "Multi-Agent Contracts" (Dütting, Ezra, Feldman, Kesselheim), STOC 2023, arXiv 2211.05434: a principal motivates a team, each agent with a binary action; constant-factor approximations to the optimal linear contract for submodular and XOS rewards. — [arXiv 2211.05434](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.05434)
- "Multi-Agent Combinatorial Contracts" (same authors), arXiv 2405.08260 (May 2024), SODA 2025: each agent in a team picks a set of actions; the model "lacks key properties" (monotonicity) that made the two earlier special cases tractable. — [arXiv 2405.08260](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.08260v1)
- Feldman, "Combinatorial Contract Design: Recent Progress and Emerging Frontiers", arXiv 2510.15065, submitted 16 Oct 2025 (single-author survey). Its results tables, as extracted: single-agent multi-action, poly-time with value oracle for additive, gross substitutes, "ultra", and supermodular-reward/submodular-cost; NP-hard for submodular with value queries, and exponentially many demand queries needed for submodular; no constant-factor approximation for submodular under value oracles unless P=NP, n^(1/2-eps) hardness for XOS; with value+demand oracles, monotone rewards admit an FPTAS using O(n^2/eps) queries. Multi-agent binary-action: additive is NP-hard but has an FPTAS; submodular (value oracle) and XOS (demand oracle) have poly-time constant approximations. — [arXiv 2510.15065](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15065); [HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15065v1)
- Feldman survey, Section 4.4 hardness for multi-agent binary actions: for submodular rewards, even with value and demand oracles any sub-exponential-query algorithm achieves an eta-approximation (some constant eta>1) only with exponentially small probability; for XOS with value oracles only, nothing better than about n^(1/6); for subadditive, nothing better than Omega(sqrt n) while a simple O(n) (best single agent) exists; for supermodular, no poly-time O(1)-approximation and no additive FPTAS unless P=NP. — [arXiv HTML 2510.15065v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15065v1)
- Feldman survey, Section 5 (multi-agent, multi-action): every contract admits a pure Nash equilibrium because the induced game is a potential game, and an equilibrium is found with one demand query; for submodular rewards with value and demand oracles there is a poly-time constant-factor approximation that holds for every equilibrium of the contract; NP-hard already for additive and for GS rewards with binary actions; with budgets, submodular rewards admit no finite approximation for randomized poly-time algorithms even with demand oracles. — [arXiv HTML 2510.15065v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15065v1)
- "Black-Box Lifting and Robustness Theorems for Multi-Agent Contracts" (Dütting, Ezra, Feldman, Kesselheim), arXiv 2511.19358, November 2025 (preprint): moves from pure Nash to mixed Nash and coarse-correlated equilibria; poly-time constant approximation for submodular rewards in any CCE; such guarantees "provably unattainable" for XOS. — [alphaxiv 2511.19358](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2511.19358)
- "Combinatorial Contracts Through Demand Types" (Baldwin, Dütting, Feldman, Schlesinger), arXiv 2604.15125, submitted 16 Apr 2026 (preprint): a geometric framework linking combinatorial contracts to demand types from consumer theory; defines the class of All Substitutes and Complements (ASC) functions with at most O(n^2) critical contract values, generalizing gross substitutes, supermodular and ultra; conjectures ASC is the largest class with polynomially many critical values under mild assumptions; gives demand queries from value queries for "succinct" demand types. — [arXiv 2604.15125](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15125)
- "Online Multi-Agent Contracts" (Dütting, Feldman, Gal-Tzur, Kesselheim), arXiv 2608.18241, submitted 18 Aug 2026 (preprint): agents arrive online, each active with some probability; the principal offers a linear share on arrival; O(1)-competitive policy for submodular rewards; if agents must decide on the spot, Omega(log n / (log log n)^2) lower bound; for XOS, Omega(log log n / log log log n) for any online policy, a submodular/XOS separation that does not exist offline. These are competitive ratios, not regret bounds. — [arXiv 2608.18241](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18241)
- "Multi-Agent Contract Design Beyond Binary Actions" (Cacciamani, Bernasconi, Castiglioni, Gatti), EC 2024 (venue reported from a later bibliography; no arXiv id located). — [search result listing](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.18876)
- "Sequential Contracts" (Ezra, Feldman, Schlesinger), arXiv 2403.09545, March 2024: an agent takes costly actions over time and chooses their order; the agent's best-response problem is equivalent to Pandora's Box. The survey's Section 12 singles this out as the example of dynamic, combinatorial contracting. — [arXiv 2403.09545](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.09545v1)
- 2025-2026 multi-agent preprints located by title and arXiv id (contents not read): "Budget-Feasible Contracts" (Feldman, Gal-Tzur, Ponitka, Schlesinger) arXiv 2504.01773; "Welfare and Beyond in Multi-Agent Contracts" arXiv 2504.18876; "Equal-Pay Contracts" arXiv 2601.15478; "Anonymous Contracts" arXiv 2602.12118; "Multi-Agent Non-Discriminatory Contracts" arXiv 2601.16835; "Strict Fairness at What Cost? Envy-Free Contracts with Subsidies" arXiv 2606.25431. — [arXiv 2504.01773](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.01773); [arXiv 2504.18876](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.18876); [arXiv 2601.15478](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.15478); [arXiv 2602.12118](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.12118); [arXiv 2601.16835](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.16835); [arXiv 2606.25431](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.25431)
- Goel and Hann-Caruthers study multi-agent contracts with a shared exclusive-use budget, each agent working on an individual succeed/fail task; a preliminary version appeared at EC 2024 under a different title (reported second-hand). — [search result](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.01773)
- Survey Section 5.4 (combinatorially-many outcomes): for arbitrarily many actions and an m-dimensional outcome space, it is NP-hard to approximate the optimal IC principal utility within any constant factor even with additive rewards; with constantly many actions there is a poly(m, 1/eps) algorithm returning an eps-IC contract with utility at least OPT; the survey calls this "a sharp dichotomy in terms of approximability between a constant and non-constant number of actions" and names going beyond product distributions to correlated succinct outcome distributions as the open direction. — [arXiv 2412.16384](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16384) (PDF text)
- Survey Section 5.5 (multiple principals / common agency, VCG contracts): "An interesting open direction is to explore approximately welfare-optimal contracts"; and a formal study of the connection between "contractible contracts" and smart contracts is suggested. — [arXiv 2412.16384](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16384) (PDF text)

### Inferences
- The tractable/hard boundary for single-agent combinatorial actions is now essentially characterised by "substitutes-like" structure; the 2026 demand-types paper is the first attempt at a necessary-and-sufficient statement (its ASC conjecture). If it holds, the single-agent frontier would be closed and the live work shifts to teams.
- The multi-agent frontier is defined by one question repeated in both surveys and the 2025 lifting paper: whether the submodular constant-factor results extend to XOS. The online-arrival paper's separation between submodular and XOS suggests the answer may be negative in some settings.
- The 2026 crop of "equal-pay", "anonymous", "non-discriminatory" and "envy-free" contract papers indicates a new sub-thread: fairness or symmetry constraints on team contracts, analogous to the fairness thread in mechanism design.

### Gaps
- I did not read the 2025-2026 fairness and budget preprints beyond their titles, so their results and venues are unverified.
- Pre-2022 anchor (DRT "Simple versus Optimal Contracts", EC 2019) is not separately sourced here; the survey's Section 4 is the reference.

## 2. Learning in contracts: online contract design, sample complexity, agents who learn

### Takeaway
Online contract design is treated as a Stackelberg bandit: with m outcomes the minimax regret is exponential in m in general (Zhu et al. 2022/2023), but polynomial when the number of actions is constant (Bacchiocchi et al., ICLR 2024) or under first-order-stochastic-dominance plus diminishing-returns regularity (Chen et al., EC 2024); the offline, type-sampling view was formalised through pseudo-dimension in 2025. The complementary thread models the agent as a no-regret learner and shows the principal can exploit it with a dynamic contract (Guruganesh et al., NeurIPS 2024).

### Cited Findings
- Zhu, Bates, Yang, Wang, Jiao, Jordan, "The Sample Complexity of Online Contract Design", arXiv 2211.05732, first posted November 2022, latest version May 2023 (the survey cites it as Zhu et al. [2023], i.e. EC 2023): contracts in [0,1]^m treated as arms of a continuum-armed bandit; Stackelberg regret upper bound ~O(sqrt(m) T^(1-1/(2m+1))) and lower bound Omega(T^(1-1/(m+2))); "exponentially many samples in the number of outcomes are necessary and sufficient for a near-optimal contract" in general; for linear contracts the bound is polynomial and independent of n and m. — [arXiv 2211.05732](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05732v2); [survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16384)
- Bacchiocchi, Castiglioni, Marchesi, Gatti, "Learning Optimal Contracts: How to Exploit Small Action Spaces", ICLR 2024, arXiv 2309.09801: with a constant number of actions, an approximately optimal contract is learned in a number of rounds polynomial in the outcome-space size, answering a question of Zhu et al.; gives ~O(T^(4/5)) regret. The survey states it as regret at most ~O(mn poly(n,m) S^(4/5) log(1/delta)). — [ICLR 2024 proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/3322a9a72a1707de14badd5e552ff466-Abstract-Conference.html); [arXiv 2309.09801](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.09801v4)
- Survey Section 7.2: "It remains an open question to prove (or disprove) that the problem admits a polynomial regret bound when the number of actions is polynomial in m. It is also open what the corresponding regret bounds are when the agent is sampled afresh in each round." — [arXiv 2412.16384](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16384) (PDF text)
- Chen, Chen, Deng, Huang, "Are Bounded Contracts Learnable and Approximately Optimal?", EC 2024 (July 2024), arXiv 2402.14486: two algorithms find a near-optimal bounded contract with polynomially many queries under FOSD (costlier actions give better outcome distributions) and diminishing returns; they learn the outcome distributions directly rather than discretising the contract space; bounded contracts can be arbitrarily worse than unbounded ones under multiplicative or additive approximation but satisfy a "mixed" approximation notion. — [arXiv 2402.14486](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14486v1)
- Survey Section 7.3 names as open: whether analogous sample-complexity results hold under only one of FOSD or CDFP; whether positive online results carry over when the agent is resampled each round. Survey Section 7.1: "An intriguing general open problem is whether there are other 'simple' contracts that can be learned efficiently, while allowing the principal to achieve higher expected utility." — [arXiv 2412.16384](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16384) (PDF text)
- Survey Section 7, "Additional Directions and Open Questions": "there is a notable gap between the frameworks studied by Ho et al. [2016]" and Zhu et al.; and "most of the existing work on learning in contracts has focused on the online learning setup, with rather strong impossibilities stemming from the hardness of learning an agent's type with restricted (bandit-type) feedback. An intriguing direction for future work is to develop a more comprehensive theory of the offline sample complexity of learning contracts, with different forms of feedback (e.g., bandit- and expert-type feedback)", drawing on Morgenstern and Roughgarden [2015]. — [arXiv 2412.16384](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16384) (PDF text)
- Dütting, Feldman, Ponitka, Soumalias, "The Pseudo-Dimension of Contracts", arXiv 2501.14474 (January 2025), EC 2025 pp. 514-539: offline learning of near-optimal contracts from sampled agent types; pseudo-dimension bounds sample complexity and measures the intrinsic complexity of a contract class (simplicity vs optimality); adapted to the online setting, polynomially many type samples suffice to learn near-optimal bounded contracts. A CORDIS listing cites a Mathematics of Operations Research DOI (10.1287/moor.2024.0684), unconfirmed. — [arXiv 2501.14474](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14474v1); [Google Research](https://research.google/pubs/the-pseudo-dimension-of-contracts/)
- Zuo, "Harnessing the Continuous Structure" (arXiv 2403.07143, March 2024; v3 later): with continuous actions and a diminishing-returns assumption, the principal's utility is Lipschitz in the contract, reducing the problem to Lipschitz bandits and giving polynomial sample complexity for identical agents. — [arXiv 2403.07143](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.07143v3)
- Bacchiocchi, Gan, Castiglioni, Marchesi, Gatti, "Contract Design Under Approximate Best Responses", arXiv 2502.15523 (February 2025): the agent may take any action that is only slightly suboptimal; poly-time algorithm for the robust optimal contract; a secondary review reports a no-regret learning extension. — [arXiv 2502.15523](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.15523v1)
- Guruganesh, Kolumbus, Schneider, Talgam-Cohen, Vlatakis-Gkaragkounis, Wang, Weinberg, "Contracting with a Learning Agent", NeurIPS 2024, arXiv 2401.16198: the agent runs a no-regret (mean-based) algorithm; in the success/failure case the optimal principal strategy is a linear contract with scalar alpha>0 followed by a switch to the zero contract, which lets the agent "drift" and yields the principal reward at no cost; sometimes both parties beat the best static contract; extends to rescaled non-linear contracts and unknown horizon. — [arXiv 2401.16198](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.16198); [NeurIPS listing](https://mlanthology.org/neurips/2024/guruganesh2024neurips-contracting)
- Lin and Chen, "Generalized Principal-Agent Problem with a Learning Agent", arXiv 2402.09721 (2024): repeated generalized principal-agent games where the agent learns; shows how no-regret vs mean-based learning changes what the principal can achieve. — [arXiv 2402.09721](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.09721v3)
- Survey Section 12 names dynamic contracting with a learning agent (Guruganesh et al. 2024) and MDP-based delegation to AI agents (Bollini et al. 2024, Ivanov et al. 2024, Wu et al. 2024) as directions not covered elsewhere in the survey. — [arXiv 2412.16384](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16384) (PDF text)
- LLM/AI-agent pointer only: "Principal-Agent Reinforcement Learning: Orchestrating AI Agents with Contracts" arXiv 2407.18074 (2024) and "Agent Contracts" arXiv 2601.08815 (2026) belong to the AI-agent researcher's scope. — [search listing](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.08815v2)

### Inferences
- The exponential-in-m lower bound is the central negative result of the thread; every positive result since 2023 buys polynomial rates by restricting something (few actions, regularity, linear or bounded contract class, continuous action structure). "Which structure is necessary" is the implicit open problem.
- The "learning agent" thread inverts the usual asymmetry: a principal who knows the agent is a mean-based learner can extract more than against a rational agent, which is likely to connect to robustness questions (what if the learner is not mean-based).

### Gaps
- I could not confirm the venue of Zuo (2403.07143) or Lin and Chen (2402.09721).
- Ho, Slivkins and Vaughan (JAIR 2016) is cited via the survey only; the original is not fetched here.

## 3. Robust contract design beyond Carroll

### Takeaway
Three strands: (i) economics is re-examining Carroll's linearity result under randomisation and generalised uncertainty (Walton-Carroll 2022; Kambhampati 2023; Kambhampati, Peng, Tang, Toikka, Vohra 2025); (ii) CS gives worst-case approximation guarantees for linear contracts under Bayesian type uncertainty (Alon, Dütting, Li, Talgam-Cohen) and robustness to approximate best response; (iii) ambiguity is being used as a design tool rather than a nuisance (Dütting, Feldman, Peretz, Samuelson), with distributionally robust formulations appearing in operations (Bayrak-Bichler).

### Cited Findings
- Anchor: Carroll (AER 2015) shows that a principal who knows only some of the agent's available actions and evaluates contracts by worst case over the unknown ones optimally uses a linear contract; Walton and Carroll, Econometrica vol. 90 no. 5 (2022) pp. 2129-2159, give "Richness" and "Responsiveness" conditions that are sufficient and in a sense necessary for linear optimality in a general maxmin problem. — [Walton-Carroll, Econometrica](https://www.economics.utoronto.ca/gabriel.carroll/ECTA17386.pdf)
- Kambhampati (2023) shows in Carroll's framework that randomising over two linear contracts strictly improves the principal's guarantee over any deterministic contract; Kambhampati, Peng, Tang, Toikka, Vohra (PIER working paper 25-004, 2025) show any randomisation over contracts can be improved by making each contract in its support linear, so linear contracts remain robustly optimal within randomised contracts. Working paper, not published. — [PIER 25-004](https://economics.sas.upenn.edu/system/files/working-papers/25-004%20PIER%20Paper%20Submission.pdf)
- Survey Section 4.4.1 reports that Dütting et al. [2021a] extend max-min optimality of linear contracts to the combinatorial-action setting when only the expected reward of each set of actions is known; the survey calls finding "formal justifications for simple contracts" a "central challenge in the economic literature on contracts". — [arXiv HTML 2412.16384v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.16384v1)
- Alon, Dütting, Li, Talgam-Cohen, "Bayesian Analysis of Linear Contracts" (EC 2023 abstract), arXiv 2211.06850 (November 2022; v3 March 2025 retitled "Approximate Optimality of Linear Contracts Under Uncertainty"): with a private Bayesian type, linear contracts give an O(1) approximation to the optimal (possibly randomised) menu under small-tail conditions, and the guarantee improves with a metric of the degree of uncertainty; debt contracts can lose a factor growing with the number of actions. — [arXiv 2211.06850](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2211.06850v3)
- Survey Section 6 (private types): optimal deterministic menus for multi-dimensional settings are poly-time when both the number of actions and types is constant; "The complexity of the more general case with a general number of actions and constantly-many types is, to our knowledge, open"; and "for many of the worst-case comparisons between different classes of contracts and benchmarks, there are rather significant gaps between the best-known lower bounds and upper bounds." — [arXiv 2412.16384](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16384) (PDF text)
- Dütting, Feldman, Peretz, Samuelson, "Ambiguous Contracts", arXiv 2302.07621 (v5 September 2024): a principal who deliberately offers an ambiguous set of contracts to a max-min (ambiguity-averse) agent can strictly gain; ambiguity pushes optimal contracts toward simplicity; the advantage vanishes if the agent can mix actions. Survey Section 9: under MRLP, optimal ambiguous contracts consist of two classic contracts (two SOP contracts, or two step contracts if monotone); the survey defines "ambiguity-proof" classes of contracts and names as open: bounded-size ambiguous contracts beyond settings where they are optimal, ambiguous contracts with multiple agents, and "whether mixed strategies still eliminate the power of ambiguity"; vague and incomplete contracts "remain mostly unexplored from an algorithmic perspective". — [arXiv 2302.07621](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07621v5); [survey PDF text](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16384)
- Bayrak and Bichler, "Distributionally Robust Contract Design with Deferred Inspection", arXiv 2506.04767 (listed version January 2026, preprint): worst-case revenue under moment-based ambiguity about the agent's value distribution; single-agent robust optimum has a concave allocation rule and linear payments; with multiple agents dominant-strategy IC becomes necessary. — [arXiv 2506.04767](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.04767v2)
- Zuo, "Linear Contracts in Multitasking: Robustness, Uniformity, and Learning", arXiv 2405.20642 (v1 May 2024, v2 March 2025): robustness result for linear contracts in multitask settings when only first-moment information is known. — [arXiv 2405.20642](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20642v2)
- Bacchiocchi et al., "Contract Design Under Approximate Best Responses" (arXiv 2502.15523, February 2025) is a robustness-to-behaviour result: the contract must work for any epsilon-best response. — [arXiv 2502.15523](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.15523v1)

### Inferences
- The economics thread and the CS thread now agree on the same object (linear contracts) but justify it differently: maxmin over unknown actions (Carroll-style) vs. constant-factor approximation under distributional uncertainty (Alon et al.). The survey explicitly treats both as Section 4.4.
- "Ambiguous contracts" is the one robustness-flavoured idea that is native to the algorithmic community and has an explicit open-problem list; it is a likely locus for 2026-2027 work.

### Gaps
- Whether "Ambiguous Contracts" has a journal version (I did not confirm; search results give only arXiv v5 of September 2024 and an EC 2023 appearance implied by the survey's citation "Dütting et al. [2024c]").
- I found no 2024-2026 paper on "contracts with unknown agent types" that is distinct from the typed-agent (adverse selection + moral hazard) line already covered by the survey's Section 6.

## 4. Contracts combined with information design, persuasion and screening

### Takeaway
Three formal combinations now exist: a regulator or principal choosing what signal about the agent's action is observed (Babichenko, Talgam-Cohen, Xu, Zabarnyi, EC 2024 / GEB 2026); joint design of information about the task and the contract (Castiglioni and Chen, SODA 2025); and a unifying "generalized principal-agency" framework containing contract design, information design and Bayesian Stackelberg games (Gan, Han, Wu, Xu). A September 2026 preprint reverses the direction and lets the agent choose which actions to disclose.

### Cited Findings
- Babichenko, Talgam-Cohen, Xu, Zabarnyi, "Information Design in the Principal-Agent Problem", EC 2024; journal version Games and Economic Behavior vol. 155 (2026) pp. 55-69; arXiv 2209.13688 (first posted September 2022): the principal observes a signal about the agent's action from an information structure chosen by a regulator; characterises implementable actions and utility profiles for risk-neutral and risk-averse agents under limited liability; deciding implementability under a restricted class of signalling constraints is NP-complete in general; worst implementable welfare degrades gracefully as information becomes noisier (last two points from a mirror listing). — [NSF PAR copy](https://par.nsf.gov//servlets/purl/10532235); [Talgam-Cohen publication list](https://inbaltalgam.com/Inbal%20Talgam-Cohen%20Selected%20Publications.pdf)
- Castiglioni and Chen, "Hiring for An Uncertain Task: Joint Design of Information and Contracts", arXiv 2407.05459 (July 2024), SODA 2025 (venue from a later bibliography). — [arXiv 2407.05459](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.05459)
- Gan, Han, Wu, Xu, "Generalized Principal-Agency", arXiv 2209.01146 (v2 February 2024): unifies contract design, information design and Bayesian Stackelberg games as special cases of one model. — [arXiv 2209.01146](https://arxiv.org/html/2209.01146v2)
- Zhao, Mezzetti, Renou, Tomala, "Contracting over persistent information", Theoretical Economics 2024 (published): a dynamic principal-agent problem in which information disclosure is the principal's only incentive instrument. — [Warwick WRAP](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/177278)
- Deng and Li, "Strategic Disclosure of Action Space in Principal-Agent Contracts", arXiv 2609.25410 (v1 21 September 2026, v2 30 September 2026; preprint, cs.GT cross-listed econ.TH): the agent chooses which actions to disclose before the principal designs a contract on the disclosed set; with unverifiable costs the agent extracts the full first-best surplus and the principal gets zero; with verifiable costs and binary outcomes or linear contracts, the agent's optimal disclosure is a two-variable convex program; the agent secures at least 1/e of first-best surplus (hence a 1/e welfare guarantee); principal revenue is Theta(1/log L) of first-best when expected rewards of non-null actions vary by a factor L. — [arXiv 2609.25410](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25410)
- Auster and Pavoni, "Optimal delegation and information transmission under limited awareness", Theoretical Economics 2024 (published): the principal is unaware of some feasible actions, and the agent optimally leaves her unaware. — [Theoretical Economics](https://www.econometricsociety.org/publications/theoretical-economics/2024/01/26/Optimal-delegation-and-information-transmission-under-limited-awareness)
- Survey footnote: problems mixing two of the three basic incentive problems (hidden type / hidden action / who moves first) exist, "e.g., Bernasconi et al. [2024]"; the survey's Figure 1 marks the fourth cell (hidden action, informed party moves first) as "Not studied". — [arXiv 2412.16384](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16384) (PDF text)
- Survey Section 12: contracts with inspections, where the principal can buy extra information about the action (Dye 1986; Georgiadis and Szentes 2020; Halac et al. 2024 in economics; Ball and Knoepfle 2023, Ezra et al. 2024c, Fallah and Jordan 2024 computationally); Ezra et al. 2024c optimise which sets of actions to inspect under a set-cost function. — [arXiv 2412.16384](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16384) (PDF text)
- The survey's Section 1 "Digression" explicitly places contract design, mechanism design and information design in Salanié's two-by-two classification and says CS work has mostly been on the first two. — [arXiv 2412.16384](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16384) (PDF text)

### Inferences
- The Deng-Li 2026 preprint effectively studies the "Not studied" cell of the survey's Figure 1 (informed party moves first with hidden actions), by letting the agent move first through disclosure; this is a very recent opening.
- The information-plus-contracts thread has a published economics branch (TE 2024, GEB 2026) and an algorithmic branch (EC 2024, SODA 2025); both are small compared to combinatorial contracts.

## 5. Multitasking, performance-measure distortion, Goodhart's law and strategic classification

### Takeaway
The Holmström-Milgrom multitask question has been revisited from three directions: multitask linear contracts with learning (Zuo 2024-2025), "metric design" where the evaluated agent chooses what the principal can measure (Wang et al., WWW 2025), and paradigm-agnostic formalisations of Goodhart's law (Majka and El-Mhamdi 2025). The algorithmic-contract survey treats strategic classification as "incentive-aware evaluation" and names the combination of accuracy and incentive objectives as the open direction; no 2024-2026 paper casting Goodhart's law directly as a principal-agent contract problem was found.

### Cited Findings
- Zuo, arXiv 2405.20642 (v1 May 2024, "Principal-Agent Multitasking: the Uniformity of Optimal Contracts and its Efficient Learning via Instrumental Regression"; v2 March 2025, "Linear Contracts in Multitasking: Robustness, Uniformity, and Learning"): the principal sees a noisy signal per task; when tasks are perfect substitutes and costs homogeneous, the optimal linear contract depends only on each task's marginal utility and the degree of homogeneity; learning the contract is a measurement-error regression solved by instrumental regression. — [arXiv 2405.20642](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20642v2)
- Wang et al., "Relying on the Metrics of Evaluated Agents", ACM Web Conference 2025 (arXiv 2402.14005, February 2024): metric design as an agency game in which the agent decides whether to disclose a cost-related observable; agents disclose metrics that separate hard from easy tasks and hide those that pick out easy tasks; letting the agent add noise to the signal is often preferred to full disclosure and can raise welfare; tested on rideshare data. — [arXiv 2402.14005](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.14005v1); [HUJI listing](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/relying-on-the-metrics-of-evaluated-agents/)
- Majka and El-Mhamdi, "The Strong, Weak and Benign Goodhart's law. An independence-free and paradigm-agnostic formalisation", arXiv 2505.23445 (May 2025, preprint): relaxes the independence assumption of earlier formalisations and distinguishes strong, weak and benign forms; builds on Manheim-Garrabrant and on earlier formalisations [EMH24, KTGA24] where the law holds or fails depending on the relative tail thickness of goal and discrepancy; notes a 2020 framework (ZHM20) "inspired by incomplete contracting" as the nearest contract-theoretic treatment. — [arXiv 2505.23445](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.23445)
- "Goodhart's Law in Reinforcement Learning", arXiv 2310.09144 (October 2023; ICLR 2024 per search), and the 2026 reward-hacking survey arXiv 2604.13602 define reward hacking as optimising an imperfect proxy lowering expected true-reward performance. — [arXiv 2310.09144](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.09144v1); [arXiv 2604.13602](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.13602)
- Anchor: Hardt, Megiddo, Papadimitriou, Wootters, "Strategic Classification" (arXiv 1506.06980, 2015; ITCS 2016) models classification as a sequential game between "Jury" and "Contestant" and ties it to Goodhart's law. — [arXiv 1506.06980](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.06980)
- Rosenfeld et al., "One-shot strategic classification under unknown costs", ICML 2024: the learner does not know the users' response costs and minimises worst-case risk over an uncertainty set of costs. — [ICML 2024 text](https://object.cloud.sdsc.edu/v1/AUTH_da4962d3368042ac8337e2dfdd3e7bf3/ml-papers-txt/ICML/2024/oneshot_strategic_classification_under_unknown_costs__111c19be.txt)
- Survey Section 8 ("incentive-aware evaluation"): strategic agents allocate a budget of effort among admissible and inadmissible actions in response to a classifier; general evaluation schemes have the same implementability power as monotone ones for a single agent (Theorem 8.3) but not in general; "One interesting open direction is to study a combination of objectives for incentive-aware evaluation", since the designer "cares about accurate classification, in addition to incentivizing desirable actions and maximizing self-improvement". — [arXiv 2412.16384](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16384) (PDF text)
- Saig, Talgam-Cohen, Rosenfeld, "Delegated Classification", NeurIPS 2023 (arXiv 2306.11475): outsourced ML as a principal-agent game; budget-optimal contracts take a threshold form and connect to the Neyman-Pearson lemma in the binary-action case; built from learning-curve/scaling-law estimates. The survey's Section 8 states that in Cai et al. [2015] the accuracy of the agent's outcome is unknown, while in Ananthakrishnan et al. [2024] and Saig et al. [2023] accuracy is known but its relation to effort is not, framing delegated ML as contract design "when the setting details are not entirely known". — [arXiv 2306.11475](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.11475); [NeurIPS 2023](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/2aab664e0d1656e8b56c74f868e1ea69-Abstract.html)

### Inferences
- The multitask/Goodhart problem is being attacked from three largely disconnected literatures (contract learning, metric-disclosure games, RL reward-hacking formalisms); the survey's Section 8 is the only place where strategic classification and contract design are treated in one framework, and its stated open problem (combined accuracy-plus-incentive objectives) is exactly the performance-measure-design question.
- Wang et al. is the closest thing to "measure management" in the recent CS literature, but it reverses the usual direction: the agent, not the principal, designs the measure.

### Gaps
- No 2024-2026 paper was found that formalises Goodhart's law as a multitask contract (Holmström-Milgrom) problem in the principal-agent sense; the search returned RL/alignment formalisations instead. This is a genuine thin spot, not a search failure, as two differently phrased searches gave the same result.
- Economics-journal multitasking work from 2022-2026 (e.g. in JET, GEB) was not located by the searches run.

## 6. The uninformed principal: a principal who does not know her own objective

### Takeaway
The literature is thin. I found no 2022-2026 paper in which the principal's own preferences are learned or revealed during a contractual relationship. The nearest work is in delegation: delegation of learning (where the principal lacks information about the state, not her tastes), robust delegation where the principal does not know the agent's preferences (Gan 2025), delegation under limited awareness of actions (Auster-Pavoni, TE 2024), and the survey's "beyond contracts" chapter on delegation and scoring rules.

### Cited Findings
- Survey Section 11 ("Incentivizing Effort Beyond Contracts") covers delegation and scoring-rule design as related problems; Section 12 frames the whole field as "optimizing the effort of others". Neither mentions a principal with unknown own preferences. — [arXiv 2412.16384](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16384) (PDF text)
- "Delegation of Learning" (European Economic Review 2025, SOAS repository): the principal can hand over both learning and decision to an expert, or retain authority to stop the expert's learning; when preferences are closely aligned complete delegation beats flexible delegation because the expert changes how he learns when he expects interruption. This is uncertainty about the state, not about the principal's preferences. — [SOAS repository](https://eprints.soas.ac.uk/43415/1/1-s2.0-S0014292125000315-main.pdf); [City repository PDF](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/34637/1/Delegation_of_Learning.pdf)
- Tan Gan, "Robust delegation" (TSE seminar, September 2025; working paper): the principal has a prior over states and evaluates a delegation set by its worst expected payoff over all possible agent preferences; the optimal delegation set is always convex, so interval delegation is optimal in one dimension. — [TSE seminar page](https://www.tse-fr.eu/seminars/2025-robust-delegation)
- Auster and Pavoni (TE 2024): the principal is unaware of some feasible actions and cannot contract on them; the agent finds it optimal to keep her unaware. — [Theoretical Economics](https://www.econometricsociety.org/publications/theoretical-economics/2024/01/26/Optimal-delegation-and-information-transmission-under-limited-awareness)
- "Delegated Contracting", arXiv 2508.19326 (econ.TH, August 2025): a principal contracts through an informed delegate and restricts the menus the delegate may offer; implements any outcome of a DSIC, ex-post-IR direct mechanism; no preference uncertainty. — [arXiv 2508.19326](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19326v1)
- arXiv 2209.13689: misaligned delegation can be optimal when the principal is ex ante biased toward one option and the agent must invest in costly information. — [arXiv 2209.13689](https://arxiv.org/html/2209.13689v2)
- Pre-2022 anchor: Boston Fed working paper (2018), "Optimal delegation under unknown bias": with unknown agent bias and no transfers, sufficiently concave principal utility makes a uniform cap optimal, otherwise screening. — [Boston Fed](https://www.bostonfed.org/-/media/Documents/Workingpapers/PDF/2018/rpa1801.pdf)
- "Dynamic Contract Design with Learning" (Operations Research, DOI 10.1287/opre.2024.0944) surfaced in search; from its title it concerns learning over the relationship, but I could not confirm whose uncertainty is being resolved. — [INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/opre.2024.0944)

### Inferences
- Every "learning" paper found places the unknown on the agent side (cost, technology, type, response) or on the state of the world. A principal whose reward vector r is itself unknown and revealed through outcomes would be a new axis in the survey's taxonomy; the closest formal hook is the survey's Figure 1 fourth cell ("informed party moves first, hidden action"), which Deng-Li 2026 begin to study from the agent's side, and the "contracts with inspections" thread where the principal buys information, though there the information is about the action rather than her own payoff.
- Robust delegation (Gan 2025) and Carroll-style maxmin are the natural templates if one wanted a principal who is uncertain about her own objective: replace uncertainty over the agent's preferences with uncertainty over the principal's.

### Gaps
- Three differently phrased searches ("principal uncertain own preferences", "principal unknown own utility reward learned over time", "principal does not know her preferences delegation") returned nothing on point. I report this as a gap in the literature as far as web search reveals, not as a certainty; Google Scholar and SSRN were not queried directly.
- The Operations Research "Dynamic Contract Design with Learning" paper was not opened.

## 8. Open problems stated explicitly in recent papers

### Takeaway
Both surveys end their chapters with named open problems; collected here verbatim or closely paraphrased, with the chapter they come from.

### Cited Findings
From Dütting, Feldman, Talgam-Cohen (FnT TCS 2024; text extracted from the arXiv PDF 2412.16384): — [arXiv 2412.16384](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16384)
- Sec. 3.1: strongly polynomial algorithm for the optimal contract (open).
- Sec. 4.3: tight worst-case approximation ratio of linear contracts for m=2 outcomes "still (partially) open".
- Sec. 4.4: "A central challenge in the economic literature on contracts" is formal justification of simple contracts.
- Sec. 5.2 (combinatorial actions): "explore the best-possible approximation guarantees that can be given for submodular, XOS, and subadditive success probabilities with value oracle access. Another direction is to explore the problem beyond binary outcome."
- Sec. 5.3 (multi-agent): "the gap between upper and lower bounds for gross-substitutes (GS) f ... whether the problem of computing an optimal contract admits a PTAS/FPTAS"; "contracts that approximately maximize welfare rather than revenue"; "contracts under budget constraints, with respect to both welfare and revenue maximization". Sec. 5.3.2: "whether the constant-factor approximation for submodular functions can be extended to XOS functions, with value and demand oracle access".
- Sec. 5.4 (combinatorial outcomes): "go beyond product distributions, and explore (succinct) distributions over outcomes that are correlated".
- Sec. 5.5 (multiple principals): "explore approximately welfare-optimal contracts"; formalise the connection between contractible contracts and smart contracts.
- Sec. 6 (private types): complexity with a general number of actions and constantly many types is open; large gaps between upper and lower bounds in class-vs-benchmark comparisons; extension to multiple typed agents.
- Sec. 7 (data-driven): "whether there are other 'simple' contracts that can be learned efficiently" with higher utility than linear; polynomial regret when actions are polynomial in m; regret when the agent is resampled each round; weaker regularity than FOSD+CDFP; "a more comprehensive theory of the offline sample complexity of learning contracts, with different forms of feedback"; the gap between the Ho et al. and Zhu et al. frameworks.
- Sec. 8 (incentive-aware ML): "a combination of objectives for incentive-aware evaluation" (accuracy plus incentives plus self-improvement).
- Sec. 9 (ambiguous contracts): bounded-size ambiguous contracts beyond known-optimal settings; ambiguous contracts with multiple agents; "whether mixed strategies still eliminate the power of ambiguity"; vague and incomplete contracts "remain mostly unexplored from an algorithmic perspective".
- Sec. 10 (social good): other sources of agent heterogeneity (discount rate, risk preference); contracts to foster cooperation among human/AI agents in social dilemmas.
- Sec. 12: dynamic contracting (temporal, naturally combinatorial), learning perspectives on dynamic contracting, MDP-based delegation to AI agents, and contracts with inspections as directions "not covered in previous sections"; the authors "believe that current research has only scratched the surface".

From Feldman (arXiv 2510.15065, Oct 2025): — [arXiv HTML 2510.15065v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15065v1)
- Sec. 3.10: "A complete characterization of the tractable frontier, however, is still lacking" (single-agent combinatorial actions), with partial progress in Baldwin et al. (2025); tight approximation for submodular/XOS/subadditive under value oracles; beyond binary outcomes.
- Sec. 4.6 (multi-agent binary): PTAS/FPTAS for GS (and OXS); the subadditive gap between Omega(sqrt n) and O(n); beyond binary outcomes.
- Sec. 5.5 (multi-agent multi-action): "A central open question is whether the positive results for submodular rewards extend to XOS"; PTAS/FPTAS for GS even with binary actions; restricted domains (few actions: submodular with value queries only; few agents: "With a constant number of agents, can we achieve exact optimization for GS rewards", PTAS for submodular, constant factor for subadditive with demand oracles).
- Sec. 6: "A key limitation of the current literature is its near-exclusive focus on binary outcomes"; combinatorial outcome spaces; ambiguous contracts; effectiveness of simple contracts; Bayesian settings with private information; learning under partial information; inspection and monitoring; and AI agents "operating over vast, evolving action spaces" needing new models.

From individual papers:
- Baldwin, Dütting, Feldman, Schlesinger (arXiv 2604.15125, Apr 2026): conjecture that ASC is the largest class of reward functions with polynomially many critical values. — [arXiv 2604.15125](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15125)
- Bacchiocchi et al. (ICLR 2024) explicitly answer the open question of Zhu et al. on constant action spaces. — [ICLR 2024](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/3322a9a72a1707de14badd5e552ff466-Abstract-Conference.html)

### Inferences
- The open problems cluster into four: (a) tightness of approximation (XOS, subadditive, GS PTAS), (b) beyond binary outcomes in combinatorial settings, (c) learning with fewer assumptions and offline sample complexity, (d) ambiguity and dynamics. The uninformed-principal question of Section 6 above appears in none of them.

### Gaps
- Open-problem statements from the individual 2025-2026 preprints (equal-pay, anonymous, budget-feasible, online multi-agent) were not extracted, since those papers were not opened.
