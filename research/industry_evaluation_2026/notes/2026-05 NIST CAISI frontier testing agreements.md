# CAISI frontier AI testing agreements (May 2026)

## One line
On 5 May 2026 CAISI added Google DeepMind, Microsoft and xAI to its voluntary pre-deployment testing programme. With OpenAI and Anthropic it now covers five frontier developers.

## What they do
- The agreements allow:
  - government evaluation of models before public release;
  - assessment after deployment;
  - targeted research.
- Developers may provide models with safeguards reduced or removed for national-security capability testing ([HPCwire reprint](https://www.hpcwire.com/off-the-wire/nists-caisi-announces-new-frontier-ai-testing-agreements-with-google-deepmind-microsoft-xai/)).
- The agreements build on the August 2024 agreements with OpenAI and Anthropic, which were renegotiated to reflect the Trump administration's priorities ([CIO](https://www.cio.com/article/4168122/us-government-agency-to-safety-test-frontier-ai-models-before-release.html)).
- The focus is national security: cyber, chem-bio, and capability comparison with Chinese models.

## Evidence
- CAISI says it has completed more than 40 evaluations, some of state-of-the-art models that remain unreleased.
- Published evaluations include DeepSeek, GLM-5.2, GLM-5.3 and Kimi K3 (jointly with UK AISI).
- **NIST AI 800-2** draft, "Practices for Automated Benchmark Evaluations of Language Models": voluntary guidance for developers, deployers and third-party evaluators ([NIST](https://www.nist.gov/node/1905466); [ExecutiveGov](https://executivegov.com/articles/nist-public-comment-ai-benchmark-test)).
  - Comments closed 31 Mar 2026.
  - It is structured in three stages: objectives and benchmark choice, running evaluations, and analysis and reporting.
  - Princeton's comment argued that reliability should be a primary objective ([Princeton comment](https://sage.cs.princeton.edu/documents/NIST_benchmark_reliability.pdf)).
- **NIST AI 800-3**: a formal framework for interpreting benchmark results and their uncertainty.
- No final version of AI 800-2 was found.

## Limitations and criticisms
- The agreements are voluntary, and CAISI cannot block a release.
- The terms were not published beyond press summaries.
- A CIO report links the agreements to a possible White House plan for its own AI vetting system.

## Relevance to agent evaluation
Government pre-deployment testing in the US is now a standing arrangement with all five US frontier labs, and it includes access to models with safeguards removed. NIST AI 800-2 is the first US government guidance on how to run benchmark evaluations of agent systems: validity, reproducibility and reporting.
