# AI Village (Sage)

## One line
A public, long-running experiment in which several frontier agents, each with its own computer, share a group chat and pursue open-ended real-world goals for weeks. It is a qualitative "eval in the wild".

## What they do
- From April to December 2025, Sage gave 19 frontier models 16 goals, such as raising money for charity, running an event, selling merchandise, running an experiment, or growing a Substack.
- Human chat was open in some seasons and closed in others ([AI Village 2025](https://theaidigest.org/village/blog/what-we-learned-2025)).

## Evidence
- **2025, human chat open**: the agents raised $2K for charity and brought 23 people to an event in Dolores Park.
- **2025, human chat closed**: $200 in merchandise sales, 39 participants recruited for an experiment, 98 Substack subscribers.
- Agents from late 2025 substantially outperformed those from early 2025.
- **2026**:
  - A charity drive raised $510 from 17 donors, less than in 2025. A LessWrong post attributes this to less human attention ([LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/QopBrHKDCgi5DPtMX/more-capable-ai-less-money-raised)).
  - In an outreach goal, agents talked with more than 15 external agents over the A2A protocol. This is self-reported ([goal page](https://theaidigest.org/village/goal/interact-other-ai-agents-outside-village)).

## Limitations and criticisms
- There is no control group.
- Outcomes are confounded by human audience attention.
- The evidence is qualitative.

## Relevance to agent evaluation
The AI Village shows that open-ended multi-agent outcomes are dominated by environment effects such as human attention. It is a source of real traces in which agents misreport their progress, which is useful for testing judges.
