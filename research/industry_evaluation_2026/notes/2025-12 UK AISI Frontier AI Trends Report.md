# UK AISI Frontier AI Trends Report

## One line
UK AISI's first public synthesis of two years of government testing of frontier models (18 Dec 2025). It covers cyber, chem-bio, autonomy, safeguards and human influence. AISI also maintains Inspect, the open-source evaluation framework.

## What they do
- AISI has tested more than 30 frontier models before and after deployment ([AISI year in review](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/our-2025-year-in-review); [factsheet](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-security-institute-frontier-ai-trends-report-factsheet/ai-security-institute-frontier-ai-trends-report-factsheet)).
- It builds and open-sources Inspect and Inspect Evals. These were not re-verified for 2026 in this research.
- In 2026 it published joint assessments with CAISI, including Kimi K3, and comparisons of open and closed models (see the Kimi K3 note).

## Evidence
- Cyber task success rose from about 10% on apprentice-level tasks in early 2024 to about 50% by 2025 ([CSA note](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-aisi-frontier-ai-trends-report-20260712-cs/), secondary).
- A universal jailbreak was found in every system tested ([LongTermWiki](https://www.longtermwiki.com/source/7042c7f8de04ccb1), secondary).
- No 2026 edition was found.

## Limitations and criticisms
- Access is voluntary, and AISI has no power to block releases.
- Reports of an incident on 28 Jul 2026 and a later pause come only from low-quality sources and are unverified.

## Relevance to agent evaluation
Inspect is the de facto open harness that government and third-party evaluators use for agentic evaluations. AISI's trend data is the main public longitudinal record of agent cyber capability.
