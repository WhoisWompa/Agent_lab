# Adaptive and statistical stop rules for LLM agent loops (2025 to October 2026)

Scope note. The project's prior survey (`research/multiagent_llm_systems/report.md`) established that harness code in ~150 built systems caps rounds and otherwise trusts the agent's own "done" (MAST: 18.6% of failures from termination errors), and that "stop rules have never been compared." These notes go past that: every rule below is a 2025-2026 proposal that computes the stop decision in the harness from a statistic, and the notes record what it stops on, what it assumes, the measured numbers, the baseline, the venue, and what the authors admit is untested. Every paper below is an arXiv preprint unless a venue is named beside it. Where a page was truncated and a number could not be read, that is stated rather than filled in.

Terminology used throughout: "harness-computed" means the loop controller computes the stop statistic from observable signals (judge scores, votes, embeddings, hidden states, replayable traces); "agent-declared" means the stop fires because the generator or critic emits a completion token or verdict.

---

## KQ1. Which stop rules have been proposed since 2025, and on what signal do they stop (harness-computed vs agent-declared)?

### Takeaway
At least twelve 2025-2026 preprints replace "fixed N rounds" or "agent says done" with a harness-computed stop statistic. They fall into five families: (i) sequential or stability tests on a judge signal (Wald-SPRT over a consensus score; KS test over judge-vote distributions); (ii) conformal or calibrated risk gates on the agents' own probability outputs (first-singleton conformal set; kNN lower confidence bound; per-turn conformal thresholds); (iii) noise-model stopping that estimates verifier and repairer error rates and stops on the sign of expected marginal gain (VRR-Stop) or a rate-ratio threshold (ECR/EIR); (iv) cheap drift or outcome predictors (embedding plateau; hidden-state failure probes; LightGBM outcome classifiers; a learned early-exit operator in MaAS); and (v) certificate verification at the COMPLETE boundary, where the agent still proposes "done" but a deterministic verifier must accept it. Only two of these carry a stated venue: MaAS (ICML 2025, by keyword on the page) and the probabilistic self-correction theory (EMNLP 2025 Main). Everything else is under review or unreviewed.

### Cited Findings

**Family (i): sequential and stability tests on a judge signal**

- **Sequential Consensus (Wald-SPRT compute governor)**, Andrea Morandi, arXiv 2605.19193, submitted 18 May 2026, v1, cs.LG, no comments line, no venue; preprint. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19193)
  - *Stops on (harness-computed):* after each debate round an LLM judge emits a convergence score s_r in [0,1]; the orchestrator ("Wald monitor") clips to [ε, 1−ε] and accumulates Λ_R = Σ log(f1(s_r)/f0(s_r)) with f0 = Beta(α0,β0) under H0 "not converged" and f1 = Beta(α1,β1) under H1 "converged". Stop with "consensus" when Λ_R ≥ A = log((1−β)/α) ≈ 2.94 at α=β=0.05; stop with "no-consensus" when Λ_R ≤ B ≈ −2.94; otherwise continue to R_max (8 in experiments); hitting the cap is flagged "capped", not treated as a decision. The agents never compute the statistic. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.19193v1)
  - *Assumes:* i.i.d. Beta-distributed judge scores under each hypothesis; static likelihoods fitted offline by method of moments on 50-100 in-domain debates (the real-LLM runs used n=40 per task, correctness-conditioned: f0 from debates whose capped answer was wrong, f1 from those that were right). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.19193v1)
  - *Monte Carlo (N=50,000 trajectories per hypothesis, R_max=8):* average rounds 2.38 (MMLU-like), 2.61 (GSM8K-like), 2.49 (JudgeBench-like) vs 5.0 for fixed-5; correct-hypothesis rate 98.1-98.3%; cap rate 0.4-0.7%; false-consensus ≈1.6% on MMLU-like. Difficulty tertiles: easy (KL≈3.8) 1.54 rounds / 0% cap; hard (KL≈0.70) 4.68 rounds / 14.2% cap. Baselines in simulation: threshold-on-score 6.79 rounds at 79.5% accuracy; threshold-on-stability 5.14 rounds at 50.1%. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.19193v1)
  - *Real LLM (200 items per benchmark; agents gpt-5, claude-opus-4-6, gemini-2.5-pro at T=0.7; judge claude-opus-4-6 at T=0):* GSM8K: SPRT 97.0% accuracy at 1.01 rounds / 4.06 LLM calls vs fixed-5 debate 99.0% at 15 calls vs single-round vote 99.0% at 3 calls; ~98% of items stopped after one round; cap 0.5%, no-consensus 1.5%. MMLU: SPRT hit the cap on 99.5% of items, 7.99 rounds / 31.96 calls, 94.2% vs fixed-5 93.8% (15 calls) vs single vote 94.4% (3 calls), i.e. 2.1× the cost of fixed-5 for no gain. Author's own caveat: 95% CI on accuracy differences ≈ ±4.4 points, none significant; the GSM8K drop is ~1.4 standard errors. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.19193v1)
  - *Why MMLU capped:* fitted f0 mean 0.95, f1 mean 0.93, KL(f1‖f0) ≈ 0: the judge gives ~0.95 convergence whether the converged answer is right or wrong, so the LLR has no drift and random-walks to the cap. Author's conclusion: "judge design matters more than stopping-rule design here." — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.19193v1)
  - *Admits untested:* adversarial agents (a colluding agent could manufacture apparent consensus); judge-agent overlap (claude-opus-4-6 was both); K=3 agents only; no real-LLM run on JudgeBench or long-form; non-Beta judge scores (MMLU calibration "falls well outside the robustness bounds"); not run in production. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.19193v1)

- **Multi-Agent Debate for LLM Judges with Adaptive Stability Detection**, Hu, Tan, Wang, Qu, Chen, arXiv 2510.12697, 14 Oct 2025, v1, cs.AI; preprint, no venue stated. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.12697v1)
  - *Stops on (harness-computed):* each round, the distribution of the 7 judges' decisions is fitted with a two-component Beta-Binomial mixture by EM; the Kolmogorov-Smirnov distance D_t between consecutive rounds' fitted CDFs is computed; debate halts when D_t < 0.05 for 2 consecutive rounds; max 10 rounds. The fetched text describes the fit over "judges' correct-decision counts"; whether the deployed rule needs labels or models them as latent is not clear from the visible text and should be checked in the PDF.
  - *Results:* debate vs single judge, Gemini-2.0-Flash: LLMBar 76.68 → 81.83, JudgeBench 63.66 → 68.06. Not uniform: Qwen-2.5-7B on BIG-Bench 74.37 single / 76.60 majority vote / 72.20 debate; Llama-3.1-8B 78.67 / 81.80 / 74.00. Adaptive stop vs full 10 rounds: Gemini-2.0-Flash BIG-Bench stopped at 4 rounds for −0.60 points; Gemma-3-4B JudgeAnything 2 rounds for 0.00; Gemini-2.0-Flash JudgeAnything 8 rounds for 0.00. Initial KS values ≈0.25-0.45 at round 1 on JudgeBench; one run dipped below threshold at round 3, rebounded, and stabilised from round 6 (so the two-consecutive-rounds rule is doing work). No token or dollar cost reported in visible text.
  - *Assumes / admits:* single-concept assumption weakens under ambiguity; conditional independence of agents may fail if they copy each other; majority vote is comparable or better on simpler tasks.

- Precursor, not multi-agent: **ConSol** (Lee et al., arXiv 2503.17587, 22 Mar 2025, preprint) applies SPRT to self-consistency sampling to stop drawing samples once the answer mode is detected; the abstract reports "comparable" accuracy and "significant" token savings without figures on the abstract page. It is the SPRT-on-votes idea that Sequential Consensus moves to debate rounds. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17587)

**Family (ii): conformal and calibrated-risk gates on agents' probability outputs**

- **From Debate to Decision: Conformal Social Choice**, Wang et al. (12 authors), arXiv 2604.07667, v1 9 Apr 2026, v2 20 Oct 2026, cs.AI/cs.MA/cs.SI; preprint, no venue. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07667)
  - *Stops on (harness-computed):* agents emit verbalised probability distributions over the closed answer set; a linear opinion pool averages them; split conformal prediction with nonconformity 1 − P_social(y|x) builds a set with coverage ≥ 1−α at each pre-specified round. Policy: singleton → act autonomously; larger set → escalate to human with the pruned candidates; empty → full manual review. "First-singleton" adaptive stopping halts at the first round whose set is a singleton. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07667v2)
  - *Assumes:* exchangeability between calibration and test; 50/50 split per domain; α ∈ {0.05, 0.10}; 3 agents (Claude Haiku 4.5, DeepSeek-R1, Qwen-3 32B via Bedrock), T=4 rounds, 12 LLM calls per question.
  - *Measured:* fixed-round coverage 93.0-97.6% at α=0.05 and 87.6-93.2% at α=0.10; over 20 splits mean coverage within 1.3 points of target. Of 586 final-round wrong-consensus cases (14.1% of 4,158), 81.9% have non-singleton round-3 sets at α=0.05; the price is 31.9% over-rejection of correct consensus. Baselines at the same operating point: fixed top-1 threshold τ=0.95 intercepts 79.4% at 26.8% over-rejection; calibration-tuned top-1 79.7% / 33.5%; calibration-tuned entropy 80.0% / 35.1%. Authors: scalar rules reach "comparable interception"; the conformal layer's distinct value is the finite-sample guarantee and explicit candidate sets. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07667v2)
  - *First-singleton stopping:* >98% of Math items reach a singleton early vs 6.2% of Law; conformal stopping escalates 93.8% of Law and ~47% of Engineering while consensus stopping escalates 0%; mean first-singleton round 1.29-2.24 vs consensus 1.36-1.81; accuracy on acted-on cases 90.0-96.8% (conformal) vs 67.9-94.9% (consensus), Law gap +22.1 points (90.0 vs 67.9). Authors attribute the gain to "a selection effect, not a reasoning improvement." Coverage after adaptive stopping is explicitly not guaranteed ("Coverage is not guaranteed after adaptive stopping") and the paper does not report empirical coverage under the adaptive rule. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07667v2)
  - *Second ensemble (Appendix L.1, Haiku 4.5 + Nova Pro + DeepSeek-R1, 5 rounds):* intercepts 77.4% of 424 wrong-consensus cases (vs 81.9%); Law 94.6%, Engineering 92.6%, Math only 16.2%; Physics undercovers at 90.5-92.6% for a 95% target. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07667v2)
  - *Admits untested:* conditional/subgroup coverage; anytime-valid sequential methods for adaptive stopping; distribution shift; open-ended generation; verbalised probabilities are noisy (0.77% parse failures default to uniform).

- **Budgeted Act-or-Defer Multi-Agent LLM Deliberation with Local Reliability Bounds**, Wang, Xie, Wang, Zhang, Woo (same group), arXiv 2606.29654, 28 Jun 2026, v1, cs.AI/cs.MA; preprint, no venue. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29654)
  - *Stops on (harness-computed):* each round, the debate prefix is mapped to a 2-D state U_t = (top social probability, margin between top two); a k-nearest-neighbour lower confidence bound on correctness given that state is computed from a calibration database (k ∈ {128, 256, 512}); act when the LCB exceeds the user's reliability threshold, otherwise defer. Wrong-action budget decomposes as β = δ + α + ε_act (calibration failure, residual risk, representation gap); δ=0.03, ε_act=0.02. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29654v1)
  - *Measured (Table 1, 10 random 50/50 splits; 3 agents as above, T=4):* LogiQA n=8,678: act 27.9%, acted-on accuracy 88.9%, wrong-action 3.2%, WA/β 0.10, 3.5 rounds. MMLU-Pro n=8,312: 71.1% / 93.9% / 4.4% / 0.12 / 2.2 rounds. ARC-Challenge n=2,590: 75.5% / 97.1% / 2.3% / 0.11 / 2.3. BBH n=2,395: 84.4% / 96.4% / 3.0% / 0.09 / 1.8. Stress sets: MuSR n=756 act 9.0% at 70.3%; GPQA n=546 act 53.3% at 82.5%, WA 9.4%. Nine baselines: CRC (conformal risk control), Selective Prediction, calibrated gradient-boosted classifier, isotonic confidence; two ablations (kNN without bias envelope; final-round-only, which is the fixed-round variant); heuristics: consensus, confidence threshold p ≥ 0.90, logistic-regression learned stopper. At near-full automation the baselines consume 18-82% of the declared budget vs 9-12% for the method. When baseline thresholds are tuned post hoc to match automation, accuracy differences are within 1.6 pp; the authors present this as a diagnostic, not a superiority claim. No entropy-threshold or SPRT baseline. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29654v1)
  - *Admits untested:* the guarantee is conditional on a valid local bias envelope and a representation-gap bound, "not distribution-free"; the diagnostics are "necessary but not sufficient"; ε_act is not shown to be small; open-ended generation is future work.

- **MiCP: Adaptive Stopping for Multi-Turn LLM Reasoning**, Zhou, Nguyen, Yu, Liu, Cheng, arXiv 2604.01413, v3 14 Aug 2026, cs.CL; preprint, no venue. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.01413v3)
  - *Stops on (harness-computed):* at each turn t, a turn-specific conformal threshold q̂_t is the quantile over calibration questions still active at t whose gold answer has not yet appeared; per-turn error budgets α_t satisfy Σ_t (1 − c_ans^t) α_t ≤ (1 − c_ans^final) α, found by grid search (G=20) on a separate optimisation set; a "Can't Answer" label is appended only if all T turns are exhausted. T=3, M=15 samples per turn at temperature 1.4.
  - *Measured:* 900 examples per dataset split 300 optimisation / 300 calibration / 300 test, 3 seeds. Average turns vs fixed 3.000: Qwen3.5-9B HotpotQA 2.562, NQ 2.621, TriviaQA 2.528, MuSiQue 2.705, 2WikiMHQA 1.778; GPT-4o-mini HotpotQA 2.497, NQ 3.000 (no saving). Set size on HotpotQA 11.45 → 10.75. Coverage at or above 1−α on nearly all cells. No accuracy metric is reported.
  - *Contested:* a public issue argues the finite-sample guarantee must account for estimation error in the per-turn fractions, and that the active set at turn t is adaptively selected using earlier thresholds, so a plug-in weighted union bound "is not automatically valid." — [GitHub issue, microprediction/metareasoning #19](https://github.com/microprediction/metareasoning/issues/19)
  - *Admits:* marginal not conditional coverage; Gemma-2-9B-IT cannot separate answerable from unanswerable, so gains are smaller; only up to 3 turns.

**Family (iii): noise-model stopping on estimated verifier and repairer error rates**

- **VRR-Stop: Verify, Repair, Repeat, or Stop?**, Wu, Shen, Yang, Peng, Hu, arXiv 2607.17641, 20 Jul 2026, v1, cs.AI/cs.SE, "18 pages, 10 figures, 10 tables. Under review"; preprint. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17641)
  - *Stops on (harness-computed):* a belief b_k that the current plan is valid, updated from the count S_k of accepts out of M=8 repeated verifier votes under a four-parameter noise model (ρ0 verifier false-accept, ρ1 verifier false-reject, α repair fix rate, β repair damage rate); expected marginal gain G_k = (1 − b_k)α − b_kβ; repair if G_k > τ (τ=0), else commit. Stopping boundary b* = α/(α+β). Fallback VRR-Guard (estimation-free): replace the incumbent only when S(p_k) ≥ S(c) + δ, δ=5 with M=8; the committed plan is the incumbent, not the last plan. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.17641v1)
  - *Headline (GSM8K prompt-mismatch stress, Qwen2.5-3B, N=500):* no repair 0.700 [.658,.740]; majority stopping 0.690 at 0.92 rounds; ConfStop-0.85 0.562 at 1.92; fixed repair K=1 0.246, K=3 0.122, K=5 0.116 [.088,.144]; last-accepted 0.504; verifier-best-of-trajectory 0.696; **VRR-Stop 0.722 [.682,.760] at 0.72 rounds**. Paired: +60.6 pp [+56.0, +65.0] vs fixed-5 (McNemar p < 2e-85); +16.0 pp vs ConfStop; +3.2 pp vs majority stopping (p < 2e-4); +2.2 pp [−0.4, +4.8] vs no repair, not significant. Reflexion and Self-Refine replayed in fixed-budget mode on the same trajectories: 0.095 and 0.080 vs VRR-Stop 0.740 / 0.710 (the authors call this an audit of the deployment mode, not a reproduction). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.17641v1)
  - *Breadth (Table 9, true validity):* Qwen-3B favourable (J=0.39): no repair 0.740, fixed-5 0.875, VRR-Stop 0.845, Guard 0.810 (stopping gives up gains when repair helps). Qwen-7B stress (J=0.23): 0.875 / 0.075 / 0.875 / 0.875. Mistral-7B stress (J=0.18): 0.507 / 0.047 / 0.463 / 0.487. Llama-3-8B stress (J=0.03): 0.803 / 0.057 / **0.223** / 0.793. MATH-500 with PRM (J=0.22): 0.798 / 0.150 / 0.798 / 0.796. BFCL single-turn (J=0.07): 0.812 / 0.372 / 0.782 / 0.810. Tasks also include MBPP (150, sandboxed tests) and BFCL multi-turn (official executor). Ground truth: exact match, symbolic check, unit tests with 55 s timeout, AST + executor. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.17641v1)
  - *Admits untested:* binary validity state does not describe "partial correctness, multiple error types, or long-horizon plans with staged goals"; stationarity is "a local approximation"; conditional independence of repeated votes assumed; the one-step objective ignores multi-round look-ahead; M and τ not swept; parameters must not be transferred across model family, verifier or repair prompt; online switching between stop and guard modes is future work; only three model families; the TPM reference is not a global optimum. — [arXiv HTML tail](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.17641v1)

- **Self-Correction as Feedback Control**, Liu (Peking) and Meng (Chicago), arXiv 2604.22273, v2 4 May 2026, cs.AI; preprint, no venue. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.22273v2)
  - *Stops on:* defines EIR = P(incorrect at k+1 | correct at k) and ECR = P(correct at k+1 | incorrect at k); Theorem 1 gives the break-even, so iterate only when ECR/EIR > Acc/(1−Acc). Adaptive Self-Correction (ASC) stops on instance-level verbalised confidence ≥ 8/10 (agent-declared) or when the running EIR estimate exceeds the ECR estimate (harness-computed, but needs labels).
  - *Measured (GSM8K, 500 problems, iterations 0-4, intrinsic self-correction):* GPT-4o-mini 91.2 → 90.0 → 89.6 → 86.6 → 85.0 (−6.2 pp; EIR rises 1.3% → 3.8%); GPT-4.1 94.6 → 94.4 (−0.2); Claude Sonnet 4 96.8 → 95.6 (−1.2); GPT-5 96.2 → 94.4 (−1.8); Claude Opus 4.6 97.6 → 98.2 (+0.6); o3-mini 93.2 → 96.6 (+3.4). Models with EIR ≲ 0.5% (o3-mini, Opus 4.6, o4-mini) do not degrade. MATH cross-check for GPT-4o-mini 72.8% → 68.5%. Verify-first prompt on GPT-4o-mini cuts EIR 2% → 0% and turns −6.2 pp into +0.2 pp (McNemar p < 1e-4). ASC's confidence prompt halted every GPT-4o-mini problem at iteration 0 but cost −3.8 pp (91.2 → 87.4). Compute-matched: self-consistency with 3 samples 93.4% vs 3 refinement iterations 86.6%.
  - *Admits untested:* no external feedback or verifier; GSM8K only for the full trajectory analysis; stationary Markov rates assumed but non-stationarity observed; verify-first causally tested on one model; ASC on one model; open-ended generation.

**Family (iv): cheap drift, outcome and failure predictors**

- **Semantic Early-Stopping for Iterative LLM Agent Loops**, Sahil Shrivastava, affiliation "Semantic Halting Problem (SHP) Project" (no institution), arXiv 2606.27009, 25 Jun 2026, v1, cs.AI; preprint. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.27009v1)
  - *Stops on (harness-computed, free):* d_t = 1 − cos(e_t, e_{t−1}) over 384-d frozen sentence embeddings of consecutive drafts; halt when d_t < 0.06 for k=2 consecutive rounds ("entropy" halt). Cascade order: Critic "APPROVED" (agent-declared) → entropy → no_gain on a RAGAS Information Score → hard failsafe at max rounds. Termination proof rests on the failsafe; the earlier Banach-contraction claim is withdrawn.
  - *Measured (HotpotQA hard multi-hop, N=60 test, llama-3.1-8b-instruct as Writer, Critic and judge):* vs fixed_k6 (6.0 rounds, IS 0.670): entropy_only 3.92 rounds, −38% operational tokens, ΔIS −0.004 (p=0.81); full cascade 2.40 rounds but +129% tokens because per-round judging dominates; fixed_k3 3.0 rounds, −53%, +0.001; fixed_k1 1.0 round, −86%, +0.030 (p=0.17, passes TOST non-inferiority); oracle best-round +0.115 (p≈3e-11). Strict non-inferiority of the entropy rule is not certified. Only ~5% of trajectories have strictly monotone distance. Iteration "slightly hurts" on average here (fixed_k1 beats fixed_k6).
  - *Admits:* single dataset that under-tests iteration; noisy 8B judge; same model as writer, critic and judge.

- **Doomed from the Start: Early Abort via a Recall-Controlled Probe Cascade**, Ruan et al., arXiv 2607.06503, v1 7 Jul 2026, v2 16 Jul 2026, cs.AI; preprint. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06503)
  - *Aborts on (harness-computed, white-box):* linear probes on the policy model's hidden states at early rounds predict eventual failure; per-round gates are distribution-free calibrated so that eventually-successful episodes pass all gates at a user-set global recall; the cascade is frozen then checked on independent data for an exact post-selection recall guarantee. Adding behavioural features gives no further gain.
  - *Measured:* at 90% recall, token reduction 60.2% (TextCraft) and 54.9% (WebShop); at 95% recall 45.0% and 41.5%; 1.5-8.8× more compute saved than the best single-gate baseline; achieved recall within one SD of target in all 24 configurations. Models Qwen-2.5-7B, Llama-3.2-3B, Qwen3-1.7B. Fraction of doomed episodes aborted and at which step is not on the abstract page; no limitations section visible; code "will be released soon."

- **EarlyEval**, Shi et al., arXiv 2609.02783, 2 Sep 2026, cs.CL; preprint with code. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02783)
  - *Halts on (harness-computed):* two LightGBM classifiers (success, failure) over behavioural, textual and reference-solution features; the run is halted when either crosses a calibrated confidence threshold. Framed as an evaluation-cost tool, not a production stop (it needs the reference solution).
  - *Measured:* removes 13-26% of agent steps; saves up to 44.1% input and 29.4% output tokens; 89-97% outcome-prediction accuracy; per-agent resolve rates move 1-2 pp. SWE-bench Verified, TerminalBench, Toolathlon. Models not named on the page.

- **MaAS early-exit operator**, Zhang, Niu, Fang, Wang, Bai, Wang, arXiv 2502.04180, v2 9 Jun 2025, cs.LG; the page lists "ICML" as a keyword but does not state acceptance. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.04180v2)
  - *Exits on (learned controller, not agent-declared, not a test):* a MoE-style sampler scores each operator from the query embedding and operators already chosen in earlier layers, activating operators until a cumulative-score threshold of 0.3; O_exit is one operator; Algorithm 1 stops at layer L=4 or when O_exit is sampled. Trained with cost penalty λ ∈ {1e-3, 5e-3, 1e-2}.
  - *Measured:* MATH inference cost $0.42 vs LLM-Debate $6.76, DyLAN $2.89, MacNet $2.35, AFlow $1.66, GPTSwarm $0.93 (≈6-45% of baselines, matching the abstract's "6∼45%"); accuracy gains over the strongest listed baseline: GSM8K +1.14, MATH +0.54, MultiArith +1.03, HumanEval +1.92, MBPP +0.50, GAIA avg +4.08 (the fetched text gives the upper gain as 16.89%, not the 11.82% in the brief). L=2 → L=4 raises performance 89.5% → 92.8%. Exit frequency: only per-query examples (exit at layer 2 with probability 0.37 and 0.47 for easy queries); no aggregate trigger rate; GAIA Level 3 shows no improvement. No limitations section.

**Family (v): the agent still proposes "done", but a deterministic verifier must accept it**

- **When May an Agent Stop? Evidence-Carrying Termination (ECT)**, arXiv 2608.23623, 22 Aug 2026, v1, cs.SE/cs.AI/cs.LG (author listed as Jason Liu; submitter Jiacheng Liu); preprint. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23623)
  - *Stops on:* the agent may return COMPLETE only with a typed certificate binding every required claim to in-scope trace evidence, and a deterministic replay must reconstruct the claimed value. The certificate "does not certify external truth, safety, or alignment."
  - *Measured:* Study 1 (48 synthetic tasks × 6 tool families, clean + 8 injected faults = 288 cases): unsafe completions 0/288 vs termination-critic core 252/288 (−87.50 pp). Study 2 (576 prespecified frozen trajectories, 22 held-out clusters): premature unsupported terminations 0/66 vs faithful controller 40/66 (−60.61 pp, 95% [−78.79, −40.91]); supported completion 97/132 vs 92/132 (+3.79 pp, [0.00, 9.09], meets a −10 pp non-inferiority margin); recovery in 18/66 trajectories, 17 then completed with support.
  - *Admits:* all tasks fully synthetic; single frozen run; small recovery evidence.

**Evidence that agent-declared stopping is the failure mode being replaced**

- **LoopTrap: Termination Poisoning**, Xu et al., arXiv 2605.05846, 7 May 2026, cs.CR; preprint. Injected prompts distort the agent's own completion judgment so it keeps running: 10 attack strategies, 8 agents, 60 tasks; average 3.57× step amplification, peak 25×; even simple static prompts give 2.33× on GAIA; tasks with objectively verifiable completion (math) are more resilient, open-ended ones "substantially more vulnerable." — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05846); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.05846)
- **DeployBench**, Wang et al., arXiv 2606.05238, v2 30 Aug 2026; preprint. 51 research-artifact deployment tasks under OpenHands with a hidden verification pipeline; pass rates 7.8-51.0% across five models; of 181 failures, 102 are agent self-stops where "the agent's pre-finish checks validate a different or weaker target." — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05238)
- **If It's Not Buggy, Don't Fix It**, Wang-Lin, Isopoussu, Mahon, arXiv 2609.10123, 9 Sep 2026, cs.SE; preprint. LLMs "consistently claim to detect bugs" in bug-free programs, so "fix until the model finds no bug" never fires; runs capped at 100 turns reach fixed points only when the model emits "- None", an identity edit, an unfound search block or degenerate text. The authors propose no rule; they conclude the findings bear on stopping under ambiguous goals. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10123v1)
- **Free-MAD** (Cui et al., arXiv 2509.11035, 14 Sep 2025, preprint) sidesteps the stop problem by running a single debate round and scoring the whole trajectory instead of voting; no figures on the abstract page. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11035)
- Practitioner sources agree on the direction but are not evidence: a confidence-gated escalation middleware combines logprob, verbalised and tool-risk scores with threshold policies for LangChain/CrewAI/AutoGen/ADK — [GitHub](https://github.com/ashutoshrana/confidence-escalation); a course lesson titled "Termination: On the Verifier, Never on Confidence" recommends a deterministic verifier plus hard cap plus oscillation detection — [Tesseract Academy](https://tesseract.academy/courses/agents-are-just-loops-generate-verify-repair/lessons/termination-on-the-verifier-never-on-confidence/). I found no paper that names or measures "confidence-gated escalation" as such.

### Inferences
- Every principled rule found moves the stop decision out of the generator. Where the generator still proposes "done" (ECT, the Critic "APPROVED" halt in Semantic Early-Stopping, ASC's confidence gate), the papers either wrap it in a deterministic check (ECT: 0/66 premature stops vs 40/66) or report that trusting it costs accuracy (ASC confidence prompt −3.8 pp; LoopTrap 3.57× step amplification).
- The two rules that work on the judge's or agents' own scores (SPRT, conformal) are only as good as the separation in those scores: SPRT capped 99.5% of MMLU items because judge means were 0.95 vs 0.93; conformal intercepted only 16.2% of wrong consensus on Math in the second ensemble because agents are confidently wrong there. Both papers say so.
- Rules that need ground-truth labels for calibration (SPRT's correctness-conditioned Beta fit, VRR-Stop's α/β, ECR/EIR, kNN LCB, MiCP, Doomed probes) are not "label-free stopping"; they move the label cost to an offline calibration set of 40 to a few hundred items, and every one of them says the parameters do not transfer across model, verifier or task.
- The cheapest rules (embedding plateau, MaAS exit) have the weakest evidence: N=60 with a non-certified non-inferiority, and no aggregate exit rate at all.

### Gaps
- I found no 2025-2026 paper that proposes a principled stop rule for orchestrator-worker or role-based (ChatDev/MetaGPT-style) teams; the search for round-cap sensitivity in ChatDev/MetaGPT returned only role and phase ablations from the original papers, nothing varying the round cap. — [ChatDev, ACL 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.810.pdf)
- No OpenReview record for any of the stop-rule preprints surfaced in search; ICLR 2026 debate papers found ("Multi-Agent Debate with Memory Masking", DREAM) are not stop-rule papers. — [ICLR 2026 poster](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10010659)
- MaAS acceptance venue is inferred from a page keyword only.
- The Stability Detection paper's dependence on labels at inference time could not be resolved from the visible text.

## KQ2. Where a rule estimates verifier or repairer error rates, how are those estimated and how sensitive is the rule to the estimate?

### Takeaway
Only VRR-Stop estimates all four rates and reports sensitivity in numbers; its finding is that the size of the estimation error matters far less than whether the error flips the sign of the marginal gain, which depends on verifier discrimination J = 1 − ρ0 − ρ1 and the decision margin: sign-flip risk 0.183 when J ≤ 0.15 and margin ≤ 0.10 vs 0.014 when J ≥ 0.4 or margin ≥ 0.30, about 13×. Sequential Consensus reports Beta miscalibration sensitivity (±25% shifts rounds by ±0.5 and accuracy by ≤0.005) but its real-LLM MMLU calibration fell outside those bounds. Two other papers measure repair and damage rates (α, β; ECR, EIR) externally without using them in a deployed rule.

### Cited Findings
- **VRR-Stop estimation procedure:** ρ0 (false accept) and ρ1 (false reject) by label-free binomial-mixture EM over repeated verifier votes (16 votes per plan in the calibration probe); α (fix rate) and β (damage rate) by frequency over ≤300 labelled before/after repair pairs; initial-validity prior from the calibration folds; five-fold cross-fitting so no instance's decision uses its own label; calibration is one-off and offline, no online re-estimation. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.17641v1)
- **Calibration sample size:** N_calib ∈ {50, 100, 200, 300}: decision agreement with the reference rises 0.973 → 0.997; sign-flip rate falls 0.016 → 0.002; validity gap to the TPM reference shrinks 0.012 → 0.001; 300 reported as sufficient. — [arXiv HTML tail](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.17641v1)
- **Estimator collapse at low J:** Llama-3-8B true ρ1 = 0.609; binomial EM gives ρ̂1 = 0.27 at N=120 and 0.077 at N=300 (worse with more data); a capped beta-binomial gives 0.620 at N=120 but is unstable across seeds; calibrated stopping recovers only when ρ̂1 ≳ 0.30. Result: VRR-Stop 0.223 vs TPM reference 0.803 (−58.0 pp [−63.7, −52.0], McNemar p < 2e-48). The paper's answer is the estimation-free VRR-Guard: 0.793 on Llama, +57.0 pp over failed VRR-Stop and +73.7 pp over fixed-5. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.17641v1)
- **Large bias, no harm:** Mistral-7B has the largest false-accept bias (Δρ0 = −0.66) yet J = 0.18 keeps the sign stable: 0.463 vs 0.467 TPM, within 0.3 pp. All eight Qwen configurations underestimate ρ0 (Δρ0 from −0.16 to −0.31) with no measurable loss. BFCL single-turn differs from TPM by 0.3 pp despite J = 0.07. Authors: "stopping reliability depends on whether finite evidence can stabilize the gain sign," not on absolute estimation error. A decision margin of ≈0.74 did not protect the Llama case; margin matters only together with J. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.17641v1)
- **Guard's own knobs:** retention margin δ=5 (of M=8) is most robust across seven settings, trailing the per-setting optimum by at most 4.0 pp; Lemma 1 bounds per-round erroneous replacement by exp(−(MJ+δ)²/(2M)), called conservative. Mode switch between Stop and Guard uses an offline held-out separation test with threshold J_min; not tuned online. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.17641v1)
- **Guard is conservative:** it does not dominate no-repair (−2.0 pp on Mistral, −0.3 pp on BFCL, CIs contain zero) and forfeits gains when repair helps (0.810 vs fixed-5 0.875 in the favourable setting). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.17641v1)
- **Verifier strength reverses the fixed-N conclusion:** with a PRM of J = 0.805 on GSM8K, fixed five-round repair drops 0.727 → 0.097; on MATH-500 (J = 0.77) 0.798 → 0.150 while the TPM reference holds 0.798, i.e. a strong verifier does not rescue a fixed round budget if the repairer damages. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.17641v1)
- **Sequential Consensus miscalibration:** ±10% perturbation of the Beta parameters shifts average rounds by ≤0.17 and accuracy by ≤0.003; ±25% shifts rounds ≈±0.5 and accuracy ≤0.005; a −25% under-scoring perturbation adds ≈0.48 rounds and costs ≈0.4 accuracy points; AR(1) correlation ρ=0.6 raises empirical (α̂, β̂) from (0.015, 0.017) to (0.065, 0.072). The real MMLU fit (f0 mean 0.95, f1 mean 0.93, KL ≈ 0) is outside this robustness range. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.19193v1)
- **Independent measurements of repairer damage rates (not used in a deployed rule):** "If It's Not Buggy" on CodeContests+ C++ (20 problems × 40 submissions ≈ 800, 100-turn cap) reports repair α vs damage β per transition: Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite search/replace τ=0: α 0.062, β 0.293; whole-file τ=0: 0.040 / 0.165; Qwen2.5-7B SRB τ=0: 0.041 / 0.424; whole-file τ=0.7: 0.008 / 0.016. Damage is "at least as large" as repair everywhere and 5-10× for search/replace edits. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10123v1)
- **Self-Correction as Feedback Control** estimates EIR and ECR per iteration from labels (GPT-4o-mini EIR 1.3% → 3.8% over four iterations) and gives the break-even ECR/EIR > Acc/(1−Acc); at Acc ≈ 0.9 the repairer must fix 9× as often as it damages. The paper observes its own stationarity assumption fails (EIR drifts upward). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.22273v2)
- **Budgeted Act-or-Defer** estimates calibration failure, residual risk and representation gap separately (β = δ + α + ε_act, δ=0.03, ε_act=0.02) and reports the actual wrong-action rate at 9-12% of the declared budget on activated sets, but ε_act is assumed, not estimated. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29654v1)

### Inferences
- Across VRR-Stop, Sequential Consensus and Conformal Social Choice the same variable decides whether the rule works: the verifier's or judge's discrimination between correct and incorrect states (J in VRR-Stop, KL(f1‖f0) in SPRT, agents' calibration on Math vs Law in conformal). All three papers end by saying the verifier or judge, not the stop rule, is the binding constraint.
- The practical recipe VRR-Stop gives (estimate noise, check whether the CI straddles the boundary, fall back to an estimation-free guard if it does, recalibrate on any shift) is the only deployment procedure in this literature that says what to do when the estimate is bad.
- The "If It's Not Buggy" α/β table is the same quantity VRR-Stop calibrates, measured on a code task where VRR-Stop was not tested; at α ≈ 0.04 and β ≈ 0.3 the VRR-Stop boundary b* = α/(α+β) ≈ 0.12 means repair should fire only when the harness is >88% sure the program is wrong.

### Gaps
- No paper reports sensitivity of a deployed rule to error in α and β separately from error in ρ0 and ρ1; VRR-Stop's sensitivity results are stated through J, the margin and sign flips.
- No online re-estimation of noise parameters has been tested anywhere; VRR-Stop names it as future work.
- Sequential Consensus gives no real-LLM miscalibration sweep; the ±10/25% numbers are simulation only.

## KQ3. Do any papers measure true validity (ground truth) separately from acceptance rate, and show the two diverging?

### Takeaway
Yes. VRR-Stop measures both and shows acceptance rising while true validity falls, at the population level (validity 0.45 → 0.87 at round 2 → 0.12 at round 6 under drift), at the instance level (a correct plan with 4/8 accepts rewritten into a wrong plan with 6/8), and in aggregate (55% of instances go correct → incorrect after one repair; 24% of those damaging repairs win majority acceptance). Conformal Social Choice shows the debate analogue: 14.1% of final-round consensus answers are wrong, and consensus stopping acts on Law cases at 67.9% accuracy. Sequential Consensus shows the judge analogue: the judge's convergence score is 0.95 for wrong converged answers and 0.93 for right ones. DeployBench shows the agent analogue: 102 of 181 failures are self-stops whose pre-finish checks passed.

### Cited Findings
- **VRR-Stop population identity:** acceptance rate Ā_t = ρ0 + J·Q_t where Q_t is true validity, so acceptance can rise while validity falls whenever J is low or parameters drift. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.17641v1)
- **VRR-Stop interior peak (Fig. 4, non-stationary diagnostic, prompt mismatch from round 3, 256-token start):** true validity 0.45 → 0.87 (round 2) → 0.12 (round 6); peak minus final +74.7 pp [+69.3, +79.7]; an independent-resampling control stays between 0.47 and 0.85, so the decline is from the loop, not sampling. The paper does not report the matching numeric acceptance curve; its Fig. 2 is illustrative. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.17641v1)
- **VRR-Stop single instance (GSM8K #292, Fig. 8):** valid plan receives 4/8 accepts, is rewritten into an invalid plan that receives 6/8 accepts; true validity 1 → 0 while acceptance rises. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.17641v1)
- **VRR-Stop stress traces (N=500):** round-1 validity 0.70 drops to 0.25 after one repair; 55% of instances go correct → incorrect; 24% of these damaging repairs win majority acceptance; "last-accepted" stopping yields 0.504 vs "accepted-first" 0.690 on the same trajectories, i.e. later acceptance is worse. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.17641v1)
- **Ground truth used:** exact match (GSM8K), symbolic verification (MATH-500), sandboxed unit tests with 55 s timeout (MBPP), AST matching plus the official executor (BFCL); all numbers from deterministic replay of frozen trajectories. — [arXiv HTML tail](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.17641v1)
- **Conformal Social Choice:** 586 of 4,158 final-round consensus answers (14.1%) are wrong; consensus stopping escalates 0% and its acted-on accuracy is 67.9% on Law vs 90.0% for conformal singletons; the conformal gain is a selection effect. Per-round debate+majority-vote accuracy (Table 5, rounds 0-3) rises in every domain, most of the gain in round 1: Engineering 67.6 / 77.9 / 79.6 / 80.0; Law 60.8 / 64.8 / 65.8 / 66.7; Chemistry 84.0 / 88.2 / 88.7 / 88.6 (dip at round 3); Math 92.2 / 93.9 / 94.0 / 94.2; Psychology 81.5 / 81.5 / 82.3 / 82.3. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07667v2)
- **Sequential Consensus:** correctness-conditioned judge-score fits on MMLU, f0 (wrong) mean 0.95 vs f1 (right) mean 0.93: the judge reports near-total convergence on wrong answers. On GSM8K the separation exists (0.38 vs 0.85). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.19193v1)
- **Self-Correction as Feedback Control:** acceptance is not measured, but the agent's own confidence gate (ASC, ≥8/10) halts every GPT-4o-mini problem at iteration 0 while costing −3.8 pp, i.e. the confidence prompt changes the answer it is supposed to gate. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.22273v2)
- **DeployBench:** 102 of 181 failures are agent self-stops where the pre-finish check "validate[s] a different or weaker target" than the hidden verifier; pass rates 7.8-51.0%. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05238)
- **If It's Not Buggy:** LLMs claim bugs in bug-free programs across both models; damage β up to 0.424 per transition vs repair α 0.041 (Qwen SRB τ=0); cycles alternate between correct and incorrect states (a length-2 cycle example). Figures 2-3 plot pass rate per turn to 100 turns but the text gives no per-round numbers. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10123v1)
- **Talk Isn't Always Cheap** (Wynn, Satija, Hadfield, arXiv 2509.05396, 5 Sep 2025, preprint; 100 items × 5 seeds, T=2 rounds): majority-vote accuracy before → after debate falls in 20 of 30 configuration-task cells, e.g. 3× Mistral MMLU 33.6 → 24.4 (−9.2), 1 LLaMA + 2 Mistral MMLU 40.0 → 28.0 (−12.0), 2 LLaMA + 1 Mistral GSM8K 82.6 → 75.8 (−6.8); correct → incorrect flips outnumber incorrect → correct "almost always" (figures only, no counts). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05396v1)
- **Multi-agent debate review** (arXiv 2506.00066, preprint) collects earlier reports that accuracy improves only to round 2 then declines, or peaks around four rounds; another 2025 paper (arXiv 2510.20963, ICML 2026 Workshop on Failure Modes of Agentic AI) states competitive-style debate "degrades with more rounds" while its collaborative variant is "robust to the number of debate rounds", with the per-round figure in an appendix not visible. — [review](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00066v1); [Chen et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.20963v2)

### Inferences
- The acceptance/validity divergence is now measured in three loop types with three different "acceptance" signals: verifier votes (VRR-Stop), agent consensus (Conformal Social Choice; Talk Isn't Always Cheap), judge convergence score (Sequential Consensus). In each the signal saturates while truth does not.
- The direction of per-round curves depends on whether the loop has external grounding: debate with majority vote on MMLU-Pro rises then plateaus by round 2-3 (Conformal Social Choice Table 5); intrinsic self-correction on GSM8K falls for 4 of 6 models (Feedback Control); verify-repair with a noisy same-model verifier collapses from round 1 (VRR-Stop fixed K=1 0.246 vs 0.700 no repair).

### Gaps
- No paper reports a numeric paired curve of acceptance rate and true validity per round on the same axis; VRR-Stop gives validity per round and acceptance only in an identity and one instance.
- No per-round true-validity numbers exist past round 6 in any paper found; "If It's Not Buggy" runs 100 turns but reports only figures and attractor types.
- No paper measures how often an agent's stated "done" is wrong against ground truth for a debate or generator-critic loop at the instance level; DeployBench does so only for single-agent OpenHands.

## KQ4. Is there any head-to-head comparison of stop rules across more than one harness or task family?

### Takeaway
No. Every comparison found is inside one harness type. The broadest is VRR-Stop (ten stop policies, five generators, five task families, but all in a verify-repair loop); Budgeted Act-or-Defer compares nine policies on six benchmarks, all inside the same 3-agent debate; Conformal Social Choice compares conformal, top-1 and entropy gates at one round; Semantic Early-Stopping compares seven policies on one dataset with N=60. No paper runs SPRT, conformal first-singleton, KS stability and VRR-Stop against each other, and none tests any statistical stop rule on a role-based team or an orchestrator-worker system.

### Cited Findings
- **VRR-Stop** compares, on identical frozen trajectories: no repair, majority stopping, ConfStop-0.85, fixed K ∈ {1,3,5}, accepted-first, last-accepted, verifier-best-of-trajectory, VRR-Stop, VRR-Guard, plus Reflexion and Self-Refine replayed in fixed-budget mode, across GSM8K, MATH-500, MBPP, BFCL single and multi-turn, with Qwen2.5-3B/7B, Mistral-7B, Llama-3-8B, Qwen2.5-Math-7B. All inside one verify-repair harness. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.17641v1)
- **Budgeted Act-or-Defer** compares CRC, selective prediction, calibrated GBM, isotonic confidence, consensus, confidence threshold 0.90, logistic learned stopper, and two ablations on LogiQA, MMLU-Pro, ARC-Challenge, BBH, MuSR, GPQA, all with the same three agents and T=4 debate; matched-automation accuracy differences ≤1.6 pp. No SPRT or entropy baseline. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29654v1)
- **Conformal Social Choice** compares conformal set vs fixed top-1 (τ=0.95), tuned top-1, tuned entropy at round 3 on MMLU-Pro: interception 81.9 / 79.4 / 79.7 / 80.0%, over-rejection 31.9 / 26.8 / 33.5 / 35.1%. Same harness. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07667v2)
- **Sequential Consensus** compares SPRT to threshold-on-score and threshold-on-stability only in simulation (6.79 and 5.14 rounds; 79.5% and 50.1% hypothesis accuracy) and to fixed-5 and single-vote on real LLMs. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.19193v1)
- **Semantic Early-Stopping** compares fixed_k1/k3/k6, critic_only, random_stop, entropy_only, full cascade and an oracle on one HotpotQA split of 60 questions. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.27009v1)
- **Self-Correction as Feedback Control** compares generic refinement, Self-Refine prompt, verify-first prompt, ASC and self-consistency on GSM8K for one degrading model. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.22273v2)
- A search for a cross-task benchmark of stopping criteria returned only these single-harness studies plus LoopTrap and DeployBench; no benchmark of stop rules exists. — [Semantic Early-Stopping abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27009)
- A search for round-cap sensitivity in ChatDev or MetaGPT returned only the original papers' role and phase ablations (ChatDev: removing roles cuts quality 0.3953 → 0.2212 and executability 0.8800 → 0.5800; removing testing left scores unchanged), nothing on the number of rounds. — [ChatDev, ACL 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.810.pdf)

### Inferences
- The two debate-stopping groups that could be compared most easily (Conformal Social Choice and Budgeted Act-or-Defer share authors, agents, and T=4) did not include each other's rule as a baseline beyond CRC; a same-trajectory comparison is feasible from their released setups.
- VRR-Stop's finding that fixed-N gets worse monotonically with N (0.246 → 0.122 → 0.116) and Sequential Consensus's finding that fixed-5 gained nothing over a single vote on GSM8K and MMLU point the same way from different harnesses, but nobody has tested the same rule in both.

### Gaps
- No comparison of any statistical stop rule across two harness types (e.g. the same SPRT on a debate and on a verify-repair loop).
- No stop rule tested on role-based software teams or orchestrator-worker systems; the only orchestrator-level item found is a "self-healing orchestrator" preprint (arXiv 2606.01416) that treats verifier rejections as recoverable signals with a recovery budget, which I did not read in full. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01416v1)
- No shared trajectory set or replay protocol across papers; VRR-Stop's frozen-trajectory replay is the only one designed for it.

## KQ5. What do the papers say remains open, and what is visible from comparing them?

### Takeaway
The authors' own open lists cluster on four items: anytime-valid guarantees under adaptive stopping (Conformal Social Choice, MiCP), non-stationary or correlated rounds (Sequential Consensus, VRR-Stop, Feedback Control), judge or verifier discrimination as the real bottleneck (Sequential Consensus, VRR-Stop), and open-ended generation (nearly all). Comparing the papers adds gaps none of them names: no head-to-head across harnesses, no per-round validity numbers past round 6, no round-cap sensitivity for role-based teams, no agent-declared vs harness-computed comparison on the same trajectories, and no test of any rule under the adversarial termination attacks LoopTrap demonstrates.

### Cited Findings (open questions named by the papers)
- **Sequential Consensus:** i.i.d. violated in real debates; adversarial agents untested; static likelihoods; non-Beta judge scores; judge-agent overlap; K=3 only; no JudgeBench or long-form real-LLM run; a transcript-reading or token-confidence judge is suggested but untested; no production deployment. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.19193v1)
- **VRR-Stop:** round-dependent α_t and β_t; multi-level plan quality instead of binary validity; instance-conditional noise; cross-task compute allocation; online switching between Stop and Guard; sweeps of M and τ; multi-round look-ahead; only three model families; no transfer of parameters across model, verifier or prompt. — [arXiv HTML tail](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.17641v1)
- **Conformal Social Choice:** anytime-valid sequential methods for adaptive stopping; conditional coverage; distribution shift; open-ended generation; cost of 12 calls per question. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07667v2)
- **Budgeted Act-or-Defer:** state sufficiency vs geometry trade-off; learned representations; open-ended generation; representation gap not shown small; low activation where calibration evidence is thin. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29654v1)
- **MiCP:** group-conditional calibration; more than 3 turns; a third party disputes the finite-sample proof. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.01413v3); [GitHub issue](https://github.com/microprediction/metareasoning/issues/19)
- **Semantic Early-Stopping:** stronger judge; more datasets; the distance-convergence conjecture holds only on average (≈5% monotone). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.27009v1)
- **Feedback Control:** external feedback and verifiers; open-ended generation needs new EIR/ECR definitions; compute trade-off across budgets; the "verify-then-edit" mechanism is a hypothesis. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.22273v2)
- **If It's Not Buggy:** larger models; environments with exact goals; multi-file codebases; no stopping rule proposed. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10123v1)
- **ECT:** real, non-synthetic tool use; the certificate says nothing about external truth. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23623)
- **Doomed from the Start:** sample complexity to certify high-recall policies; code not yet released. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06503)
- **Stability Detection:** ambiguity breaks the single-concept assumption; agents copying each other breaks independence. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.12697v1)

### Inferences (open questions visible from comparing the papers)
- **No head-to-head across systems.** SPRT (debate), KS stability (judge ensembles), conformal first-singleton (debate), kNN act-or-defer (debate), VRR-Stop (verify-repair), embedding plateau (writer-critic), EIR/ECR (self-refine) have never been run on each other's harness or on a shared trajectory set. The frozen-trajectory replay VRR-Stop uses would make this cheap.
- **No per-round degradation curve past round 5-6 with numbers.** Feedback Control stops at iteration 4, Conformal Social Choice at round 3 (4 in the appendix), VRR-Stop at round 6, Stability Detection reports KS not accuracy to round 10, and the 100-turn bug-fixing runs give figures only. Whether late rounds are flat, cyclic or still falling is unmeasured.
- **No round-cap sensitivity for role-based teams.** ChatDev/MetaGPT ablate roles and phases, not the cap; no 2025-2026 paper applies any statistical stop rule to a software-team harness.
- **No agent-declared vs harness-computed comparison on the same runs.** The closest are the Critic-APPROVED halt inside Semantic Early-Stopping's cascade (full cascade +129% tokens, no quality change) and ECT's critic core (252/288 unsafe completions), both inside their own paper.
- **No stop rule tested under termination attacks.** LoopTrap shows 3.57× step amplification against agent-declared stopping; none of the harness-computed rules has been attacked, and Sequential Consensus explicitly lists adversarial agreement as untested.
- **Convergent constraint none of them resolves.** Three independent papers end on the same sentence in different words: stopping is bounded by verifier or judge discrimination (SPRT: KL ≈ 0 on MMLU; VRR-Stop: J ≤ 0.15 flips the sign; conformal: Math interception 16.2%). The open question that follows, how to get a discriminating signal cheaply when the generator and verifier share a model, is not addressed by any stop-rule paper found.
- **Statistical status.** Of the twelve rules, zero have a confirmed peer-reviewed venue for the stop-rule claim itself; MaAS (ICML keyword) and the EMNLP 2025 scaling theory (Acc_t = Upp − α^t(Upp − Acc_0), which predicts monotone convergence and cannot express the interior peak VRR-Stop observes) are the only reviewed items. — [EMNLP 2025 theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.16456)

### Gaps
- OpenReview reviews for any of these preprints: none found by search; ICML 2026 and NeurIPS 2026 submission status unknown.
- The Stability Detection paper's cost figures and its Appendix C limitations were not in the visible text.
- The Budgeted Act-or-Defer, Conformal Social Choice, Stability Detection and ECT HTML pages were truncated at 100k characters; appendix-only numbers beyond those cited above were not read.
