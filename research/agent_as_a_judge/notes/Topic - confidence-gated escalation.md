# Topic - confidence-gated escalation

A cheap model or judge answers first and its low-confidence cases go to a stronger model, a judge or a human. Cascades, deferral, how the threshold is chosen, whether it transfers to new data, whether the stages make the same errors.

Group: Mechanisms. See [[Topics]]. Same group: [[Topic - calibration]], [[Topic - presentation sensitivity]], [[Topic - abstention and fallback]], [[Topic - adaptive reasoning depth]], [[Topic - reasoning prompting]].

## Papers (13)
- [[2024-01 Scalable meta-evaluation via agent debate ScaleEval]] — ScaleEval uses debate among several LLM agents to meta-evaluate LLM evaluators, escalating to humans only when agents disagree. (also: [[Topic - judge and decision-model audits]], [[Topic - alternative evaluation paradigms]])
- [[2024-07 Trust or escalate]] — Selective evaluation: the judge abstains when unconfident, with a calibrated threshold giving a provable lower bound on human agreement; Cascaded Selective Evaluation escalates from cheap to strong judges. (also: [[Topic - LLM and agent judges]], [[Topic - abstention and fallback]])
- [[2024-08 Can unconfident LLM annotations be used for confident conclusions]] — Confidence-driven inference: combine LLM annotations with a few strategically chosen human labels (where the LLM is unconfident) to get valid statistical estimates. (also: [[Topic - LLM and agent judges]], [[Topic - stopping rules]])
- [[2026 Does System One Transfer]] — A pre-registered test of whether the typed-decision model Jev (label plus probability in one forward pass) stays accurate and calibrated on Indonesian regional languages, how many labels a local classifier needs to match it, and whether routing its least confident decisions to a fallback pays off. (also: [[Topic - decision models]], [[Topic - calibration]], [[Topic - judge and decision-model audits]])
- [[2026-09 Calibrated Decisions at Scale]] — An independent audit of Jev, a typed decision model that returns probabilities and no text, coding Texas crash narratives at population scale: its probabilities rank well but overstate prevalence until a two-parameter map fitted on a few thousand human labels fixes them, and the calibrated probabilities become a per-variable human-review budget. (also: [[Topic - decision models]], [[Topic - judge and decision-model audits]], [[Topic - calibration]])
- [[2026-09 Decision Models vs LLMs as Rubric Judges]] — Tests whether typed "decision models" (Jev and Perplexity's open Decider), which return only probabilities over the permitted answers, can replace or front-run flash-tier LLM rubric judges. They mostly can replace them at a small fraction of the cost, but they make the same errors, so a cascade adds almost no accuracy. (also: [[Topic - decision models]], [[Topic - judge and decision-model audits]])
- [[2026-09 JEV-as-a-Judge]] — Decision-only judge outputs label probabilities and escalates low-confidence cases to a reasoning judge; cascade is 0.9 pts above a GPT-6 judge at 41% of its cost on 1,610 held-out pairs. (also: [[Topic - decision models]], [[Topic - LLM and agent judges]])
- [[2026-09 Jev-as-a-Judge for RAG Claim Verification]] — Pits a non-generative decision model (jev-1.13.0: typed verdict plus native probability, no text) against a frontier LLM judge on RAG claim verification, finds an accuracy tie at 187x lower cost but different errors, and tests a cheap-first confidence-gated cascade. (also: [[Topic - decision models]], [[Topic - LLM and agent judges]])
- [[2026-09 Laya as a Typed Probabilistic Assessor]] — An independent, preregistered study showing the open 421M Laya typed-decision assessor reproduces its card's accuracy but ships uniformly under-confident, one fitted temperature fixes most of that, and its confidence gate beats random escalation but misses a 10% error target. (also: [[Topic - decision models]], [[Topic - judge and decision-model audits]], [[Topic - calibration]])
- [[2026-10 Benchmarking System One Decision Models]] — One harness sends the same typed requests to hosted and open decision models (Jev and five open families), open generative LMs and trained classifiers, then turns accuracy, calibration, option-naming robustness, selective risk and cascade cost into condition-dependent rules for automated decision gates. (also: [[Topic - decision models]], [[Topic - judge and decision-model audits]])
- [[2026-10 Fast Models, Slow Evidence]] — Compares the open-weight LAYA and hosted JEV System-1 decision models on 11 agent-harness decision points, then audits its own first draft and finds that errors in cost, quality and threshold accounting, not in scored predictions, had changed the deployment advice. (also: [[Topic - decision models]], [[Topic - judge and decision-model audits]])
- [[2026-10 JevAgentHarness]] — Hands an agent's routine multiple-choice steps (which tool, which argument, whether to reflect) to a cheap decision model such as JEV, accepting its answer only above a per-choice confidence threshold fitted on the agent's own runs, and trains a 4B decision model on the states the agent actually visits. (also: [[Topic - decision models]], [[Topic - open decision models]])
- [[2026-10 System Switch]] — A fast one-pass "System One" decision model takes every decision in real-time Doom and hands control to a slow vision reasoner only when a gate on its confidence or on lack of progress opens, and the paper measures what that deferral buys offline and in closed loop. (also: [[Topic - decision models]], [[Topic - adaptive reasoning depth]])
