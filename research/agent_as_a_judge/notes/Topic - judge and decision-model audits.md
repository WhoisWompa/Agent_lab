# Topic - judge and decision-model audits

Empirical studies of how judges or decision models behave: biases, adversarial cues, calibration measurement, probabilistic coherence, robustness, cost, failures inside agent loops, meta-evaluation benchmarks of judges. Measurement is the contribution.

Group: Judging and evaluation. See [[Topics]]. - [[2026-10 Reading Right, Acting Wrong]] — Pairs each next-action question to a System One decision model with independent factual probes on the same agent state, and finds that most wrong actions by strong models follow correct reading of the facts. (also: [[Topic - decision models]])
- [[2026-10 Typed Decision Models on Tabular Data]] — Tests typed decision models against LLMs on fixed-answer table decisions and finds them cheaper and as accurate, robust to question wording but not to table layout, with their probability marking which decisions will flip. (also: [[Topic - decision models]], [[Topic - presentation sensitivity]])
- [[2026-10 TypedBench]] — A contamination-resistant benchmark that tests whether typed decision models (hosted Jev, open Laya encoder, open Kev decoders) give probabilities software can act on, judged by wording stability, calibration against a noise floor, and decision cost. (also: [[Topic - decision models]], [[Topic - calibration]], [[Topic - presentation sensitivity]])
- [[2026-10 When Faster Decisions Do Not Make Better Agents]] — Swaps the LLM action selector in five text-only agents (ALFWorld and AndroidWorld) for Jev, a typed decision model, and shows that a 66-78% cut in per-decision cost does not reliably become cheaper or faster delivered tasks, because extra workflow work, failed recovery and premature completion eat the savings. (also: [[Topic - decision models]])
