# FunDM: Cheap & Label-Free Decision Models from Frozen Language Models

## One line
Trains a 1-5M-parameter head on two-thirds of a frozen Qwen3-8B to copy the full model's own answer probabilities, giving a label-free decision model (and a distilled agent-run judge) that keeps about 99% of the full model's accuracy at about 1.56x the throughput.

## Method
The problem: decision models (Jev, Contrastive Language Models/CLM) score candidate answers or actions, but CLM encodes each option separately, trains on labels, and scores far below the same backbone simply asked for a letter. The claim: a pretrained LM already knows how to choose, so a cheap readout suffices.

The construction:
- **Joint encoding.** The question, all lettered options and an "Answer:" cue go in one prompt. At a fixed layer L (24 of 36), take the hidden state at the end of each option and at the answer position. The answer state has seen everything (causal attention), so one forward pass scores all K options.
- **Scoring head.** Two projections map the answer state to a query and each option state to a key; a scaled dot product plus softmax gives option probabilities. Shared weights let K vary (1.05M parameters). FunDM+1T adds one bidirectional transformer layer inside the head so earlier options can attend to later ones (5.38M).
- **Label-free training.** The teacher is the same frozen model at full depth with its LM head; its next-token probabilities over the option letters, renormalized, are the target. The head minimizes KL(teacher || head). No correct-answer labels are used for fitting or checkpoint selection (validation KL selects).
- **Inference** runs layers 1-24 and the head, skipping 12 layers and the LM head.

Why it should work: soft teacher probabilities carry more information than one-hot labels, and the decision is bet to be mostly formed by two-thirds depth. The same recipe distills a yes/no "will hidden tests pass?" judge over coding-agent runs into a head that ranks runs by its yes-minus-no logit.

## Evaluated on
- Keeps the teacher's accuracy at two-thirds depth: 0.750 (FunDM+1T) and 0.747 (FunDM) mean over eight MCQ benchmarks vs 0.757 for the full model; every benchmark within 2.5 points.
- Far above CLM on the same backbone: 0.464 for fine-tuned CLM, 0.443 released, 0.357 CLM-shaped from scratch. But the full model alone (0.757) already beats CLM, so most of the gap is the backbone, not the head.
- Teacher probabilities beat labels as a target: 0.747/0.751 vs 0.735/0.740 for the teacher's top choice and 0.713/0.734 for correct-answer labels, with everything else held fixed.
- Cheaper: 1.56-1.57x vLLM throughput, first-decision latency 93.7 to about 58 ms, peak memory 20.5 to 13.8 GiB on MMLU-Pro.
- Agent-run selection matches the judge it copies: 74.6/74.4 of 100 SWE-rebench tasks vs 74 for the full-model judge, 52.0 for an action-trained CLM-shaped head, 54.9 random.

## Limitations admitted by the authors
- Only Qwen3-8B tested; "two-thirds depth is enough" may be backbone-specific.
- Agreement with the teacher is about 88% on average and about 75% on MMLU-Pro: similar accuracy hides different answers.
- Depth tolerance is uneven: at layer 20 MMLU-Pro keeps only 86-88% of teacher accuracy vs 94-95% on the mean, so harder sets need more layers.
- Agent selection is "comparable", not shown equal: paired 95% intervals span -8.0 to +8.8 tasks, and heads pick a different run from the judge on 60-69 of 100 tasks.
- The agent teacher is barely informative as a probability: it says yes to 94% of runs and its top run exceeds 0.999 in every task, so distillation copies a near-saturated signal.
- The student inherits the teacher's errors and biases by construction.
- Efficiency measured on one GPU and one workload, excluding queueing and HTTP, so serving gains are indicative only.

## What it leaves untried
- Distilling a stronger or external teacher. The ceiling is the frozen model's own accuracy (0.757); the method compresses a judge but cannot improve it.
- Calibration of the head. Only CE heads get ECE (0.067-0.194 on MMLU-Pro); whether KL-distilled FunDM scores are trustworthy as probabilities, which matters for a judge, is not measured.
- Adaptive exit depth per input, since hard sets like MMLU-Pro lose more at shallow layers.
