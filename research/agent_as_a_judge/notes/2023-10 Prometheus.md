# Prometheus: Inducing Fine-grained Evaluation Capability in Language Models

## One line
A 13B Llama-2-Chat fine-tuned to give rubric-conditioned 1-5 scores plus written feedback, as an open substitute for GPT-4 grading.

## Method
- Training data: the Feedback Collection, fully synthetic, generated by GPT-4: about 1K fine-grained score rubrics, 20K instructions, 100K responses with GPT-4-written feedback and scores.
- Input: instruction, response, a user-written score rubric and a reference answer (score-5 exemplar). Output: feedback text then an integer score (pointwise, absolute grading).
- Sees only the final output; executes nothing.

## Evaluated on
- Reported Pearson correlation about 0.897 with human evaluators on 45 customised rubrics, on par with GPT-4 (0.882) and far above GPT-3.5 (figures from the paper's abstract, recalled; verify against arXiv:2310.08491).
- Humans preferred Prometheus feedback over GPT-4 feedback in a majority of pairwise comparisons (reported 58.6% in the paper).

## Limitations admitted by the authors
- Depends heavily on the reference answer; ablation removing it drops correlation sharply.
- Trained only on GPT-4 outputs, so it inherits GPT-4's preferences.
- Pointwise absolute scoring only (fixed in Prometheus 2).

## Limitations found by others
- Huang et al. 2024 (arXiv:2403.02839): fine-tuned judges such as Prometheus behave like task-specific classifiers and lose to GPT-4 on generalisability, fairness and adaptability out of their training format.
- JudgeBench (arXiv:2410.12784): fine-tuned judges, Prometheus 2 included, are near random on hard, objectively-checkable response pairs.

## What it leaves untried
- Judging without a reference answer, on tasks where the judge must derive correctness itself.
- Any verification step (execution, retrieval).
