# Topic - presentation sensitivity

Decisions that change with option order, option names or labels, the set of listed options, question form or request serialisation, though the content is logically the same; and methods that remove the sensitivity.

Group: Mechanisms. See [[Topics]]. Same group: [[Topic - confidence-gated escalation]], [[Topic - calibration]], [[Topic - abstention and fallback]], [[Topic - adaptive reasoning depth]], [[Topic - reasoning prompting]].

## Papers (36)
- [[2023-05 LLMs are not Fair Evaluators]] — Shows that simply swapping answer order can flip GPT-4/ChatGPT verdicts and proposes multiple-evidence, balanced-position and human-in-the-loop calibration. (also: [[Topic - judge and decision-model audits]], [[Topic - LLM and agent judges]])
- [[2023-06 Judging LLM-as-a-judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena]] — Introduces MT-Bench and early Chatbot Arena; GPT-4 judge agrees with humans over 80%, on par with human-human agreement, while naming position, verbosity and self-enhancement biases. (also: [[Topic - LLM and agent judges]], [[Topic - judge and decision-model audits]], [[Topic - alternative evaluation paradigms]])
- [[2023-09 Benchmarking Cognitive Biases CoBBLEr]] — CoBBLEr measures six cognitive biases (egocentric, order, bandwagon, attention, etc.) across 15 LLM judges: biased on ~40% of comparisons, RBO with humans 49.6%. (also: [[Topic - judge and decision-model audits]])
- [[2023-09 Split and Merge PORTIA]] — PORTIA splits candidates into aligned segments and merges them, raising consistency by 47.46% relative on average and GPT-4 consistency to 98%. (also: [[Topic - LLM and agent judges]])
- [[2023-10 JudgeLM]] — Vicuna-based 7B/13B/33B pairwise judges fine-tuned on 100K GPT-4 judgments, with augmentations against position, knowledge and format bias. (also: [[Topic - trained judges and reward models]], [[Topic - LLM and agent judges]])
- [[2024-05 Inconsistent and Biased Evaluators]] — On SummEval, LLM evaluators show familiarity bias, skewed rating distributions, anchoring across attributes, low inter-sample agreement and prompt sensitivity. (also: [[Topic - judge and decision-model audits]])
- [[2024-06 Systematic study of position bias]] — 150K+ judgments by 15 judges on MTBench and DevBench: position bias is systematic, judge- and task-specific, weakly tied to length and strongly to the quality gap between candidates. (also: [[Topic - judge and decision-model audits]])
- [[2024-10 Justice or Prejudice CALM]] — CALM framework quantifies 12 judge biases (position, verbosity, authority, bandwagon, sentiment, self-enhancement, CoT, distraction, etc.) by automated principle-guided perturbation; significant biases persist in strong models on specific tasks. (also: [[Topic - judge and decision-model audits]])
- [[2025-03 Know Thy Judge safety judges]] — Output style changes shift safety judges' false-negative rate by up to 0.24; adversarial generations make some judges label 100% of harmful outputs as safe. (also: [[Topic - judge and decision-model audits]], [[Topic - LLM and agent judges]])
- [[2025-03 Safer or Luckier safety evaluators]] — 11 safety judges: apologetic-language artifacts skew preferences by up to 98%; larger is not more robust; juries help but artifact sensitivity persists. (also: [[Topic - judge and decision-model audits]])
- [[2025-04 Assessing Judging Bias in Large Reasoning Models]] — Reasoning models (R1, o1) remain susceptible to bandwagon, authority, position and distraction bias, prefer later positions, and show 'superficial reflection bias' toward text like 'wait, let me think'. (also: [[Topic - judge and decision-model audits]])
- [[2025-04 Pairwise or Pointwise feedback protocols]] — Shows pairwise judging is more exploitable by distractor features than absolute scoring: preferences flip in ~35% of cases vs 9% for absolute scores. (also: [[Topic - judge and decision-model audits]])
- [[2025-05 LLMs get lost in multi-turn conversation]] — Sharded simulation of underspecified multi-turn instructions shows average performance drops of about 39% vs single-turn, mainly from unreliability (as recalled). (also: [[Topic - user simulation]])
- [[2025-09 Silent Judge shortcut bias]] — GPT-4o and Gemini-2.5-Flash shift verdicts on injected provenance (Expert > Human > LLM > Unknown) and recency cues yet almost never mention the cue in their justification. (also: [[Topic - judge and decision-model audits]])
- [[2026-02 Position bias in rubric-based judging]] — Rubric scoring behaves like multiple choice: judges prefer score options at model-specific positions and criterion order shifts scores; a few random permutations reduce the error. (also: [[Topic - judge and decision-model audits]])
- [[2026-02 SCOPE selective conformal optimized pairwise LLM judging]] — Selective conformal risk control for pairwise judges; Bidirectional Preference Entropy (query both orders) as the uncertainty score. (also: [[Topic - LLM and agent judges]], [[Topic - abstention and fallback]])
- [[2026-04 Bias mitigation strategies in LLM-as-a-Judge]] — Head-to-head test of nine debiasing strategies on five 2025-26 judges: style (markdown) bias dominates, position bias is now small, and a debiased mid-tier judge beats frontier judges at 1/15 the cost. (also: [[Topic - judge and decision-model audits]], [[Topic - LLM and agent judges]])
- [[2026-04 Coin Flip Judge]] — Repeated identical judging: pairwise verdicts flip 13.6% on average (28% of questions >20%), GPT-4o-mini picks A 72% of the time, cross-judge kappa 0.51, and ~11 trials are needed for a stable majority. (also: [[Topic - judge and decision-model audits]])
- [[2026-06 Reliability without Validity]] — 21 judges, 541K judgments: exact-match agreement exceeds Cohen's kappa by 33-41 pp on MT-Bench, judge rankings move up to 14 places across benchmarks, and >0.95 test-retest reliability coexists with >0.10 position bias. (also: [[Topic - judge and decision-model audits]])
- [[2026-09 AnyJev]] — Reads a Jev-style typed decision (a probability over a fixed option list) from one prefill of a frozen open instruction-tuned LLM, and corrects the readout's label and position biases without any training. (also: [[Topic - decision models]], [[Topic - open decision models]])
- [[2026-09 More Choices, Fewer Decisions]] — Shows that JEV-like direct-decision models (JEV 1.13, KEV, StartLux) squeeze their verdicts onto far fewer levels of a graded rating scale than the gold labels occupy, that finer scales make this worse, and that targeted LoRA post-training can largely undo it on the trained scales. (also: [[Topic - decision models]], [[Topic - judge and decision-model audits]])
- [[2026-09 PACT]] — Replaces plain cross-entropy in fine-tuning a single-token typed-decision model (Nimble-style, one logit read per allowed answer code) with four loss terms drawn from the contrastive-pair structure already in its training data; it lowers position bias and rubric error and steadies training, without raising accuracy. (also: [[Topic - decision models]], [[Topic - open decision models]], [[Topic - calibration]])
- [[2026-09 Probability Contracts]] — A benchmark of small synthetic worlds with exact posteriors that scores a model's probability reports on three separate axes (error against the true posterior, coherence across equivalent requests, and the loss of the act-or-defer decision they trigger), and shows the three axes rank models differently. (also: [[Topic - decision models]], [[Topic - judge and decision-model audits]], [[Topic - calibration]])
- [[2026-09 Type-Safe Is Not Error-Free]] — Shows that typed decision models (Jev and two open-weight copies, Laya and Open-Jev) choose options by the conventional meaning of their names, such as yes/no, rather than by the explicit definitions bound to them, while still reporting a 0% type-error rate. (also: [[Topic - decision models]], [[Topic - judge and decision-model audits]])
- [[2026-10 Adversarial Cues in Decision Models Used as Judges]] — Shows that one colon added to a wrong candidate answer makes the Jev decision model accept it, but only when the same judging request is sent with sorted JSON keys, so a judge that passes every competence control can still be exploitable under one logically equivalent presentation. (also: [[Topic - decision models]], [[Topic - judge and decision-model audits]])
- [[2026-10 All Verdicts are Not Equal]] — Reliability audit of six frontier judges showing verdicts change across identical temperature-0 runs, order swaps flip most verdicts on hard tasks, and the most consistent judge is consistently wrong (51% ground-truth agreement). (also: [[Topic - judge and decision-model audits]])
- [[2026-10 Cheap, Consistent, Calibrated, but Biased]] — Audits eight typed decision models (TDMs) against the CALM catalogue of LLM-judge biases, finding each biased in 3 to 8 of 12 tests, though they are 37 times cheaper and more consistent than 2026 LLM judges. (also: [[Topic - decision models]], [[Topic - judge and decision-model audits]])
- [[2026-10 Consider, Then Choose]] — Runoff, a training-free rule that scores each option alone to keep a few candidates, then compares only those in every rotation of a model-derived ranking, so a single-pass decision model's choice no longer depends on option order. (also: [[Topic - decision models]])
- [[2026-10 Does a Hosted Decision Model Respect Irrelevant Alternatives]] — Shows that the Jev decision model's option probabilities depend on which other options are listed (it violates independence of irrelevant alternatives), so its probabilities are only meaningful relative to the option list, not as fixed properties of each option. (also: [[Topic - decision models]], [[Topic - judge and decision-model audits]])
- [[2026-10 Jeva]] — A decision-native architecture for fast choose/yes-no/score judgments that encodes request and options separately, making decisions order-invariant, null-aware, scalable to thousands of options and cacheable across requests. (also: [[Topic - decision models]], [[Topic - open decision models]], [[Topic - abstention and fallback]])
- [[2026-10 Labels Override Definitions in Jev-Style Typed Decision Models]] — Shows that open Jev-style typed decision models (and LLMs scoring label strings) decide from the option's name rather than the caller's written definition, and traces this to one line of prompt rendering that writes "{label}: {definition}" into the input. (also: [[Topic - decision models]], [[Topic - judge and decision-model audits]])
- [[2026-10 Lost in the Fallback]] — Introduces the Paired Fallback Robustness (PFR) Test and shows that open typed decision models (Jev-style choosers) often fail to pick a supplied fallback option ("Other", "Defer") even when they recognise the input's category, and flip decisions under logically equivalent wordings of the fallback rule. (also: [[Topic - decision models]], [[Topic - judge and decision-model audits]], [[Topic - abstention and fallback]])
- [[2026-10 Prism-1]] — Shows that the only one-pass decision read that ignores option order and cannot be steered by an added option is a softmax over scores that each see one option alone, and builds that read into a fine-tuned LLM. (also: [[Topic - decision models]], [[Topic - open decision models]])
- [[2026-10 Typed Decision Models on Tabular Data]] — Tests typed decision models against LLMs on fixed-answer table decisions and finds them cheaper and as accurate, robust to question wording but not to table layout, with their probability marking which decisions will flip. (also: [[Topic - decision models]], [[Topic - judge and decision-model audits]])
- [[2026-10 TypedBench]] — A contamination-resistant benchmark that tests whether typed decision models (hosted Jev, open Laya encoder, open Kev decoders) give probabilities software can act on, judged by wording stability, calibration against a noise floor, and decision cost. (also: [[Topic - decision models]], [[Topic - judge and decision-model audits]], [[Topic - calibration]])
- [[2026-10 Verdict Encode Once, Decide Many]] — An open ModernBERT encoder (Verdict-MQ) that reads one piece of state once and returns a distribution for every typed question about it, with option-order and question-grouping invariance built into the attention mask. (also: [[Topic - decision models]], [[Topic - open decision models]])
