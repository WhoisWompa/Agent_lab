# SanSi: A Looped Typed Decision Model for System 1.5 Thinking

## One line
Turns a language model pre-trained to loop (Ouro-1.4B) into a typed decision model that reads option probabilities after every pass of the same layers, so one small model buys accuracy and reasoning depth with extra compute instead of extra parameters or generated text.

## Method
Problem: typed decision models (Jev, Kev) give every decision one fixed forward pass, which fails on decisions that need several dependent steps. The Jev API drops from 100% on one-step liar chains to chance from six steps on. The usual fixes cost parameters (a bigger model) or tokens and latency (chain-of-thought).

Idea ("System 1.5"): apply the same 24-layer stack T times to its own hidden state before the typed readout. This adds compute, but no parameters and no tokens. What is new:
- **A readout after every loop.** Each loop has its own small readout, so one 8-loop run gives the answer at every budget from 1 to 8.
- **Every loop is trained with proper scoring rules** (cross-entropy plus Brier). The target is uniform for unanswerable items. Only LoRA and the readouts are trained (61M parameters).
- **Key assumption:** the backbone must already have been pre-trained to loop. The recipe adapts the loops; it cannot create them.

Why it should work: each loop lets the model revise its state before it commits, like stepping through a chain of relations. Supervising every loop makes the early exits usable: with only the last loop trained, loop 1 gets 35.6% instead of 58.4%.

## Evaluated on
- **Looping, not the backbone, drives the gain.** 72.0% vs 58.4% for SmolLM2-1.7B of the same shape and recipe. Ouro trained with one loop gets 58.6%.
- **Small but costly.** 1.8 points below Qwen3.5-4B (3x the parameters), at 3.3x its GPU time. The Jev API is far ahead: 83.5% vs 71.9% for SanSi-2.6B on sources neither side trained on.
- **Most of the gain arrives early.** Loops 2 and 3 deliver 88% of it. Ouro's frozen exit gate keeps loop-8 accuracy with 44% fewer loops.
- **Depth beyond training.** On liar chains of 9 to 16 steps (training goes up to 8), SanSi gets 72.6% while Qwen3.5-4B sits at chance (50.0%), even when trained 4x longer (51.5%).
- **Better at spotting missing evidence.** Hard answers on unanswerable items fall from 27.1% to 17.5% across the loops.
- **As the only reward for GRPO, with no gold answers,** it lifts the generator from 37.0% to 52.1% correct when read at loop 8. Read at loop 1, it changes nothing (37.5%).

## Limitations admitted by the authors
- The loops add confidence, not self-knowledge. Confidence on wrong answers rises from 0.567 to 0.660, the AUROC for separating right from wrong answers moves only from 0.760 to 0.795, and 19.7% of errors carry confidence of at least 0.9. That hurts callers who accept or escalate on the probability. ECE is best at loop 3 (0.082) and reaches 0.149 on near transfer; averaging the loops halves the overall ECE to 0.044.
- The method depends on looped pre-training. Adding a loop to SmolLM2 gives no gain (33.5% or 58.3% vs 58.4%), and only the Ouro family was tested.
- The compute is capped at the trained loops. Running 16 loops drops accuracy to 70.1%.
- Later loops can break answers: 11.7% of items are right at some loop but wrong at loop 8.
- The gate's 44% saving shrinks to about 25% with static batches.
- The depth results rest on two synthetic tasks of at most 336 tokens, and the verifier result on one dataset and one generator.

## What it leaves untried
- A per-item loop selector or a learned halting rule. The 11.7% of items that are right at some earlier loop show there is headroom the gate does not reach.
- Using the loop trajectory as a confidence signal. Answers that never change are 82.2% right, but the paper never feeds that signal into the output probability.
- A head-to-head against judges that generate reasoning, at equal cost and across several generators. Without it, "System 1.5 can stand in for System 2" is untested.
