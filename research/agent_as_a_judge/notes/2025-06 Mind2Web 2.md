# Mind2Web 2: Evaluating Agentic Search with Agent-as-a-Judge

## One line
Benchmark of 130 long-horizon agentic-search tasks judged by task-specific judge agents that walk a tree-structured rubric and verify each claim and its cited source.

## Method
- Each task has a rubric tree (avg ~50 nodes, max 603, up to 6 levels). Leaves are binary checks; **critical** node failure zeroes its parent, **non-critical** nodes give partial credit; **sequential** nodes short-circuit later siblings; aggregated "gate-then-average".
- One judge **script** per task (agentic workflow) over a shared toolkit with two LLM tools: **Extractor** (pulls names, prices, URLs from the answer) and **Verifier** (checks a claim against the cited page's text and screenshot; pages pre-cached). Judge LLM o4-mini.
- Scripts drafted by Claude-3.7-Sonnet, self-debugged against a Deep Research answer with verification stubbed, self-reflected against checklists, then two-stage human review.
- Judges only the final answer + its cited URLs, not the agent trajectory.

## Evaluated on
- 130 tasks, >1,000 human hours to build; 10 agentic search systems + humans. Best (OpenAI Deep Research) reaches 50-70% of human performance in half the time.
- Judge reliability: on 15 sampled tasks (720 leaf verifications) a human agreed with all 15 rubrics; of 35 node-level discrepancies, 27 were human errors; reported verifier correctness 99.03%.
- No dollar cost reported; a short-circuit mechanism skips blocked nodes to save cost.

## Limitations admitted by the authors
- LLM extraction/verification can err; URL attribution assumes cited pages are truthful; collapsed/hidden content may be unreachable; each attribution must be checkable on a single page.
- Excludes vague/subjective queries, video tasks, non-English sites; live sites drift and need maintenance; 120 of 130 tasks private.

## Limitations found by others
- Per-task judge scripts need heavy human review, so the approach does not scale cheaply to new tasks (own inference from the pipeline).
- AJ-Bench (2604.18240) reuses Mind2Web 2 tasks and finds misreading tool outputs is the most common judge failure in the Search domain.

## What it leaves untried
- Generating rubric trees fully automatically and measuring how much human review is actually needed.
- Judging the search trajectory (e.g. whether sources were actually visited) rather than only the answer.
