# Topic - calibration

Making reported probabilities mean what they say: proper-scoring-rule training (Brier, log score), post-hoc recalibration (temperature, Platt, isotonic), probabilistic coherence, and when a fix transfers.

Group: Mechanisms. See [[Topics]]. Same group: [[Topic - confidence-gated escalation]], [[Topic - presentation sensitivity]], [[Topic - abstention and fallback]], [[Topic - adaptive reasoning depth]], [[Topic - reasoning prompting]].

- [[2025-07 Beyond Binary Rewards RLCR]] — RLCR adds a Brier-score term to the binary correctness reward in RL reasoning training, so the model learns to state a calibrated confidence alongside its answer at no cost in accuracy. (also: [[Topic - judges as reward signals]])

- [[2026-01 Calibration-Aware RL for Decision-Making LLMs]] — Shows that RL with verifiable rewards leaves reasoning classifiers near-certain on every verdict because the final decision token only copies the reasoning's conclusion, and fixes it by training that one token with a cross-entropy loss toward one-hot when right and uniform when wrong. - [[2026-09 Beyond Calibration]] — A label-free battery of logically linked questions shows that Jev's native probabilities follow the probability axioms more closely than an open LLM's, but still break them in specific question forms, mainly by over-endorsing single-label statements. - [[2026-09 Bongard]] — An open 4B-4B encoder-decoder decision model that reads a state once, answers many typed questions about it with a trained probability head, and is trained on one GPU through supervised judgments, contrastive alignment and sandbox outcome distributions. - [[2026-09 Calibrated Decisions at Scale]] — An independent audit of Jev, a typed decision model that returns probabilities and no text, coding Texas crash narratives at population scale: its probabilities rank well but overstate prevalence until a two-parameter map fitted on a few thousand human labels fixes them, and the calibrated probabilities become a per-variable human-review budget. - [[2026-09 Chinese-Jev]] — A 322M-parameter encoder-only Jev-style decision model trained on 10 million Chinese decisions, then fine-tuned separately for medicine, law and finance, that matches or beats the hosted Jev on general Chinese tasks at a fraction of the latency. (also: [[Topic - decision models]], [[Topic - open decision models]])
- [[2026-09 Do System One Decisions Add Up]] — Tests whether the probabilities that decision models (Jev and Laya) give for a broad category, a fine label, and a fine label within an assumed category are consistent with each other, and finds they are not: splitting a decision into stages moves accuracy by about 20 points, up for one model and down for the other. (also: [[Topic - decision models]], [[Topic - judge and decision-model audits]])
- [[2026-09 Evaluating and Benchmarking the System One Model Jev]] — The first independent public-benchmark evaluation of Jev, a commercial non-generative "System One" decision model, checking its accuracy, the vendor's calibration claim, and where it fails, against two open-weight LLMs scored on identical requests. - [[2026-09 Jev Thinks I Dont Know Sys1Cal-v1]] — Builds a True/False benchmark with exactly known probabilities to test whether the System One decision model Jev returns probabilities that mean what they say, and finds that its Choice answers act as if a hidden "uncertain" mass had been dropped and the rest renormalised. - [[2026-09 Laya as a Typed Probabilistic Assessor]] — An independent, preregistered study showing the open 421M Laya typed-decision assessor reproduces its card's accuracy but ships uniformly under-confident, one fitted temperature fixes most of that, and its confidence gate beats random escalation but misses a 10% error target. - [[2026-09 OpenJev-RLCD]] — An open recipe for "reinforcement learning for calibrated decisions": it scores the answer distribution a reasoning model reads out after each of its own rationales with a proper scoring rule, calibrating that readout first and then reinforcing the reasoning with the same score. (also: [[Topic - decision models]], [[Topic - open decision models]], [[Topic - judges as reward signals]])
- [[2026-09 PACT]] — Replaces plain cross-entropy in fine-tuning a single-token typed-decision model (Nimble-style, one logit read per allowed answer code) with four loss terms drawn from the contrastive-pair structure already in its training data; it lowers position bias and rubric error and steadies training, without raising accuracy. (also: [[Topic - decision models]], [[Topic - open decision models]], [[Topic - presentation sensitivity]])
- [[2026-09 Probability Contracts]] — A benchmark of small synthetic worlds with exact posteriors that scores a model's probability reports on three separate axes (error against the true posterior, coherence across equivalent requests, and the loss of the act-or-defer decision they trigger), and shows the three axes rank models differently. (also: [[Topic - decision models]], [[Topic - judge and decision-model audits]], [[Topic - presentation sensitivity]])
- [[2026-09 this-that-model-1.0]] — A 1.88B open typed decision model that reads a calibrated distribution over caller-declared options from one hidden state, generating no tokens, so a program gets a 30.9 ms branch instead of an 8758 ms API call. (also: [[Topic - decision models]], [[Topic - open decision models]])
- [[2026-10 Audio System One Model]] — ASOM is a System One model for speech: a 6.2M-parameter reader over frozen Whisper and Qwen encoders that answers typed choice, score and yes/no questions written at request time about a clip, in one pass, each with a calibrated distribution trained on listener votes. - [[2026-10 Beyond Answer Confidence]] — Shows the decision model Jev's calibrated confidence does not signal missing knowledge, while yes/no questions about the case (settled? enough evidence?) do and "do you know?" mostly reads surface cues. - [[2026-10 CaLLM multicalibrated judge]] — Post-hoc multicalibration of a pairwise judge that bounds bias on every group captured by a representation (position, verbosity, family, unknown groups) while improving accuracy. (also: [[Topic - LLM and agent judges]])
