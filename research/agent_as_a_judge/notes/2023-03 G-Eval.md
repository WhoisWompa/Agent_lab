# G-Eval: NLG Evaluation using GPT-4 with Better Human Alignment

## One line
Prompts GPT-4 with auto-generated chain-of-thought evaluation steps and a form-filling score, weighting each score by its token probability, to grade NLG outputs reference-free.

## Method
Task description and criteria go in; the LLM writes evaluation steps (auto-CoT); then fills a form with a score; final score = sum over score tokens of probability x score (probability-weighted to get continuous, less-tied scores). Pointwise, reference-free, sees only the output text.

## Evaluated on
SummEval, Topical-Chat, QAGS. G-Eval-4 reaches Spearman 0.514 with humans on summarisation, above prior metrics.

## Limitations admitted by the authors
- Flags that LLM evaluators may be biased toward LLM-generated text (preliminary analysis).
- Relies on GPT-4 token probabilities (needed sampling for GPT-4 since logprobs were not exposed).

## Limitations found by others
- Stureborg et al. 2024 (2405.01724): LLM evaluators on SummEval show familiarity (low-perplexity) bias, skewed score distributions, anchoring across multi-attribute judgments and low inter-sample agreement.
- Wataoka et al. 2024 (2410.21819): judges rate lower-perplexity text higher regardless of who wrote it, the mechanism behind the bias G-Eval noted.

## What it leaves untried
- No test on objective-correctness tasks; correlation with humans on style-heavy summarisation does not show the judge can detect factual errors.
