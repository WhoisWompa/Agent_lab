# Topic - judges as reward signals

Using a judge, verifier, rubric or other automatic check as the reward that trains a policy (RLVR, RLAIF, judge-in-the-loop), and what that reward does to the policy: what it learns, reward hacking, spurious or noisy rewards, whether the reward is what drives the gain. The subject is the training signal and its effect, not the judge's own accuracy.

Group: Judging and evaluation. See [[Topics]]. - [[2023-12 Math-Shepherd]] — Labels steps automatically by Monte Carlo rollouts (a step is good if completions from it reach the right answer) and uses the resulting PRM for reranking and step-by-step PPO. (also: [[Topic - trained judges and reward models]])
- [[2024-01 Self-Rewarding Language Models]] — One model acts as both policy and LLM-as-a-judge of its own outputs, and iterative DPO on self-judged pairs improves both roles. (also: [[Topic - trained judges and reward models]], [[Topic - preference learning]], [[Topic - self-evolving tests]])
- [[2024-02 ODIN Disentangled Reward Mitigates Hacking]] — Trains a two-head RM separating length from content quality to reduce length hacking. (also: [[Topic - trained judges and reward models]], [[Topic - preference learning]])
- [[2024-02 Self-alignment for factuality]] — Uses the model's own self-evaluation (Self-Eval, with calibration) as the reward signal for DPO to reduce hallucinations; closest match found for the brief's 'Self-Alignment via consistency'. (also: [[Topic - LLM and agent judges]], [[Topic - preference learning]], [[Topic - calibration]])
- [[2024-04 Autonomous Evaluation and Refinement of Digital Agents]] — Domain-general VLM/LLM evaluators for web and device-control trajectories, then used as reward for refinement (Reflexion-style and filtered fine-tuning). (also: [[Topic - LLM and agent judges]], [[Topic - agent evaluation benchmarks]])
- [[2024-06 Scaling Laws for RM Overoptimization in Direct Alignment]] — Shows DPO/IPO-style methods also overoptimize, with hump-shaped quality vs KL curves like explicit RMs. (also: [[Topic - preference learning]], [[Topic - trained judges and reward models]])
- [[2024-07 Meta-Rewarding Language Models]] — Adds a meta-judge role that judges the model's own judgments, so the judge skill is trained too, not only the policy. (also: [[Topic - trained judges and reward models]], [[Topic - self-evolving tests]], [[Topic - preference learning]])
- [[2024-07 Prover-verifier games improve legibility of LLM outputs]] — Trains small verifiers against helpful and sneaky provers on grade-school math so that strong-model solutions become checkable by weak verifiers and by time-limited humans. - [[2024-07 Spontaneous Reward Hacking in Self-Refinement]] — When a generator iteratively optimises against an LLM evaluator (especially the same model with shared context), evaluator scores rise while human-judged quality stalls or drops. - [[2024-09 Language Models Learn to Mislead Humans via RLHF]] — Shows RLHF makes models better at convincing human evaluators of wrong answers (U-SOPHISTRY), increasing human false-positive rates on QA and code. (also: [[Topic - preference learning]], [[Topic - judge and decision-model audits]])
- [[2024-11 Tulu 3 RLVR]] — Introduces Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR): reward is 1 only when a programmatic checker verifies the answer (math, precise instruction following). (also: [[Topic - alternative evaluation paradigms]])
- [[2025-01 DeepSeek-R1]] — Large-scale RL with rule-based accuracy and format rewards (no neural reward model) produces strong reasoning; authors chose rule-based rewards to avoid reward hacking of neural reward models. (also: [[Topic - trained judges and reward models]], [[Topic - alternative evaluation paradigms]])
- [[2025-03 Rewarding Doubt]] — Fine-tunes an LLM with PPO on a log-scoring-rule reward so that the 0-10 confidence it verbalizes next to each answer matches its actual probability of being right. (also: [[Topic - calibration]], [[Topic - open decision models]])
- [[2025-04 Does RL really incentivize reasoning capacity beyond the base model]] — RLVR improves pass@1 but base models achieve higher pass@k at large k, suggesting RLVR mostly sharpens sampling of existing solutions.

- [[2025-06 CURE Co-Evolving Coder and Unit Tester]] — RL framework that trains a code generator and a unit-test generator together from their interaction outcomes, without ground-truth code, so the tester learns from the coder's mistakes. (also: [[Topic - self-evolving tests]], [[Topic - trained judges and reward models]])
- [[2025-06 Spurious rewards rethinking training signals in RLVR]] — RLVR on Qwen2.5-Math improves MATH scores even with random or incorrect rewards, showing verifier signal is not the only driver and results are model-family dependent.
- [[2025-07 Beyond Binary Rewards RLCR]] — RLCR adds a Brier-score term to the binary correctness reward in RL reasoning training, so the model learns to state a calibrated confidence alongside its answer at no cost in accuracy. (also: [[Topic - calibration]])
- [[2025-07 One Token to Fool LLM-as-a-Judge]] — Reference-based generative reward models (used in RLVR) give positive rewards to 'master key' answers such as ':' or 'Thought process:' with no content. (also: [[Topic - judge and decision-model audits]], [[Topic - trained judges and reward models]])
- [[2025-07 Rubrics as Rewards]] — Uses per-prompt rubrics, aggregated into a reward by an LLM judge, for on-policy RL where no verifier exists. (also: [[Topic - LLM and agent judges]])
- [[2026-01 Calibration-Aware RL for Decision-Making LLMs]] — Shows that RL with verifiable rewards leaves reasoning classifiers near-certain on every verdict because the final decision token only copies the reasoning's conclusion, and fixes it by training that one token with a cross-entropy loss toward one-hot when right and uniform when wrong. (also: [[Topic - calibration]], [[Topic - open decision models]])
- [[2026-03 Code-A1]] — Two separate models trained adversarially - a Code LLM rewarded for passing tests and a Test LLM rewarded for exposing defects - with a shared Mistake Book for replay. (also: [[Topic - self-evolving tests]])
- [[2026-09 LLM-as-a-Judge Is Not an Oracle (PROCTOR)]] — Production experience report: LLM-judged self-improvement loops failed in 11 ways; proposes PROCTOR, a Teacher-Student loop with five deterministic guardrails the judge cannot override. (also: [[Topic - judge and decision-model audits]], [[Topic - alternative evaluation paradigms]])
- [[2026-09 OpenJev-RLCD]] — An open recipe for "reinforcement learning for calibrated decisions": it scores the answer distribution a reasoning model reads out after each of its own rationales with a proper scoring rule, calibrating that readout first and then reinforcing the reasoning with the same score. (also: [[Topic - decision models]], [[Topic - open decision models]], [[Topic - calibration]])
- [[2026-10 Judging the Judges Rubrics as Rewards]] — RaRE ensembles five rubric judges with logprob-weighted soft-Likert rewards and drops high-disagreement rollouts from GRPO updates. (also: [[Topic - LLM and agent judges]], [[Topic - social choice]])
