# PACT: Pairwise-Anchored Calibrated Tuning for Single-Token Typed Decisions

## One line
Replaces plain cross-entropy in fine-tuning a single-token typed-decision model (Nimble-style, one logit read per allowed answer code) with four loss terms drawn from the contrastive-pair structure already in its training data; it lowers position bias and rubric error and steadies training, without raising accuracy.

## Method
Problem: these models answer a schema field (choice, boolean, ordinal score) from one logit per answer code. Their training data comes as contrastive pairs whose one edited sentence (at most 8 words) flips the answer, each certified so that deleting that sentence makes the fact "unknown". Cross-entropy sees records alone, so pairing, certificate and letter arbitrariness never reach the loss.

PACT adds four terms, none needing new labels:
- **Difference-in-differences margin**: d = how much the y_a-vs-y_b log-odds moves between the two pair members, hinged at m = 2 nats. Any logit offset shared by the pair cancels (Proposition 1), so a topic prior cannot lower it; only reacting to the edited fact can.
- **Permutation consistency**: Jensen-Shannon divergence between answer distributions under two relabellings of the codes. Bounded and label-free; it fixes position bias inside a single pass instead of averaging K permutations at inference.
- **Evidence necessity**: on the context with the decisive sentence deleted, it pushes the logits of y_a and y_b toward equality and is silent on other answers: the certificate supports indifference, not a label.
- **Ordinal transport**: squared earth-mover distance on rubric CDFs, so near misses cost less.

Serving adds a three-parameter temperature T = softplus(a + b log C + c log(L/1000)), conditioned on the number of choices and the prompt length. It never changes an argmax; serving is otherwise unchanged.

## Evaluated on
- No accuracy gain over the published recipe: 84.6% vs 85.2% on a 324-item holdout, McNemar p >= 0.50 at every seed.
- Lowest position bias of all runs: flip rate under relabelling 9.8% vs 13.8% (Nimble), total variation 5.6% vs 6.9%. It is the only arm that gains nothing from 4-permutation ensembling (-0.7 pp vs +2.4 pp for CE-only).
- Lowest rubric error: Score MAE 0.232 vs 0.311. Score accuracy is +6.2 to +15.6 pp over Nimble at every seed, offset by losses on Choice (-3.4 to -4.8 pp) and Boolean fields.
- Stabilises an aggressive optimiser. Against a CE-only control with the same schedule: +2.4 pp accuracy (significant at 2 of 3 seeds), seed standard deviation 6.0 to 2.8 pp, NLL -26%, ECE -25%.
- The necessity term carries the most weight. Removing it costs 3.1 pp accuracy and 8.0 pp pair accuracy, and raises TV from 5.0% to 11.7%.

## Limitations admitted by the authors
- The pair margin, the paper's motivating idea (H1), is inconclusive. Removing it lowers pair accuracy by only 1.2 pp at K=1, and the direction reverses at K=4.
- The dominant failure survives training: in 24% of holdout pairs (33% for Choice) the model gives the same answer to both members, ignoring the edited fact. That is 74% of imperfect pairs. The margin is met on training pairs but does not transfer to new source families.
- Calibration is worse than Nimble (ECE after temperature 7.9% vs 5.4%); at seed 17 every term removal improves raw NLL and ECE.
- The temperature's shape is unidentified. Across runs the log C slope ranges from -1.60 to +2.73 on 226 calibration records, so only its overall level transfers. It also worsens selective risk (AURC 3.95 to 4.21).
- Ablations are single-seed, against a weak reference draw: per-term claims are directional.
- The holdout is 324 synthetic, model-labelled items from 6 families, so errors shared with the training labels cannot be detected. A wrong certificate teaches indifference where evidence actually decides.
- One 9B model, one LoRA rank; about 5x the training cost (1.37 h vs 0.26 h).

## What it leaves untried
- Attacking the ignored-edit failure directly, with margin schedules, harder negative edits or pair-level augmentation. Named by the authors, untested, and the main remaining error.
- Tuned loss weights: they were fixed a priori, so "no term raises accuracy" holds only for this weighting.
- Human-labelled or out-of-family evaluation, to rule out artefacts of model-generated labels.
