# Optima: Optimizing Effectiveness and Efficiency for LLM-Based Multi-Agent System

## One line
Fine-tunes the agents of a two-agent LLM system with an iterative generate, rank, select, train loop whose reward trades off task score, token count and fluency, so the agents learn to talk with far fewer tokens and score higher.

## Method
Problem: multi-agent LLM systems talk verbosely (alignment training gives a length bias), which inflates token cost, and nobody had a parameter-updating method that optimises the system as a whole rather than evolving prompts or memory.

Idea: treat the conversation as the thing to train. For each task, sample several conversations, score each with one reward, keep the good ones, fine-tune, and repeat. The reward is task metric minus a weighted normalised token count (against the longest sample for that task) plus a weighted term from the base model's language-modelling loss on the conversation. The token term pushes for brevity. The loss term keeps messages probable under the original model, so they stay readable instead of collapsing into private shorthand, the usual outcome of emergent-communication RL.

Three pieces:
- Initialisation: sampled conversations all look alike even at high temperature, so exploration would be poor. The base model is prompted with randomly chosen response formats (JSON, list and so on), the best conversations are kept, the format instruction is stripped, and the model is fine-tuned on them. It then produces varied styles without being told to.
- Three training variants: iterative SFT on the best trajectory per task (iSFT); iterative DPO (iDPO); and an alternation of the two (iSFT-DPO). The authors reason that SFT relies on a single best trajectory, while DPO uses comparisons.
- MCTS-inspired DPO data: conversation turns are tree nodes. Nodes are expanded by reward-weighted sampling, 3 rollouts each, 8 rounds, giving 24 trajectories. Preference pairs come from nodes sharing a history whose estimated rewards differ by enough.

One model plays both agents. Setting: two agents, no tools, Llama 3 8B (3.2 3B in an appendix).

## Evaluated on
- Large gains in information-asymmetric QA with far fewer tokens: on 2WikiMultiHopQA, iSFT-DPO raises F1 by 2.8x using about 10% of multi-agent debate's tokens. It reaches 74.2 F1 against 25.9 for debate and 28.7 for 8-sample self-consistency.
- Debate tasks are mixed. ARC-C and MMLU improve with fewer tokens (MMLU 60.2 against 54.0 for self-consistency, at about 5% of its tokens). On MATH and GSM8k the variants match or trail self-consistency (MATH 30.1 against 35.7 for iSFT), though with much lower token use. The authors blame task difficulty and limited training data.
- Efficiency buys better inference scaling: GSM8k-trained iDPO matches chain-of-thought self-consistency with 88.5% fewer tokens, because the saved budget pays for more samples. Majority-vote accuracy plateaus while coverage (solved at least once) keeps rising logarithmically.
- Both reward terms matter. Dropping the token term multiplies tokens by 2.7x to 6.3x with small accuracy changes in most cells. Dropping the loss term gives terser output, but accuracy falls (iSFT-DPO loses 7.5 F1 on 2WMHQA); the authors say the messages become too thin and hallucination-prone.
- Some transfer: HotpotQA to 2WMHQA and TriviaQA, and MATH to GSM8k (close to GSM8k-trained models). Learning is two-phase: early iterations raise both score and tokens, later ones cut tokens while score keeps rising.

## Limitations admitted by the authors
- Mainly two agents sharing one model. Teams of 5 to 10 and mixed models are untested, and larger groups may bring coordination problems. Three-agent runs (appendix) look good but are preliminary.
- Transfer is limited to nearby domains. MATH to ARC-C was hard, and QA to math or code is unexplored, so each domain likely needs its own training.
- Only standard benchmarks. Real deployment constraints are not addressed.
- The conclusion names scaling to larger models as open. Only 3B and 8B models were used.

## What it leaves untried
- Heterogeneous agents with separate models per role. One shared model hides whether roles specialise their messages.
- Answer selection beyond majority vote. The coverage gap over vote accuracy is left unexploited, even though the authors note it.
- The hybrid's advantage over iSFT or iDPO is shown only as a trade-off in a table. No ablation explains why interleaving helps.
