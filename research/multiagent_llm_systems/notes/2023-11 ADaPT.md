# ADaPT: As-Needed Decomposition and Planning with Language Models

## One line
A recursive controller lets an LLM executor try a task directly and calls an LLM planner to split it into sub-tasks only when the executor reports failure, repeating at each level.

## Method
Two kinds of LLM agent have complementary weaknesses. Iterative executors (ReAct) choose each next action from the full action-observation history, and they break down as tasks get longer and more compositional. Plan-and-execute systems fix a plan up front. One sub-task the executor cannot do then sinks the whole task, and the planner cannot know in advance which sub-task will be hard (for example, a mug may be found at once or in an obscure place).

ADaPT combines them in a fixed, hand-written recursive program, the controller:
- The executor first attempts the (sub-)task. It ends its run by emitting "task completed" or "task failed", so success is judged by the LLM itself and not by the environment's reward.
- On "failed", the planner writes a short plan (typically 3 to 5 steps) with a logical operator. AND means all steps run in order. OR means alternatives, as in "find the mug on the countertop OR in the cabinets", and the later ones run only if earlier ones fail.
- The controller calls itself on each step, up to a depth limit d_max. The depth limit is the only stop rule besides success.

Plans are kept short and abstract on purpose, because a detailed 10-step plan made before exploring invites cascading wrong assumptions. The planner is only called where the executor has shown it needs help. The executor, planner and heuristic are all prompted LLMs, and the controller is the only enforced structure. Both roles can be different models, so a cheap executor can pair with a stronger planner.

## Evaluated on
With GPT-3.5 and a comparable LLM-call budget across methods:
- Beats iterative, plan-and-execute and retry baselines. Success on ALFWorld is 71.6 vs 43.3 for ReAct and for plan-and-execute, on WebShop 44 vs 32 and 17, and on TextCraft (a new Minecraft crafting game the authors built) 52 vs 19 and 27.
- Beats Reflexion by 14.1 points on ALFWorld (71.6 vs 57.5), 9 on WebShop and 20 on TextCraft, and beats the search method LATS by 6 points on WebShop (44 vs 38). The authors attribute this to retrying only the failed sub-task, where Reflexion repeats the whole trial.
- Depth matters. Raising d_max from 1 to 2 gives the largest gain on all three datasets, and 3 adds more. In ALFWorld, "pick two objects" tasks go from under 12% for baselines to 52.9%.
- Decomposition follows difficulty. On TextCraft recipes of depth 3, ReAct reaches 1.8% and ADaPT 38.7%, and the average decomposition depth used rises from 1.9 (depth-2 recipes) to 2.8 (depth-3 recipes) under the same budget.
- Helps weak executors and other models. An atomic-skills-only executor goes from 3.3% to 41.7%. GPT-4 gains up to 37 points and LLaMA-2 70B up to 15 on TextCraft. LLaMA-2-70B as executor with a GPT-3.5 planner rises from 20.4% to 43.3% on ALFWorld dev, which cuts cost since the planner is called rarely.

## Limitations admitted by the authors
- The whole method rests on the executor judging its own success. On ALFWorld and TextCraft it slightly over-estimates, and on WebShop, where a product can match a request partly, the self-assessed success is inflated by more than 30 points over the environment reward. ADaPT then stops although further decomposition was needed, which costs performance.
- Self-evaluation by LLMs is known to be unreliable (Huang et al.; Stechly et al.). The authors suggest external verifiers or calibration but do not test them. They say this blocks extension to non-decision tasks such as question answering.

## What it leaves untried
- Replacing the self-generated success signal with an external verifier or a calibrated confidence. The appendix shows it is the weak link on graded tasks like WebShop.
- Mixing stronger and weaker LLMs inside the recursion beyond one planner-executor pairing (deferred to future work). Cost savings from a rarely-called planner are shown only on ALFWorld dev.
- Tasks without an environment that checks actions, since only three interactive text environments are tested.
