# OWL: Optimized Workforce Learning for General Multi-Agent Assistance in Real-World Task Automation

## One line
A planner / coordinator / worker framework (Workforce) keeps planning domain-agnostic so that only the planner is fine-tuned (OWL: SFT then DPO), lifting a Qwen2.5-32B planner from 36.36% to 52.73% on GAIA.

## Method
The problem: multi-agent systems are built for one domain (MetaGPT's procedures are software-specific) and training them means tuning every agent (MALT's generator-verifier-refiner), so moving to a new domain means redesign and retraining.

Workforce splits the system into three parts. A Planner decomposes the task according to a registry of what the workers can do. A Coordinator assigns each subtask to a worker and tracks dependencies. Worker nodes (a web agent, a document and multimodal agent, a reasoning and coding agent) run subtasks with their own tools. All traffic goes through one shared task channel: workers post only final results, and their tool-call traces stay inside the subtask. Each worker therefore sees only its subtask and the concise results of the subtasks it depends on. A new domain is handled by adding or swapping workers while the planner stays unchanged.

Replanning is the only feedback loop. A worker judges for itself whether its subtask failed. On failure the channel posts the reason and the planner writes new subtasks. The default limit is 2 replans.

OWL trains only the planner, on the argument "stable core, variable periphery". Stage one is SFT on 1,599 filtered trajectories that Workforce produced with GPT-4o-mini on HotpotQA, WikiTableQuestions, math problems and Infinity-MM, with no GAIA data. Stage two is DPO on 1,009 pairs, built from 4 rollouts per question, with correct answers as "chosen" and incorrect ones as "rejected". The curriculum was chosen to cover different capabilities (multi-hop search, tables, math, multimodal) so that the planner learns decomposition rather than domain facts. The paper gives this as design intent and has no ablation of the curriculum.

## Evaluated on
- Workforce with Claude-3.7-Sonnet scores 69.70% on GAIA validation, above OpenAI Deep Research (67.36% in Table 1; the text says the margin is 2.34 points over a 55.15% figure, which does not match the table). It also beats every open-source framework listed.
- Under the same model and tools (GPT-4o), Workforce gets 60.61%, against 37.58% for a single agent and 54.55% for two-agent role playing. The structure itself contributes about 6 points over role playing.
- OWL lifts the Qwen2.5-32B planner from 36.36% to 52.73%, above GPT-4o-mini (47.27%) and Qwen2.5-72B (49.09%), but below GPT-4o (60.61%). It ties GPT-4o on Level 3 (26.92%).
- SFT alone reaches 41.21% and drops Level 3 by 3.85 points. Adding DPO gains 7.69 points on Level 3 over the base. The authors credit RL for generalization.
- Training only the planner scores 45.45%, training only workers 31.51%, and training both 46.68%. Planner-only training therefore captures nearly all of the gain.
- Filtering training trajectories helps: 52.73% filtered against 46.06% unfiltered after SFT+DPO.
- Replanning raises GPT-4o from 0.43 to 0.60 across 0, 1 and 2 replans, and Claude from 0.54 to 0.69, with no ground truth shown to the system. Role playing falls from 62.3% to 34.6% on tasks needing three or more capabilities, while Workforce stays level.

## Limitations admitted by the authors
- Performance depends on having good domain tools. About half the failures in the manual error analysis (52 failures, Claude run) come from tools (32.69%) or model limits (19.23%), which a better planner cannot fix.
- RL from real-world feedback is slow because online search adds latency.
- Planner errors are still the largest agent-side failure at 21.15% of failures, mostly entirely wrong decompositions (19.23%).

## What it leaves untried
- Cross-domain transfer is the central claim, yet every evaluation is on GAIA. The paper never swaps in workers for a different domain, so "plug and play" is not measured.
- Planner-only training was compared with worker-only and both-together, but only on GAIA. It was not compared with training the planner on in-domain data, so whether the curriculum's breadth is what produces transfer is untested.
- Workforce uses pass@3 for GPT-4o and pass@1 for Claude, and the text does not say how the baselines were sampled. A matched-sampling comparison is missing.
