# Topic - agent evaluation benchmarks

Benchmarks and harnesses for agents: task suites, environments, trajectory and step-level scoring, verifier failures, deployment-aware evaluation.

Group: Judging and evaluation. See [[Topics]]. Same group: [[Topic - LLM and agent judges]], [[Topic - judge and decision-model audits]], [[Topic - trained judges and reward models]], [[Topic - user simulation]], [[Topic - alternative evaluation paradigms]], [[Topic - self-evolving tests]], [[Topic - judges as reward signals]], [[Topic - stopping rules]].

## Papers (15)
- [[2023-09 Cooperation, Competition, and Maliciousness]] — A scorable six-party, five-issue negotiation game that measures how well LLM agents cooperate, compete and resist a greedy or sabotaging teammate. (also: [[Topic - reasoning prompting]], [[Topic - strategic play among LLM agents]])
- [[2024-02 Feedback Loops With Language Models Drive In-Context Reward Hacking]] — Shows that when an LLM's outputs or actions feed back into its next context, it optimizes its stated goal at test time and picks up harmful side effects on the way (in-context reward hacking), and that static evaluation misses this. (also: [[Topic - self-refinement and critique loops]], [[Topic - agent system failure analysis]])
- [[2024-02 How Well Can LLMs Negotiate]] — An open-source platform of three two-player negotiation games is used to rank four LLMs against each other and to show that persona prompts raise payoffs while anchoring and other irrational habits leave the agents exploitable. (also: [[Topic - strategic play among LLM agents]])
- [[2024-02 TDAG]] — A main agent splits a task into subtasks, rewrites the remaining subtasks as earlier ones succeed or fail, and has an LLM generate a tailored subagent (tool document plus retrieved skills) for each, alongside a travel-planning benchmark that gives partial credit. (also: [[Topic - orchestration and delegation]])
- [[2024-11 Magentic-One]] — An Orchestrator agent plans with two ledgers and directs four tool-specialist agents (web, files, code, terminal) to solve open-ended web and file tasks, reaching results statistically comparable to the best systems on three benchmarks without per-benchmark tuning of the team. (also: [[Topic - orchestration and delegation]])
- [[2026-02 MERIT Feedback Elicits Better Bargaining in LLM Negotiators]] — Builds a nine-market buyer-seller bargaining benchmark (AgoraBench), a human-preference-fitted score for buyers (MERIT), and shows that giving that score to an LLM buyer as a private reward in the prompt, or fine-tuning on human-preferred dialogues, improves bargaining. (also: [[Topic - strategic play among LLM agents]], [[Topic - preference learning]])
- [[2026-02 PieArena]] — A negotiation benchmark built from MBA-course scenarios that ranks 13 LLM agents by head-to-head play, anchors them against trained human negotiators, and profiles behaviour (lying, arithmetic, rule compliance) beyond the deal outcome. (also: [[Topic - strategic play among LLM agents]])
- [[2026-03 Beyond Task Completion]] — Scores agents on how they reach an outcome (policy, grounding, honesty of statements) and disqualifies "corrupt successes", finding that 27-78% of tau-bench successes hide violations. (also: [[Topic - LLM and agent judges]], [[Topic - agent system failure analysis]])
- [[2026-04 Detecting Multi-Agent Collusion Through Multi-Agent Interpretability]] — Frames collusion detection as a composite hypothesis test over per-agent activation probes (HELIOS) and tests it on a new benchmark, NarcBench, where probes catch collusion that transcript monitors miss but transfer poorly to emergent collusion. (also: [[Topic - strategic play among LLM agents]])
- [[2026-04 Evaluating LLM Agents on Automated Software Analysis Tasks]] — A benchmark of 35 tool-project setup tasks plus a staged single agent (AnalysisAgent) that reaches 94% manually verified success where the best existing agent reaches 77%. (also: [[Topic - LLM and agent judges]])
- [[2026-06 Hardening Agent Benchmarks with Adversarial Hacker-Fixer Loops]] — Three LLM agents (hacker, fixer, solver) take turns attacking and patching a benchmark's outcome verifier until it stops accepting shortcuts, replacing manual, task-by-task patching. (also: [[Topic - judges as reward signals]], [[Topic - role-based agent teams]])
- [[2026-06 Recognize Your Orchestrator]] — Models the entropy of an orchestrator's executor-scheduling distribution as a damped oscillation plus a logarithmic context-load term, fits it to 6 LLM orchestrators on a synthesized checkpoint benchmark, and finds that reasoning-heavy models make worse orchestrators. (also: [[Topic - orchestration and delegation]])
- [[2026-08 SwarmBench]] — A benchmark that scores an LLM only as the orchestrator of a dynamically spawned subagent swarm (decompose, delegate, aggregate), plus SwarmExp, which replays experience distilled from past trajectories into the orchestrator's context. (also: [[Topic - orchestration and delegation]])
- [[2026-09 Rethinking the Evaluation of Efficiency Methods for Multi-Agent Systems]] — A controlled benchmark re-tests five published methods that prune agents or communication edges to cut multi-agent cost, and finds many reported gains come from the setup, structural collapse or easy-to-prune starting systems, not from better structure. (also: [[Topic - communication topology and channels]], [[Topic - automated pipeline and topology search]])
- [[2026-09 The Unreliable Progress Bar]] — Measures whether LLMs can tell the runtime which lifecycle stage a task is in, at every stage, and finds that reports are reliable at some stages and collapse at others. (also: [[Topic - agent system failure analysis]])
