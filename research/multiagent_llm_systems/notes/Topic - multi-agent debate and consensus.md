# Topic - multi-agent debate and consensus

Several LLM agents, often from different models, exchange answers and explanations over rounds, revise their positions after seeing each other's, and settle on a team answer by vote, judge or convergence. The subject is what the exchange adds over one model or a plain ensemble: agent diversity, conformity and echo chambers, convergence, and when rounds stop helping. Debate as a way of producing an answer, not as a way of evaluating one.

## Papers (33)
- [[2023-09 ReConcile]] — Several different LLMs (ChatGPT, Bard, Claude2) discuss a reasoning question over up to three rounds, seeing each other's answers, explanations and stated confidence, and the team answer is a confidence-weighted vote. (also: [[Topic - social choice]])
- [[2023-10 A Dynamic LLM-Powered Agent Network]] — DyLAN first picks the most useful agents from a candidate pool with an unsupervised peer-rating score, then lets that team collaborate layer by layer, dropping weak agents and stopping early when two thirds agree. (also: [[Topic - role-based agent teams]], [[Topic - automated pipeline and topology search]])
- [[2023-10 Corex]] — Corex has five LLM agents solve a reasoning question in one of three collaboration modes (Discuss, Review, Retrieve), and with five agents it matches or beats self-consistency voting over ten chains at a fraction of the tokens. (also: [[Topic - self-refinement and critique loops]])
- [[2023-10 Exploring Collaboration Mechanisms for LLM Agents]] — Builds small "machine societies" of three ChatGPT agents with easy-going or overconfident personas and tests which order of debate and reflection rounds works best, finding that debate-first strategies win and that agents show human-like conformity. (also: [[Topic - self-refinement and critique loops]])
- [[2023-10 Let Models Speak Ciphers]] — CIPHER lets debating LLMs pass each other the probability-weighted average of vocabulary embeddings instead of a sampled token, so no belief is thrown away, and it beats natural-language debate by 0.5-5.0% with no weight changes. (also: [[Topic - communication topology and channels]])
- [[2023-12 Exchange-of-Thought]] — Three LLMs share their reasoning chains over several rounds, in one of four fixed communication layouts, and stop on a majority answer, with each model's confidence measured by how often its own answer changed. (also: [[Topic - communication topology and channels]])
- [[2024-02 Large Language Model based Multi-Agents]] — A survey that organises LLM-based multi-agent work along four axes (environment interface, agent profiling, communication, capability acquisition) and two application streams (problem solving, world simulation), then lists open challenges. (also: [[Topic - role-based agent teams]], [[Topic - strategic play among LLM agents]])
- [[2024-02 More Agents Is All You Need]] — Sampling the same LLM many times and taking a majority vote (Agent Forest) raises accuracy steadily with the number of samples, often matching or beating more elaborate prompting and multi-agent frameworks. (also: [[Topic - reasoning prompting]], [[Topic - communication topology and channels]])
- [[2024-02 Rethinking the Bounds of LLM Reasoning]] — Re-tests the claim that multi-agent discussion beats a single LLM on reasoning, and finds that a single agent with a strong prompt (a worked demonstration) matches it, while discussion helps mainly when no demonstration is given. (also: [[Topic - reasoning prompting]])
- [[2024-06 Croto]] — Croto runs several independent ChatDev-style agent teams on the same task, pauses them at key phases, merges their drafts into one better draft with an aggregator agent, and hands it back to every team. (also: [[Topic - role-based agent teams]])
- [[2024-06 Improving Multi-Agent Debate with Sparse Communication Topology]] — Letting each debating LLM agent see only a few peers' answers instead of all of them matches or beats fully-connected debate while cutting input-token cost by roughly 30 to 50%. (also: [[Topic - communication topology and channels]])
- [[2024-06 Mixture-of-Agents]] — Stacks several layers of different LLMs, each layer reading all answers from the layer before and synthesizing a better one, so a team of open-source models beats GPT-4o on AlpacaEval 2.0 with no fine-tuning. (also: [[Topic - role-based agent teams]], [[Topic - communication topology and channels]])
- [[2024-12 A Survey on LLM-based Multi-Agent System]] — A survey of 125 LLM multi-agent papers from 2023 and 2024 that sorts them by what the system is for (solving tasks, simulating scenarios, evaluating or training agents) and lists open challenges. (also: [[Topic - role-based agent teams]], [[Topic - communication topology and channels]])
- [[2024-12 Multi-LLM Debate]] — Models debate as Bayesian updating over latent concepts, which predicts that debate collapses to the majority view (including shared errors), and three interventions (diversity pruning, quality pruning, misconception refutation) are added to counter it.
- [[2025-01 Communicating Activations Between Language Model Agents]] — Two frozen LLMs communicate by overwriting one model's last-token hidden activation with another's mid-layer activation, replacing natural-language messages at under a quarter of the compute. (also: [[Topic - communication topology and channels]])
- [[2025-01 Do as We Do, Not as You Think]] — Measures how often an LLM drops a correct answer to match peer agents, using a new benchmark (BenchForm) built on Asch-style protocols, and tests two prompt fixes.
- [[2025-01 Multiagent Finetuning]] — Finetunes a society of copies of one base model, each on its own debate-generated data and in a generator or critic role, so that self-improvement keeps gaining over five rounds where single-model self-finetuning stalls after one. (also: [[Topic - self-refinement and critique loops]])
- [[2025-02 Rethinking Mixture-of-Agents]] — Shows that Mixture-of-Agents works better when the aggregator synthesizes several samples from the single best model (Self-MoA) than when it mixes different models, because proposer quality matters more than cross-model diversity.
- [[2025-02 Voting or Consensus]] — Holding everything else fixed, it compares seven ways a debating LLM team picks its final answer (four voting rules, three consensus thresholds) and proposes two drafting schemes that raise answer diversity. (also: [[Topic - social choice]])
- [[2025-07 Debating Truth]] — DebateCV has two opposed debater agents argue a claim over shared evidence while a moderator, fine-tuned on synthetic debates, issues the verdict and justification, beating single-agent and other multi-agent verifiers on AVeriTeC. (also: [[Topic - role-based agent teams]])
- [[2025-09 Peacemaker or Troublemaker]] — Defines and measures inter-agent sycophancy in multi-agent debate, then shows with persona-controlled agents that it drives debates to wrong consensus and that a mix of agreeable and contrarian debaters works best. (also: [[Topic - judge and decision-model audits]])
- [[2025-09 Talk Isn't Always Cheap]] — Shows empirically that multi-agent debate among mixed-capability LLMs often lowers accuracy below plain majority voting, because agents flip from correct to incorrect answers under peer pressure. (also: [[Topic - agent system failure analysis]])
- [[2025-09 Tree of Agents]] — Splits a long document across several agents that share summaries, then re-read each other's chunks in many different orders and vote, so an 8B model comes close to much larger long-context models on long-document QA. (also: [[Topic - communication topology and channels]])
- [[2025-10 Thought Communication in Multiagent Collaboration]] — Agents in a debate stop exchanging only text: a sparse autoencoder pulls latent "thoughts" out of every agent's hidden state, and each agent receives the thoughts relevant to it as a learned prefix, which lifts MATH and GSM8K accuracy and agreement over a multiagent-finetuning baseline. (also: [[Topic - communication topology and channels]])
- [[2026-01 Tool-MAD]] — Two debating LLM agents, one with a Wikipedia RAG tool and one with a live web search API, re-query after every round and fall back on a judge that sees RAGAS faithfulness and answer-relevance scores when they do not agree.
- [[2026-04 From Debate to Decision]] — A post-hoc layer pools the verbalized probabilities of debating LLM agents and applies split conformal prediction, so a singleton answer is acted on and a larger candidate set goes to a human. (also: [[Topic - confidence-gated escalation]], [[Topic - calibration]], [[Topic - social choice]])
- [[2026-04 Single-Agent LLMs Outperform Multi-Agent Systems on Multi-Hop Reasoning Under Equal Thinking Token Budgets]] — Once the thinking-token budget is held equal, a single agent matches or beats five multi-agent designs on multi-hop questions, and an information-theoretic argument says why and when that flips. (also: [[Topic - communication topology and channels]])
- [[2026-05 Sequential Consensus for Multi-Agent LLM Debates]] — Wraps a multi-agent debate in Wald's sequential probability ratio test on a judge's per-round consensus score, so the debate stops when the score is informative enough, and the calibration of that score tells you whether stopping can be trusted at all. (also: [[Topic - stopping rules]])
- [[2026-05 The Cost of Consensus]] — Teams of ten identical 7-8B models that debate and plurality-vote do no better than the same models revising their own answers alone, while using 2.1 to 3.4 times the tokens. (also: [[Topic - self-refinement and critique loops]], [[Topic - communication topology and channels]])
- [[2026-06 Not All Flips Are Conformity]] — Adds a no-peer control to multi-agent debate and shows that answer changes and convergence are not evidence that peers helped, because many flips happen without peers and peer adoption can correct, corrupt, or leave an answer wrong.
- [[2026-08 DEAR]] — DEAR trains two small reinforcement-learning agents that decide, after each debate round, which peers each LLM may read and how randomly it samples, using the agreement between answers as the signal, to curb blind conformity and save tokens. (also: [[Topic - automated pipeline and topology search]], [[Topic - communication topology and channels]])
- [[2026-08 Emergence of Biased Consensus in Multi-Agent LLM Debates]] — Models a debate among LLM agents as a noisy spin system (Ising/Potts) and shows that a small single-model bias locks the group into a biased consensus once conformity times group size, divided by sampling temperature, passes a critical value. (also: [[Topic - communication topology and channels]])
- [[2026-09 Remember and Reweight]] — R2-MAD gives each debating agent a memory of past debates, used to correct its biased prior and to down-weight peers whose stance has historically been wrong, so that a wrong majority is less able to talk a correct agent out of its answer.
