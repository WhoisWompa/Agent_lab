# Agent S2: A Compositional Generalist-Specialist Framework for Computer Use Agents

## One line
A screenshot-only computer-use agent in which a generalist Manager replans after every subgoal and a Worker routes each action to a specialist grounding expert, reaching state of the art on OSWorld, WindowsAgentArena and AndroidWorld.

## Method
The paper attacks three weaknesses of computer-use agents: imprecise mapping of a text description to pixel coordinates, brittle long-horizon planning amid pop-ups and changing screens, and one generalist model doing planning, action and grounding at once. Fine-tuning one model for all of it also tends to cost broader ability, and the datasets are expensive.

The answer is composition. A Manager (generalist LLM) splits the instruction into subgoals. A Worker (generalist LLM) takes the top subgoal and emits one atomic action at a time, each with a language description of its target. It stops the subgoal with SUCCESS or FAILURE and hands control back.

Two ideas are new:

- **Mixture of Grounding.** The Worker acts like the gate in a mixture of experts and sends each action to one of three experts. The visual expert (UI-TARS-72B-DPO) turns a description and screenshot into coordinates. The textual expert runs OCR on the first and last phrases of a span, to hit word edges that visual models miss. The structural expert writes a cell-to-value dictionary straight into a spreadsheet through a programmatic interface, because cells resize and scroll. The Worker can reword its description to retry, or switch expert.
- **Proactive Hierarchical Planning.** Reactive planners revise only after a failure. Here the Manager re-reads the original instruction, the previous subgoals and the newest screenshot after every completed subgoal, and rewrites the remaining list. The Worker also re-chooses its expert at every action. The argument is that fresh observations and the earlier subgoals give context to fix small earlier errors and ignore UI noise.

The agent keeps the Agent S knowledge base of task experience and web knowledge. Everything runs from screenshots, with no accessibility tree.

## Evaluated on
- New state of the art on OSWorld: 27.0% at 15 steps and 34.5% at 50 steps with Claude-3.7-Sonnet, against 22.7% and 32.6% for the best baselines (UI-TARS-72B-DPO at 15 steps, OpenAI CUA at 50). With Claude-3.5-Sonnet the agent gets 24.5% and 33.7%, above Claude Computer Use on the newer Claude-3.7-Sonnet (15.5% and 26.0%). This is the claim for modular over monolithic.
- Generalizes to other systems: 29.8% on WindowsAgentArena against 19.5% for NAVI (+52.8% relative), and 54.3% on AndroidWorld against 46.6% for UI-TARS-72B-SFT (+16.5% relative). On AndroidWorld the agent runs Worker-only.
- Mixture of Grounding helps most at long horizons, on a 65-task OSWorld subset: 27.69% to 30.77% at 15 steps, 33.85% to 38.46% at 50. Removing the textual expert lowers subtask success from 70.6% to 65.2%, and removing the structural expert lowers it from 73.7% to 69.4%.
- Proactive over reactive planning: +4.62 points at 15 steps and +6.15 at 50 on the same subset.
- Small specialists inside the framework beat a large generalist as grounder: UI-TARS-7B-DPO reaches 29.23% and UGround-V1-7B 24.61%, against 24.61% for Claude-3.7-Sonnet itself as grounder (the 7B UGround ties it).
- Of 29 tasks failed at 15 steps but solved at 50, 34.5% came from adaptive navigation and 31.0% from adaptive interaction, which the authors read as test-time self-correction.

## Limitations admitted by the authors
- Planning is now the main bottleneck: 41.0% of failures are planning, 20.5% grounding, 17.9% interaction, 10.3% navigation, 10.3% infeasible tasks the agent fails to flag. Grounding is no longer the worst problem, but the Manager's plans are still often inadequate because of noisy subtask information or misalignment with the task.
- Interaction and navigation errors show the Worker lacks GUI domain knowledge and layout understanding.
- The agent cannot predict that a task is infeasible, which accounts for 10.3% of failures.
- The gap to humans remains large: the intro cites a gap of about 40 points on OSWorld.
- Gains rely on longer runs (step limits of 15 and 50), which the authors treat as extra compute at test time.

## What it leaves untried
- No ablation of the generalist-specialist split beyond grounding. The Manager and Worker are always generalists, and the claim that composing "slightly suboptimal" parts beats the best monolith is shown only for the grounding expert.
- The ablations use a 65-task OSWorld subset with no variance reported, so the 3 to 6 point gaps are only suggestive. The text offers no repeated runs.
- No fix for the leading failure, planning, such as a plan verifier or a feasibility check for impossible tasks.
