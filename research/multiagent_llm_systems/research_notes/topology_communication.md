# Topology and communication structure as the design object in LLM multi-agent systems (2023 to Oct 2026)

Scope: papers where the structure of the multi-agent system is what gets designed or searched: who talks to whom, what passes over each edge, how many agents, which roles, and whole-workflow search. Role-based teams, orchestrator-worker, generator-critic loops, debate, failure surveys and production frameworks are other researchers' scope and get pointers only.

Conventions used below:
- "Venue" comes from the arXiv comment field or the proceedings page where one was found in this run. Where a venue is marked "(not verified here)", it comes from prior knowledge and no source fetched in this run confirmed it.
- PDF URL is always `https://arxiv.org/pdf/<id>`. All listed PDFs were downloaded to `research/multiagent_llm_systems/pdfs/<id>.pdf` unless a failure is noted under "Download log" in the last Gaps section.
- Coding of each entry: **DV** = design variable; **Opt** = how it is optimised and against what; **Edge** = what flows on edges and what is stripped; **Code vs prompt** = what is enforced in code and what is only asked for in prompts; **Comm** = findings on more or less communication; **Limits**; **Numbers**.

## Catalogue: what each paper designs, how, and what it found

### Takeaway
The field moved through three phases. In 2023-24 the graph was searched or learned with a fixed agent pool (GPTSwarm, DyLAN, AgentPrune, G-Designer, MacNet). In late 2024-25 the whole workflow became code searched by an LLM (ADAS, AFlow, MASS, MaAS, EvoFlow, MAS-GPT, FlowReasoner, MAS-Zero). In 2025-26 two branches grew: RL-trained, query-adaptive topology generators (ARG-Designer, AgentConductor, Graph-GRPO, Agent Q-Mix, HieraMAS, TopoDIM), and non-text channels (CIPHER, activation communication, KVComm, C2C, LatentMAS, Interlat). A 2026 controlled re-evaluation (EMNLP 2026) finds that many reported pruning gains do not survive a matched random-pruning control.

### Cited Findings

#### A. Graph and topology optimisation over a fixed agent pool

**GPTSwarm: "Language Agents as Optimizable Graphs"**. Zhuge, Wang, Kirsch, Faccio, Khizbullin, Schmidhuber et al. arXiv 2402.16823 (Feb 2024). Venue: ICML 2024; the arXiv comment begins "In For[ty-first ICML]", truncated, so this is partly verified. PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.16823 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16823)
- DV: agents as computational graphs. Nodes are LLM or tool operations, edges are information flow, and composite graphs connect operations across agents. Two optimisers: node optimisation (prompts) and edge optimisation (connectivity) — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16823)
- Opt: each potential edge gets a probability θᵢ. DAGs are sampled (edges that would create cycles are skipped) and the expected utility E[u(G′)] is maximised by REINFORCE over M sampled graphs (Adam). Node prompts are rewritten by an LLM improver from each node's (input, output) history — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.16823v3)
- Edge: natural-language strings. A node's output becomes context for its successors. In Mini Crosswords the passed items are candidate board solutions — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.16823v3)
- Comm/sparsity: on Mini Crosswords the learned distribution averaged about 32.76 ± 1.93 edges, nearly the same as a uniform θ=12.5% graph (32.80). Gains are therefore not from more edges, but the total number of potential edges is not stated. In the 2T2A case study, disconnected agents are pruned — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.16823v3)
- Numbers: Mini Crosswords (GPT-3.5) 0.575 optimised vs 0.465 initial vs 0.510 uniform graph. GPT-4-Turbo 0.800 vs Tree-of-Thought 0.675. HumanEval 0.76 to 0.88. GAIA average 18.45 vs GPT-4-Turbo 9.70 and AutoGPT 4.85. MMLU with adversarial agents: the optimised swarm "aligns asymptotically" with the single-agent baseline, with no numeric recovery figure given — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.16823v3)
- Limits: only inter-agent edges are optimised (intra-agent topology fixed), graphs are restricted to DAGs, utility is non-differentiable, and scaling past about 100 agents is called a significant challenge. DyLAN beat it on MMLU by 0.0065 at higher cost — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.16823v3)

**DyLAN: "A Dynamic LLM-Powered Agent Network for Task-Oriented Agent Collaboration"**. Liu, Zhang, Li, Liu, Yang. arXiv 2310.02170 (Oct 2023). Venue: COLM 2024 (arXiv comment). PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.02170 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02170)
- DV: the agent team (which candidates are kept) and a dynamic, query-dependent communication structure. Two stages: Team Optimization, then Task Solving — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02170)
- Opt: an unsupervised "Agent Importance Score" computed from contributions in a preliminary trial selects the team. No gradient is used; selection is a heuristic — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02170)
- Numbers: on specific MMLU subjects, team selection improves accuracy by up to 25.0% — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02170). G-Designer measured DyLAN at 2.2×10⁷ tokens on GSM8K vs 9.8×10⁶ for a complete graph — [G-Designer HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.11782v3). AgentPrune measured DyLAN at 2.4 to 5.3× the tokens of a random graph — [AgentPrune HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02506v1)

**AgentPrune: "Cut the Crap: An Economical Communication Pipeline for LLM-based Multi-Agent Systems"**. Guibin Zhang, Yanwei Yue, Zhixun Li et al. arXiv 2410.02506 (Oct 2024). Venue: ICLR 2025 ([ICLR proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/bbc461518c59a2a8d64e70e2c38c4a0e-Abstract-Conference.html)). PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.02506
- DV: binary masks on spatial edges (within a round) and temporal edges (one round's output carried into the next) of a spatial-temporal message-passing graph. Definition 1, "communication redundancy": a subgraph with a subset of spatial and temporal edges that reaches utility at least as high as the full graph — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02506v1)
- Opt: continuous masks are learned for a few rounds with a nuclear-norm (low-rank) surrogate under a Frobenius constraint. Then one-shot magnitude TopK pruning gives binary masks, and the pruned graph is fixed for the remaining queries — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02506v1)
- Edge: whole text messages are either kept or dropped, with no compression of their content. Pruning works on edges, not on message content — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02506v1)
- Comm: in a preliminary test, randomly removing 10 to 30% of connections in a mesh and in LLM-Debate on MMLU gained up to 2.83% accuracy. Removing communication helped even at random — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02506v1)
- Numbers: comparable results to SOTA topologies at $5.6 vs $43.7. Token reduction 28.1 to 72.8% across frameworks. Robustness against two agent-based attacks improves by 3.5 to 10.8% — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02506). Prompt tokens with AgentPrune: AutoGen MMLU 486,034 to 349,583; GPTSwarm MMLU 3,055,230 to 990,312; GPTSwarm GSM8K 14,005,945 to 3,526,035 — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02506v1)
- Limits: suits systems with more than three agents. The first K′ optimisation rounds cost extra tokens. Chains and direct-output structures are too simple to benefit — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02506v1)

**G-Designer: "Architecting Multi-agent Communication Topologies via Graph Neural Networks"**. Guibin Zhang, Yanwei Yue, Xiangguo Sun et al. arXiv 2410.11782 (Oct 2024). Venue: ICML 2025 (not verified here; the HTML keywords list "ICML"). PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.11782
- DV: a directed communication graph, decoded per query by a variational graph auto-encoder over agent embeddings plus a task-specific virtual node — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.11782)
- Opt: L = L_utility (REINFORCE, M=10 sampled graphs) + L_anchor (stay near a predefined chain anchor) + L_sparse (nuclear norm). Trained on only 40 or 80 queries — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.11782v3)
- Edge: each agent's prompt holds the query plus its in-neighbours' full responses. After K=3 rounds a summariser agent aggregates the outputs — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.11782v3)
- Numbers: MMLU 84.50%, HumanEval 89.90%. Up to 95.33% fewer tokens on HumanEval. Only 0.3% accuracy drop under an agent attack — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.11782). GSM8K total tokens: G-Designer 8.5×10⁶ vs complete graph 9.8×10⁶, GPTSwarm 1.4×10⁷, DyLAN 2.2×10⁷. With 20 agents on MMLU: complete graph about 5.6×10⁶ prompt tokens, GPTSwarm about 3.0×10⁷, G-Designer about 1.9×10⁶. Under a system-prompt attack on one of five agents, chain and complete graph drop by up to 11.0% while G-Designer goes from 84.5 to 84.2 — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.11782v3)
- Limits: no edge-density numbers are reported. The robustness mechanism (the encoder spots malicious nodes and cuts their edges) is asserted with little direct evidence — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.11782v3)

**AgentDropout: "Dynamic Agent Elimination for Token-Efficient and High-Performance LLM-Based Multi-Agent Collaboration"**. Zhexuan Wang, Yutong Wang, Xuebo Liu et al. arXiv 2503.18891 (Mar 2025). Venue: ACL 2025 (not verified here). PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.18891
- DV: node and edge removal per communication round, done by optimising the adjacency matrices. Numbers: 21.6% fewer prompt tokens, 18.4% fewer completion tokens, and +1.14 performance on average — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18891)

**Adaptive Graph Pruning (AGP)**. Boyi Li, Zhonghan Zhao, Der-Horng Lee et al. arXiv 2506.02951 (Jun 2025). Venue: ECAI 2025 (arXiv comment). PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.02951
- DV: agent count ("hard pruning") and edges ("soft pruning"), optimised jointly inside a maximum complete graph in two training stages. Numbers: +2.58 to 9.84% performance, more than 90% fewer tokens, and it beats baselines after about 10 training steps — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.02951)

**SafeSieve**. Ruijia Zhang, Xinyan Zhao, Ruixiang Wang et al. arXiv 2508.11733 (Aug 2025). Venue: AAAI 2026 poster (arXiv comment). PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.11733
- DV: edges, pruned progressively. LLM semantic evaluation sets the starting point, accumulated performance feedback refines it, and 0-extension clustering keeps coherent agent groups instead of greedy Top-k cuts. Numbers: 94.01% average accuracy, 12.4 to 27.8% fewer tokens, 1.23% average drop under prompt injection, 13.3% lower deployment cost in heterogeneous settings — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11733)

**"Improving Multi-Agent Debate with Sparse Communication Topology"**. Yunxuan Li, Yibing Du, Jiageng Zhang et al. arXiv 2406.11776 (Jun 2024). Venue: EMNLP 2024 Findings (not verified here). PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.11776. Debate is another researcher's area; this entry is here because connectivity is the variable.
- DV: hand-set connectivity, from fully connected to sparse. Finding: sparse topologies give "comparable or superior performance while significantly reducing computational costs" on GPT and Mistral models — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11776)

**MacNet: "Scaling Large Language Model-based Multi-Agent Collaboration"**. Chen Qian, Zihao Xie, YiFei Wang et al. arXiv 2406.07155 (Jun 2024). Venue: ICLR 2025 (arXiv comment). PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.07155
- DV: DAG shape (chain, star, tree, mesh, layered/MLP, random) and agent count, from 2⁰ to 2⁶ nodes (more than 1,000 agents in mesh). Hand-designed families, no search — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07155v3)
- Edge: nodes are "actors" that produce artifacts, and edges are "critics" that give instructions. **Only the refined artifact is passed to the next node, not the dialogue.** Long-term memory keeps only the final artifact, so non-artifact reasoning is hidden from downstream agents. This memory control makes context growth O(n) instead of O(n²) — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07155v3)
- Comm: denser is better on average (mesh > tree > chain), but the densest graph is not always best. **Irregular random topologies scored highest on average (0.6522 vs mesh 0.6316)** and used about 51.92% less time than mesh. At equal density, wider (star) beats deeper (tree). Divergent beats convergent, and reversing edges hurts — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07155v3)
- Scaling: logistic growth with agent count. Most topologies saturate around 100 agents, and 2⁴ is called "a reasonable choice". Majority voting adds about 0.9% and plateaus near 8 agents. AgentVerse suffers context explosion past about 30 agents — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07155v3)
- Limits: GPT-3.5 by default, three exchange rounds per edge, and a fit that covers only network size. Convergent nodes are hard to aggregate — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07155v3)

**"Exploring Collaboration Mechanisms for LLM Agents: A Social Psychology View"**. Jintian Zhang, Xin Xu, Ningyu Zhang et al. arXiv 2310.02124 (Oct 2023). Venue: ACL 2024 main (arXiv comment). PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.02124
- DV: agent traits (easy-going or overconfident) and the per-round thinking pattern (debate p₀ or reflection p₁), hand-set grid — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02124)
- Comm: the order of strategies matters a lot (MMLU society S4: p₀p₀p₁ 65.2% vs p₁p₀p₀ 34.4%). Reflection-heavy strategies are cheaper (about 1,976 vs 4,364 tokens per strategy on MMLU). 3 agents is optimal and odd counts beat even. Going from 3 to 10 rounds gives no clear gain. Conformity grows with rounds; it helps ChatGPT and Qwen-72B but hurts LLaMA2 and Mixtral. Mixing patterns within a round hurts — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.02124v3)

#### B. Whole-workflow and architecture search

**ADAS / Meta Agent Search: "Automated Design of Agentic Systems"**. Shengran Hu, Cong Lu, Jeff Clune. arXiv 2408.08435 (Aug 2024). Venue: ICLR 2025 (not verified here). PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.08435
- DV: the whole agent as a Python `forward()` function (prompts, tools, control flow), on a framework of about 100 lines — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.08435v2)
- Opt: a GPT-4 meta agent writes new agents from a growing archive, and they are evaluated with GPT-3.5. Objective: validation accuracy or F1 only. Cost and latency are not optimised, and multi-objective search is named as future work — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.08435v2)
- Numbers: DROP F1 79.4 vs 65.8, MGSM 53.4 vs 39.0, transfer +25.9% on GSM8K and +13.2% on GSM-Hard, ARC 13.7 vs 8.0 (GPT-3.5). Gains are small on MMLU (69.6 vs 67.6) and GPQA (34.6 vs 32.9) — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.08435v2)
- Limits: single-step QA only, single objective, small ARC subset (20 validation, 60 test) — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.08435v2). AFlow re-measured ADAS at an average of 67.2, below the best manual baseline (CoT-SC, 76.0) on GPT-4o-mini — [AFlow HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10762v4)

**AFlow: "Automating Agentic Workflow Generation"**. Jiayi Zhang, Jinyu Xiang, Zhaoyang Yu et al. arXiv 2410.10762 (Oct 2024). Venue: ICLR 2025 (not verified here). PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.10762
- DV: a code-represented workflow. Nodes are LLM calls, edges are Python control flow, and optional human-designed operators are available (Generate, Format, Review & Revise, Ensemble, Test, Programmer, Custom) — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10762v4)
- Opt: MCTS where each tree node is a complete workflow, LLM-driven expansion (Claude-3.5-Sonnet optimiser), 5 validation runs, 20 rounds. Objective: task score only. Cost is tracked for Pareto fronts but is not in the objective — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10762v4)
- Edge: Python variables, for example a list of candidate solutions passed to an ensemble, then the best solution passed to a test step — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10762v4)
- Numbers (GPT-4o-mini): average 80.3 vs best manual 76.0 vs ADAS 67.2. MBPP 83.4 vs 73.6. Smaller models beat GPT-4o on some tasks at 4.55% of its dollar cost — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10762v4); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10762)
- Limits: reasoning tasks with numeric evaluators only. Workflows are not fully model-agnostic (a DeepSeek-found workflow was weaker on GPT-4o-mini) — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10762v4)

**MASS: "Multi-Agent Design: Optimizing Agents with Better Prompts and Topologies"**. Han Zhou, Xingchen Wan, Ruoxi Sun et al. (Google). arXiv 2502.02533 (Feb 2025). Venue: ICLR 2026 (arXiv comment). PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.02533
- DV: per-block prompts plus a workflow topology built from building blocks (aggregate, reflect, debate, summarise, tool use), then workflow-level prompts — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02533)
- Opt: three interleaved stages: (1) block-level prompt optimisation, (2) topology search over a space pruned by each block's measured influence, (3) workflow-level joint prompt optimisation — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02533)
- Comm: **most topologies give little or no gain, or hurt.** On HotpotQA only debate helped (about +3%). Beneficial topologies are "a small fraction" of the space. Prompt-optimised single agents scale better per token than self-consistency, self-refine or debate — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02533v2)
- Numbers (8-task average, Gemini 1.5 Pro): block-level prompt optimisation 74.56 vs single-agent APO 67.44, plus topology 77.55, MASS 78.79. Baselines: ADAS 69.72, debate 70.26. Claude 3.5 Sonnet: 72.43 vs CoT 60.21 — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02533v2)
- Sparsity: the found designs are not sparse (for example nine parallel agents on MATH). The authors list the fully connected debate block and sparse communication as future work — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02533v2)
- Principles stated: optimise individual agents before composing them, build only from influential topologies, then jointly optimise — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02533v2)

**MaAS: "Multi-agent Architecture Search via Agentic Supernet"**. Guibin Zhang, Luyang Niu, Junfeng Fang et al. arXiv 2502.04180 (Feb 2025). Venue: ICML 2025 (not verified here; the HTML keywords list "ICML"). PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.04180
- DV: a probabilistic supernet over layered operators (CoT, LLM-Debate, Self-Consistency, Self-Refine, Ensemble, Testing, ReAct, Early-exit). A controller samples a query-specific architecture — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.04180v2)
- Opt: maximise expected utility minus λ·token-cost (λ in {1e-3, 5e-3, 1e-2}). The distribution is updated with Monte Carlo gradients and operators with LLM "textual gradients". **The objective includes cost explicitly**, unlike ADAS and AFlow — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.04180v2)
- Comm: easy queries exit at layer 2 (exit probability 0.37 to 0.47), and harder queries sample deeper architectures — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.04180v2)
- Numbers: 6 to 45% of the inference cost of existing systems, +0.54 to 11.82% accuracy — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04180). On MATH vs AFlow: training $3.38 vs $22.50, inference $0.42 vs $1.66, accuracy 51.82 vs 51.28. GAIA 20.69 vs AFlow 8.00 — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.04180v2)
- Limits: the paper is internally inconsistent on benchmark count (six or seven), and the abstract versions differ (11.82% vs 16.89%) — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.04180v2)

**EvoAgent**. Siyu Yuan, Kaitao Song, Jiangjie Chen et al. arXiv 2406.14228 (Jun 2024). Venue: NAACL 2025 main (arXiv comment). PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.14228
- DV: agent settings (role, prompts) used to expand one specialised agent into a multi-agent set. Opt: evolutionary operators (mutation, crossover, selection) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.14228)
- EvoMAS (Amazon, ICML 2026, arXiv 2602.06511) later reports beating EvoAgent by +10.5 on BBEH and +7.1 on WorkBench — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06511)

**EvoFlow**. Guibin Zhang, Kaijie Chen, Guancheng Wan et al. arXiv 2502.07373 (Feb 2025). Preprint (no venue found). PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.07373
- DV: a population of heterogeneous-LLM workflows. Opt: niching evolutionary search with tag-based retrieval, crossover, mutation and niching selection; the population spans simple I/O to multi-turn. Numbers: +1.23 to 29.86% over handcrafted and automated workflows, and beats o1-preview at 12.4% of its cost with weaker open models — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07373)

**AgentNet**. Yingxuan Yang, Huacan Chai, Shuai Shao et al. arXiv 2504.00587 (Apr 2025). Venue: not stated in arXiv; NeurIPS 2025 (not verified here). PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.00587
- DV: a decentralised, dynamically restructured DAG. Each agent keeps a RAG memory, adjusts its own connectivity and routes tasks on local expertise, with no central orchestrator — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.00587)
- Edge: framed as "minimizing centralized control and data exchange" for privacy across organisations. The abstract claims only higher accuracy than single-agent and centralised baselines, with no numbers — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.00587)

**MAS-GPT** (Rui Ye, Shuo Tang et al., arXiv 2503.03686, Mar 2025; ICML 2025, not verified here; PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.03686). Trains a medium-size LLM to emit an executable-code MAS per query in one inference. Beats 10+ MAS baselines on 9 benchmarks and 5 LLMs — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03686)

**ScoreFlow** (Yinjie Wang, Ling Yang, Guohao Li et al., arXiv 2502.04306, Feb 2025; preprint; PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.04306). Gradient-based workflow optimisation with Score-DPO. +8.2% over baselines on six benchmarks — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04306)

**FlowReasoner** (Hongcheng Gao, Yue Liu, Yufei He et al., arXiv 2504.15257, Apr 2025; preprint; PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.15257). A query-level meta-agent distilled from R1 and then RL-trained with a reward on performance, complexity and efficiency. +10.52% over o1-mini on three code benchmarks — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15257)

**MAS-Zero** (Zixuan Ke, Austin Xu, Yifei Ming et al., arXiv 2505.14996, May 2025; SEA@NeurIPS 2025 workshop oral; PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.14996). Inference-time meta-design without a validation set, **and it can reduce to simpler systems when appropriate**. Up to +16.69% on reasoning, +16.66% on coding, +5.45% on agentic tasks — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14996)

**MasRouter** (Yanwei Yue, Guibin Zhang et al., arXiv 2502.11133, Feb 2025; ACL 2025, not verified here; PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.11133). Routing over collaboration mode, role allocation and the LLM per agent with a cascaded controller. +1.8 to 8.2% on MBPP, up to 52.07% lower overhead on HumanEval — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11133)

**MetaAgent** (Yaolun Zhang, Xiaogeng Liu, Chaowei Xiao, arXiv 2507.22606, Jul 2025; ICML 2025 per arXiv comment; PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.22606). Generates a MAS as a **finite state machine; at deployment the FSM controls agent actions and state transitions**, so control flow is enforced in code. Comparable to human-designed MAS — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.22606)

**Puppeteer: "Multi-Agent Collaboration via Evolving Orchestration"** (Yufan Dang, Chen Qian et al., arXiv 2505.19591, May 2025; NeurIPS 2025 per arXiv comment; PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.19591). An RL-trained orchestrator sequences agents. Orchestrator-worker is another researcher's area; this paper is relevant here for its structural finding: gains "consistently stem from the emergence of more compact, cyclic reasoning structures" — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19591)

**EvoMAS (Amazon)** (Yuntong Hu et al., arXiv 2602.06511, Feb 2026; ICML 2026; PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.06511). Evolves structured configurations rather than code, citing executability failures of code generation. 79.1% on SWE-Bench-Verified with Claude-4.5-Sonnet — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06511)

**EvoMAS (execution-time)** (Chengdong Xu et al., arXiv 2605.08769, May 2026; preprint; PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.08769). Rebuilds a layered workflow at each stage of a task from an explicit task state, with a policy-gradient Workflow Adapter. Evaluated on GAIA, HLE and DeepResearcher — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08769)

**OptiMAS** (Yuxin Cheng et al., arXiv 2608.21918, Aug 2026; EMNLP 2026; PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.21918). Continuous MAS evolution using textual trajectories as loss signals, with a dual-track memory — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21918)

#### C. 2025-2026 learned, query-adaptive topology generators

- **ARG-Designer** (Shiyuan Li, Yixin Liu, Qingsong Wen et al., arXiv 2507.18224; AAAI 2026 oral; PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.18224): autoregressive graph generation from scratch, choosing agent count, roles from a pool, and links. Reports SOTA on six benchmarks with "significantly greater token efficiency" — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.18224)
- **TopoDIM** (Rui Sun, Jie Ding et al., arXiv 2601.10120; ACL 2026 Findings; PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.10120): one-shot generation of heterogeneous interaction modes (evaluation, debate) for decentralised execution. **46.41% fewer tokens with +1.50% performance** over SOTA — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10120)
- **AgentConductor** (Siyu Wang et al., arXiv 2602.17100; preprint; PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.17100): an RL-trained orchestrator builds a layered DAG whose density is set by inferred difficulty, using a "topological density function" and difficulty intervals "to avoid excessive pruning". Competition code: +14.6% pass@1, 13% density reduction, 68% token reduction — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17100)
- **HieraMAS** (Tianjun Yao, Zhaoyi Li, Zhiqiang Shen, arXiv 2602.20229; preprint; PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.20229): supernodes that mix several LLMs per role (propose then synthesise) plus an inter-node topology. Topology is chosen by whole-graph classification rather than edge-by-edge, because of credit-assignment problems — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20229)
- **Graph-GRPO** (Yueyang Cang et al., arXiv 2603.02701; preprint; PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.02701): samples a group of graphs per query and computes edge advantages relative to the group. Motivation: single-sample REINFORCE with binary reward (as in GPTSwarm and G-Designer) has high variance and poor credit assignment — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02701)
- **Agent Q-Mix** (Eric Hanchen Jiang et al., arXiv 2604.00344; preprint; PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.00344): topology selection as cooperative MARL (QMIX, CTDE). Each agent picks a communication action, and together they induce the round's graph. Reward balances accuracy and token cost. HLE 20.8% with Gemini-3.1-Flash-Lite vs 19.2% for Microsoft Agent Framework and LangGraph — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00344)

#### D. Message-content design: structured, summarised, compressed or latent

- **Chain of Agents** (Yusen Zhang, Ruoxi Sun, Yanfei Chen et al., Google, arXiv 2406.02818; NeurIPS 2024, not verified here; PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.02818). Workers read sequential chunks and each passes a **communication unit (CU)** to the next: evidence for QA, a running summary for summarisation, function and class names for code. **The manager sees only the final CU, not the raw chunks. Giving it all CUs reduced performance because of conflicting content.** Removing the manager: MuSiQue 37.09 to 26.79. vs RAG: HotpotQA 53.62 vs 51.91, NarrativeQA 25.26 vs 14.20, QuALITY (Gemini Ultra) 80.60 vs 62.40. Information loss in CUs is not measured — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.02818v1)
- **Agents' Room** (Fantine Huot et al., Google, arXiv 2410.02603; ICLR 2025 per arXiv comment; PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.02603). Narrative writing decomposed into specialised planning and writing agents. Preferred by experts over baselines on the new Tell Me A Story dataset. The abstract does not describe the handoff format — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02603)
- **TalkHier: "Talk Structurally, Act Hierarchically"** (Zhao Wang et al., Sony, arXiv 2502.11098; preprint; PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.11098). Each message has three parts: message, background, intermediate output (background is omitted from member-to-supervisor messages). **The structure is enforced by prompt templates, not by code validation.** Ablation (3-domain average 87.21): normal text protocol 84.43; no background 78.30; no intermediate output 82.55. MMLU 88.38% vs o1-preview 87.56%. About $2,100 in GPT-4o spend — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11098v1)
- **Optima** (Weize Chen, Jiarui Yuan, Chen Qian et al., arXiv 2410.08115; ACL 2025 Findings, not verified here; PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.08115). **Trains** agents (SFT, DPO, MCTS-style data) with a reward on task performance, token efficiency and readability. Up to 2.8× performance with less than 10% of the tokens on information-exchange tasks (Llama 3 8B) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08115)
- **CIPHER: "Let Models Speak Ciphers"** (Chau Pham et al., arXiv 2310.06272; ICLR 2024; PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.06272). Debate agents exchange the expected output embedding over the vocabulary instead of sampled tokens. +0.5 to 5.0% over natural-language debate — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06272)
- **Communicating Activations Between LM Agents** (Vignav Ramesh, Kenneth Li, arXiv 2501.14082; ICML 2025; PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.14082). One model's intermediate activation is merged into another's forward pass. Up to +27.0% over natural language with less than 1/4 the compute — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14082)
- **KVComm** (Xiangyu Shi et al., arXiv 2510.03346; ICLR 2026; PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.03346). Selective KV-pair sharing by layer, chosen by attention importance. Matches the merged-input upper bound while sending as few as 30% of layers' KV pairs — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.03346)
- **Cache-to-Cache (C2C)** (Tianyu Fu et al., arXiv 2510.03215; ICLR 2026; PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.03215). A learned projector fuses one model's KV cache into another's, with gated layer selection. +6.4 to 14.2% over individual models, +3.1 to 5.4% over text communication, 2.5× lower latency — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.03215)
- **LatentMAS** (Jiaru Zou et al., arXiv 2511.20639; ICML 2026 spotlight; PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.20639). Training-free. Agents generate latent thoughts from last-layer hidden states and share them through a latent working memory. Up to +14.6% accuracy, 70.8 to 83.7% fewer output tokens, 4 to 4.3× faster — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.20639)
- **Interlat** (Zhuoyun Du et al., arXiv 2511.09149; ACL 2026; PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.09149). Last hidden states used as messages, plus learned compression. Up to 24× faster inference at competitive performance. Framed as a feasibility study — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.09149)
- **Thought Communication** (Yujia Zheng et al., arXiv 2510.20733; NeurIPS 2025 spotlight; PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.20733). A latent-variable model with identifiability proofs. Shared and private latent thoughts are recovered, and **each agent is given only the relevant thoughts and their sharing pattern**: an explicit, theory-backed visibility assignment — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.20733)
- **"When Latent Agents Lie"** (Luís Brito, Carlos Baquero, arXiv 2606.28958, Jun 2026; preprint; PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.28958). Specialists send a short visible commitment plus their full KV cache. Clean latent runs beat text (HiddenBench Qwen3-4B EM/F1 0.338/0.486 vs 0.231/0.369). Tampering with the hidden state collapses performance while the visible text still looks plausible, and text-only verifiers miss it. An HMAC-SHA256 manifest accepted all 774 honest payloads and rejected all 295 tampered ones — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28958)
- **"Beyond tokens"** survey (Yingzhuo Liu, arXiv 2606.05711, Jun 2026; PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.05711). 18 latent-communication methods (2024-2026) classified on three axes: what is sent (embeddings, hidden states, KV), how sender and receiver are aligned, how the message is fused — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05711)

#### E. Prompt and program optimisation applied to pipelines

- **DSPy** (Omar Khattab et al., arXiv 2310.03714; ICLR 2024, not verified here; PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.03714). LM pipelines as text-transformation graphs of declarative modules with typed **signatures**, compiled against a metric. Over 25% (GPT-3.5) and 65% (llama2-13b) above few-shot prompting — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03714). MASS used DSPy and MIPRO as prompt optimisers: on MATH with Gemini Flash, 78.2 with DSPy vs 81.0 with MIPRO — [MASS HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02533v2)
- **TextGrad** (Mert Yuksekgonul et al., arXiv 2406.07496; later Nature 2025, not verified here; PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.07496). Backpropagates LLM textual feedback through compound-system graphs. GPQA 51% to 55%, +20% relative on LeetCode-Hard — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07496). MaAS uses textual gradients for operator updates, and removing them is its largest ablation drop (MATH 51.82 to 48.23) — [MaAS HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.04180v2)

#### F. Attribution, evaluation and scaling studies bearing on structure

- **"Which Agent Causes Task Failures and When?"** (Shaokun Zhang, Ming Yin, Jieyu Zhang et al., arXiv 2505.00212; ICML 2025, not verified here, arXiv says "camera-ready"; PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.00212). Who&When dataset: failure logs from 127 MAS. The best method gets the responsible agent right 53.5% of the time but the decisive step only 14.2%, some methods are below random, and o1 and R1 are not practically usable — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00212)
- **"Rethinking the Evaluation of Efficiency Methods for Multi-Agent Systems"** (Jiamu Zhang et al., arXiv 2609.05933, Sep 2026; EMNLP 2026; PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05933). Re-tests AgentPrune, AgentDropout, AGP, DAGP and GPTSwarm with a shared backbone, agent registry and runtime. Averaged over 20 cells, optimised methods changed accuracy by −0.77 points vs −0.44 for matched random pruning. Methods beat their random control in only 12 of 25 topology-method pairs, and only DAGP clearly won (4 of 5 topologies). "Structural collapse": AGP on Random HumanEval cut about 11.4K prompt tokens but lost 17.39 points. GPTSwarm on Chain GSM8K lost 86.27 points with almost no token change. AgentPrune on Star GSM8K added 2.83K prompt tokens through an appended decision node. GPTSwarm permuted roles in 13 of 38 cells. Run-to-run SD on MMLU-500 was 0.6 to 2.1 points, about the size of most reported gains — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05933v1)
- **"Towards a Science of Scaling Agent Systems"** (Yubin Kim, Ken Gu, Chanwoo Park et al., Google Research, Google DeepMind and MIT, arXiv 2512.08296; preprint; PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.08296). 260 configurations, 6 benchmarks, 5 architectures, 3 LLM families. Error amplification relative to a single agent: Independent 17.2×, Decentralized 7.8×, Hybrid 5.1×, Centralized 4.4×. Above a single-agent baseline of about 45%, adding agents gives diminishing or negative returns. Token overhead vs single agent: Independent 58%, Decentralized 263%, Centralized 285%, Hybrid 515%. Success rises logarithmically with message density and plateaus near 0.39 messages per turn. Change vs single agent ranges from +80.8% (Finance Agent, centralised) to −70.0% (PlanCraft, independent). The framework picks the best architecture for 87% of held-out configurations — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08296v3); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08296)
- Pointer (debate area): "Rethinking the Bounds of LLM Reasoning: Are Multi-Agent Discussions the Key?" (arXiv 2402.18272; PDF downloaded): a single agent with strong prompts nearly matches the best discussion method, and discussion wins only without demonstrations — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18272). "Talk Isn't Always Cheap" (arXiv 2509.05396, ICML MAS workshop 2025): debate can lower accuracy over time; models switch from correct to incorrect answers to agree with peers — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05396)
- Surveys: "Beyond Self-Talk: A Communication-Centric Survey of LLM-Based MAS" (arXiv 2502.14321; Frontiers of Computer Science) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14321)

### Inferences
- The objective function is the most important hidden variable. ADAS and AFlow optimise accuracy only. MaAS, Agent Q-Mix, FlowReasoner and Optima put cost in the reward. Pruning papers (AgentPrune, G-Designer, AgentDropout, AGP) add a sparsity or low-rank regulariser. So "sparser" results are partly built into the objective, not discovered.
- Almost every system in group A is evaluated on the same benchmarks (MMLU, GSM8K, HumanEval, MATH) with a small agent pool. The 2609.05933 re-evaluation suggests these are "non-MAS-demanding" settings, where any pruning looks free.

### Gaps
- Exact venues for AgentNet, G-Designer, MaAS, AFlow, ADAS, DSPy, TextGrad (Nature), Chain of Agents, Who&When, AgentDropout, MasRouter, Optima and Sparse-MAD were not confirmed from a primary source in this run; they are marked "(not verified here)".
- DyLAN's message content and early-stopping mechanics, Agents' Room's handoff format (scratchpad), and EvoAgent's numbers were not fetched beyond the abstracts.
- "Agents' Room / Chain of Agents" was given in the brief as one item. They are two different Google papers (2410.02603, a narrative writing system; 2406.02818, long-context handoffs), and both are covered.

## When topology is optimised for accuracy, does it end up sparser than hand-designed systems? By how much, and what gets cut?

### Takeaway
Usually yes when sparsity or cost is in the objective: reported token savings run from about 12% to more than 95% against complete-graph or debate baselines. When accuracy is the only objective (MASS, ADAS, AFlow), the result is not clearly sparser and is sometimes wider (for example nine parallel agents). What gets cut is mostly redundant peer-to-peer edges within a round, carry-over of history between rounds, and whole agents on easy queries. A 2026 controlled study warns that a large share of these cuts are no better than random pruning, and some are "structural collapse".

### Cited Findings
- AgentPrune cuts both spatial (within-round) and temporal (between-round history) edges. Token reduction is 28.1 to 72.8%, and even random removal of 10 to 30% of mesh/debate edges gained up to 2.83% on MMLU — [AgentPrune HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02506v1)
- G-Designer: up to 95.33% fewer tokens on HumanEval. At 20 agents on MMLU it uses about 1.9×10⁶ prompt tokens vs about 5.6×10⁶ for a complete graph. Sparsity is enforced by a nuclear-norm term and a chain anchor, and edge-density numbers are not reported — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.11782); [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.11782v3)
- AGP: more than 90% fewer tokens by jointly cutting agents and edges — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.02951). AgentDropout: −21.6% prompt and −18.4% completion tokens by removing agents and edges per round — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18891). SafeSieve: −12.4 to 27.8% tokens — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11733). TopoDIM: −46.41% tokens with +1.50% performance — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10120). AgentConductor: −13% density, −68% tokens, +14.6% pass@1 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17100)
- GPTSwarm's learned crossword graph has about the same edge count as a uniform θ=12.5% graph (32.76 vs 32.80), so the gain came from which edges, not how many — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.16823v3)
- MaAS: easy queries exit at layer 2, and inference costs 6 to 45% of existing systems — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.04180v2); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04180). Puppeteer: an RL orchestrator evolves "more compact, cyclic" structures — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19591). MAS-Zero can reduce to simpler systems — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14996)
- Counter-evidence (accuracy-only search): MASS's found systems are not sparse (nine parallel agents on MATH; five reflection rounds plus a debate on HotpotQA), and the authors leave sparse communication to future work — [MASS HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02533v2)
- MacNet (hand-designed families): irregular random DAGs beat full mesh on average (0.6522 vs 0.6316) at about 51.92% less time, but mesh > tree > chain on average, so moderate density is better than minimal — [MacNet HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07155v3)
- Controlled re-test: optimised pruning methods changed accuracy by −0.77 points vs −0.44 for matched random pruning, beat random in only 12 of 25 topology-method pairs, and sometimes cut needed pathways (AGP −17.39 points on HumanEval; GPTSwarm −86.27 on Chain GSM8K) — [2609.05933 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05933v1)
- AgentConductor explicitly adds difficulty intervals "to avoid excessive pruning", which shows over-pruning is a known failure mode — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17100)

### Inferences
- In the systems that report it, the "hand-designed" baseline being beaten is nearly always the complete graph or all-to-all debate. Against that baseline, sparsification looks large. Against a chain, a star or a single strong agent, the margin is much smaller or missing (2609.05933, MASS, 2402.18272).
- A plausible reading for harness design: the reliable savings come from (a) dropping between-round history carry-over and (b) skipping agents on easy queries, more than from fine edge selection within a round. This is an inference from the mix of mechanisms; no paper isolates it directly.

### Gaps
- No paper found reports a single comparable "edge density of learned graph / edge density of hand-designed graph" ratio across benchmarks. G-Designer and AgentPrune report tokens, not edge counts.
- Whether learned sparse graphs transfer across backbone models was not systematically tested in the topology-pruning line (MaAS, ADAS and AFlow report some cross-model transfer of workflows).

## Which papers show that passing less information between agents improves results, and which show the opposite?

### Takeaway
Less helps: AgentPrune (random edge removal +2.83%), MacNet (artifact-only passing; random beats mesh), Chain of Agents (the manager does better seeing only the last CU than all CUs), G-Designer, TopoDIM and AgentConductor (fewer tokens with higher accuracy), and the Google scaling study (gains plateau near 0.39 messages per turn; more coordination hurts sequential and tool-heavy tasks). More helps or less hurts: MacNet (mesh > chain on average), TalkHier (removing background or intermediate output from messages costs 4.7 to 8.9 points), the 2609.05933 collapse cases, and the latent-channel line, where passing richer state (KV, hidden states) beats text.

### Cited Findings
**Less information helps**
- AgentPrune: random removal of 10 to 30% of edges gained up to 2.83% on MMLU, and pruning also defends against adversarial agents (+3.5 to 10.8%) — [AgentPrune HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02506v1); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02506)
- MacNet: only the refined artifact passes between nodes (reasoning is hidden). Random topologies beat mesh, which the authors attribute to "reduced information overload". Context explosion breaks AgentVerse past about 30 agents — [MacNet HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07155v3)
- Chain of Agents: the manager gets only the final CU. Giving it all CUs, or only the relevant ones, lowered performance because of conflicting content — [CoA HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.02818v1)
- Sparse-topology debate: comparable or better performance at lower cost — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11776)
- G-Designer adversarial test: complete and chain graphs lose up to 11.0% when one agent is compromised, vs 0.3% for the pruned topology — [G-Designer HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.11782v3)
- Google scaling study: success rises logarithmically with message density and plateaus near 0.39 messages per turn. Above a single-agent baseline of about 45%, more agents give diminishing or negative returns. Independent agents amplify errors 17.2× vs 4.4× for centralised. PlanCraft goes from −39.1% to −70.0% for all MAS variants — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08296v3)
- Collaboration-mechanisms study: 3 to 10 rounds gives no clear gain, and conformity grows with rounds and hurts some models — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.02124v3)
- Optima: a trained protocol with less than 10% of the tokens gives up to 2.8× performance on information-exchange tasks — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08115)
- MASS: most added topologies do not help, and prompt optimisation of single agents beats adding debate or self-consistency per token — [MASS HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02533v2)
- Pointer: "Talk Isn't Always Cheap" finds agents switch from correct to incorrect answers in response to peer reasoning — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05396)

**More information helps, or cutting it hurts**
- MacNet: the authors say higher edge density generally helps (mesh > tree > chain); Table 1 averages are mesh 0.6316, chain 0.6078, tree 0.6015, so chain and tree are close. Wider beats deeper because memory control makes deep agents "lose track of distant agents" — [MacNet HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07155v3)
- TalkHier ablation: full 87.21; no background 78.30; no intermediate output 82.55; plain text protocol 84.43. Richer structured context in each message helps — [TalkHier HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11098v1)
- 2609.05933: structural collapse cases where pruning removes useful pathways (AGP HumanEval −17.39; GPTSwarm Chain GSM8K −86.27), and executor or retrieval agents get disabled — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05933v1)
- Latent channels (more bandwidth per message than text): CIPHER +0.5 to 5.0% — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06272); activations up to +27% — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14082); C2C +3.1 to 5.4% over text — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.03215); LatentMAS up to +14.6% — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.20639); full-KV beats text on HiddenBench (F1 0.486 vs 0.369) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28958)
- Google scaling study: decomposable tasks benefit strongly from coordination (Finance Agent +80.8% centralised) — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08296v3)
- Chain of Agents: removing the manager (the last worker answers directly) costs over 10 points on MuSiQue — [CoA HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.02818v1)

### Inferences
- The results line up if two quantities are kept apart: how many peer messages an agent sees (fewer is often better because of conformity, conflict and overload) and how much task-relevant state each message carries (richer is often better: TalkHier fields, latent state). Chain of Agents and MacNet do both: one compact but complete artifact per edge.
- In information-design terms, the latent-channel results lift the ceiling on what one edge can carry, while the pruning results say most edges should carry nothing. The open design question is the visibility map, which Thought Communication (2510.20733) addresses directly by assigning each agent only its relevant latent thoughts.

### Gaps
- No paper found runs a controlled sweep that holds topology fixed and varies only the message content (full transcript vs summary vs fields vs verdict only) across several tasks. TalkHier's ablation is the closest.
- Latent-channel results mostly use same-family open models (Qwen, Llama). Cross-architecture alignment is an open problem in the 2026 survey — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05711). This matters for model-invariant harnesses.

## Is any message structure imposed in code (fixed fields, verdict-only) rather than left to the models?

### Takeaway
Rarely in the research systems. Structure is enforced by code mostly through control flow and routing (DAG execution, which output goes to which node, FSM states, artifact-only propagation), not through validated message schemas. The fields themselves (TalkHier, Chain of Agents CUs) are asked for in prompts. The only schema-validated content found is in protocols (MCP `outputSchema`) and in programming frameworks (DSPy signatures). Latent methods remove text entirely and impose structure by construction.

### Cited Findings
- **Enforced by code (routing and visibility):**
  - GPTSwarm, G-Designer, AgentPrune: the graph decides whose outputs go into whose prompt. A pruned edge is a message the receiver never sees, so code fully controls visibility — [GPTSwarm HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.16823v3); [G-Designer HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.11782v3); [AgentPrune HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02506v1)
  - MacNet: memory control passes only the artifact between nodes, which hides reasoning from downstream agents by construction — [MacNet HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07155v3)
  - AFlow and ADAS: edges are Python, so what passes (for example a list of solutions into an ensemble, then the best into a test) is set by generated code. The content of each item is still free text — [AFlow HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10762v4); [ADAS HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.08435v2)
  - MetaAgent: a finite state machine controls agent actions and state transitions at deployment — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.22606)
  - Chain of Agents: in code, the manager sees only the last CU. What the CU contains (evidence, summary) is set by the prompt — [CoA HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.02818v1)
  - "When Latent Agents Lie": an HMAC-SHA256 manifest in code binds specialist, session, model, visible commitment and tensor digest. It accepted 774 of 774 honest payloads and rejected 295 of 295 tampered ones — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28958)
- **Asked for by prompt only:**
  - TalkHier's three-part message (message, background, intermediate output) comes from prompt templates, with no programmatic schema validation described — [TalkHier HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11098v1)
  - Collaboration-mechanisms study: traits and thinking patterns are prompt-assigned — [paper HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.02124v3)
- **Schema enforced in a framework or protocol:**
  - DSPy modules declare signatures (typed input and output fields) that the compiler optimises against a metric — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03714)
  - MCP: if a tool declares `outputSchema`, servers MUST return conforming `structuredContent`, and clients SHOULD validate it. `inputSchema` MUST be valid JSON Schema — [MCP tools spec](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2025-11-25/server/tools)
- **Learned or trained structure:** Optima trains agents toward short, readable messages by reward, so the structure is learned into the weights rather than enforced — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08115)

### Inferences
- No surveyed paper enforces a verdict-only or fixed-field message channel in code and measures it against free text. For a harness designer this is an open and testable gap. The nearest evidence (TalkHier) suggests that removing context fields hurts, which argues for structured but complete messages over verdict-only ones, at least for refinement tasks.
- Code-enforced visibility (pruned edges, artifact-only passing, manager-sees-last-CU) is the established lever in the literature. Code-enforced message content is not.

### Gaps
- Whether the found AFlow or ADAS workflows contain code-level parsing of LLM outputs (for example answer extraction) was not checked case by case. It is likely, given operators like Format, but unverified.
- I found no study of JSON-schema-constrained inter-agent messages (constrained decoding) in a MAS topology paper.

## What do the protocol papers (MCP, A2A, ACP, ANP) actually standardise: content, or only transport?

### Takeaway
They standardise the envelope, lifecycle, discovery and identity, not the meaning of messages. MCP gives JSON-RPC 2.0 framing, typed tool calls and optional JSON-Schema-validated tool outputs. A2A gives Agent Cards, a Task state machine, Message/Part/Artifact containers and three transport bindings, and it deliberately keeps agents opaque to each other. ACP (IBM) gave REST plus MIME multipart and merged into A2A in August 2025. ANP gives DID identity, JSON-LD agent descriptions and a meta-protocol by which agents negotiate the content protocol themselves, often in natural language. None of them prescribes what one agent should tell another.

### Cited Findings
- **MCP (Anthropic, Nov 2024; spec 2025-11-25):** JSON-RPC 2.0, stateful connections, capability negotiation. Server features: Resources, Prompts, Tools. Client features: Sampling, Roots, Elicitation. Security principles are stated, but "MCP itself cannot enforce these security principles at the protocol level" — [MCP spec](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2025-11-25)
- MCP tools: `inputSchema` MUST be valid JSON Schema (2020-12 by default). Optional `outputSchema`: servers MUST conform and clients SHOULD validate `structuredContent`. Unstructured content types: text, image, audio, resource_link, embedded resource. Two error channels (JSON-RPC protocol errors vs `isError: true` execution errors that are fed back to the model) — [MCP tools spec](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2025-11-25/server/tools)
- MCP is client-server for tool and context access, not peer-to-peer between agents; surveys class it as "context-oriented" vs "inter-agent" protocols — [Yang et al. survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16736); [Ehtesham et al. survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02279)
- **A2A (Google, Apr 2025; latest spec 1.0.0):** core objects are AgentCard (capabilities, skills, endpoint, auth), Task, Message (role plus parts), Part, Artifact. Task states: SUBMITTED, WORKING, COMPLETED, FAILED, CANCELED, REJECTED, INPUT_REQUIRED, AUTH_REQUIRED. Bindings: JSON-RPC, gRPC, HTTP+JSON/REST, required to be functionally equivalent — [A2A spec](https://a2a-protocol.org/latest/specification/)
- A2A Part: exactly one of `text`, `raw` (bytes), `url`, or `data` (arbitrary JSON), plus optional `mediaType`, `filename`, `metadata`. No schema validation of `data` is specified. Extensions are declared on the AgentCard, opted into via an `A2A-Extensions` header, and carried in message or artifact metadata keyed by URI — [A2A spec](https://a2a-protocol.org/latest/specification/)
- A2A opacity principle: agents collaborate "without needing access to each other's internal state, memory, or tools" — [A2A spec](https://a2a-protocol.org/latest/specification/). The spec page says A2A joined the "Agentic AI Foundation". Other sources say Linux Foundation and LF AI & Data (see ACP below), so the governance naming differs between sources.
- **ACP (IBM Research / BeeAI):** RESTful HTTP with MIME-typed multipart messages, sync and async, sessions — [Ehtesham et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02279). IBM's project page says "ACP is now part of A2A under the Linux Foundation". The merger was announced August 2025; sources disagree on the exact date (27 vs 29 August) — [IBM Research](https://research.ibm.com/projects/agent-communication-protocol); [IBM explainer](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/agent-communication-protocol)
- **ANP (Agent Network Protocol, open community):** three layers: identity and encryption (W3C DID, `did:wba`, ECDHE end-to-end), a meta-protocol negotiation layer, and an application layer (JSON-LD Agent Description Protocol with schema.org vocabulary, discovery via `/.well-known/agent-descriptions`). **Message payload semantics are not standardised.** Agents negotiate the concrete protocol, data formats and processing in natural language, then generate code and test it jointly. The white paper admits negotiation is costly and suggests caching — [ANP white paper](https://agent-network-protocol.com/specs/white-paper.html)
- Surveys: Yang et al. 2025 (arXiv 2504.16736; PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.16736) classify protocols as context-oriented vs inter-agent and general vs domain-specific, and compare security, scalability and latency — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16736). Ehtesham et al. 2025 (arXiv 2505.02279; PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.02279) propose a phased adoption roadmap: MCP, then ACP, then A2A, then ANP — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02279)

### Inferences
- For information design, the protocols are neutral containers. They decide who can address whom (discovery, cards, DIDs) and the shape of the envelope, while what is revealed stays fully with the agent author. A2A's opacity principle is the one place a protocol takes a stance on visibility: internal state stays private by default, so only declared outputs (Artifacts) cross the boundary. That fits contract-theoretic framings of hidden information and hidden action.
- MCP `outputSchema` is the only normative content constraint found in any of the four, and it applies to tool outputs, not agent-to-agent messages. A harness wanting code-enforced message fields between agents could reuse this mechanism by wrapping agents as MCP tools with output schemas. That is an inference, not a documented pattern in the surveyed papers.
